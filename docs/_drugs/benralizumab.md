# Benralizumab: From Eosinophilic Asthma to Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Benralizumab is an anti-IL-5 receptor alpha antibody, originally used as add-on maintenance treatment for severe eosinophilic asthma and for relapsing or refractory eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Dermatitis**, the highest-ranked prediction that has any supporting studies, with **3 clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this direction.
The evidence is weak: the only placebo-controlled efficacy trial in atopic dermatitis was terminated, and a published report describes a lack of effect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Benralizumab binds the IL-5 receptor alpha subunit (IL5RA) and depletes eosinophils and basophils through antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity. Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the compiled drug record, but the target and mechanism are consistent with its approved use in eosinophil-driven diseases.

Severe eosinophilic asthma and atopic dermatitis both involve type 2 inflammation. Eosinophils, basophils and innate lymphoid type 2 cells are present in inflamed skin, so depleting them is a plausible approach. A small Phase 2 mechanistic study (NCT03563066) was designed to examine these cell effects.

The biological rationale has not translated into clinical benefit so far. A published report of the Phase 2 HILLIER trial is titled "Lack of effect of benralizumab on signs and symptoms of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis." Much of the retrieved literature consists of general reviews of biologics in allergic disease rather than direct efficacy evidence for dermatitis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, but the only randomized efficacy trial in atopic dermatitis was terminated and its published report describes no clinical effect. The remaining literature is general review material. The other nine predictions, including rank 1 (immune thrombocytopenia), have only a model score, no trials or literature, and weak mechanistic support.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The reason for termination and the full results of NCT04605094
- A review of the HILLIER publication, including any subgroup signals such as high-eosinophil patients
- Results from the DRESS trial (NCT06734884) once available, as an eosinophil-driven skin indication may be a better fit
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the current EU authorization status
