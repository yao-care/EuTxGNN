## Grazoprevir: HIV infectious disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Grazoprevir is an antiviral that inhibits the NS3/4A protease of the hepatitis C virus and is marketed in the EU as part of the Zepatier combination. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **HIV infectious disease**. The 10 clinical trials and 20 publications compiled for this report enrolled people with HIV, but they measured hepatitis C cure, not HIV control. This is therefore a model prediction without direct supporting evidence (**Evidence Level L5**).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Published reviews describe grazoprevir as an inhibitor of the HCV NS3/4A serine protease, taken together with the NS5A inhibitor elbasvir in a fixed-dose tablet.

The mechanistic link to HIV is weak. HIV uses its own aspartyl protease, which is structurally and functionally different from the HCV NS3/4A enzyme. No direct anti-HIV activity of grazoprevir has been documented. The high TxGNN score (0.9973) most likely reflects the model's pattern of placing antivirals close to HIV-related annotations.

The trials that mention HIV enrolled patients co-infected with both viruses. They show that grazoprevir/elbasvir can be used in people with HIV, but they say nothing about whether it treats HIV. This is a population overlap, not a repurposing signal.

## Safety Considerations

Several moderate-level interactions are also listed (for example aprepitant, cimetidine, ticagrelor, erythromycin). ## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The model score is high (0.9973), but no trial or publication tests grazoprevir against HIV infection. The HIV-related studies measure hepatitis C cure in co-infected patients, and the mechanism does not explain any anti-HIV effect, because HIV uses a different protease. The evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- In vitro antiviral assays showing whether grazoprevir has any activity against HIV
- Mechanism of action data and a structural rationale for any HIV target
- A clinical question that differs from the co-infection studies, with HIV viral load as the endpoint
- Interaction review with antiretroviral combinations if any HIV-directed use is considered
