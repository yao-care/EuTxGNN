# Brexpiprazole: From Schizophrenia to Dysthymic Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Brexpiprazole is an atypical antipsychotic (a serotonin-dopamine activity modulator) that was originally authorised in the EU for the treatment of schizophrenia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dysthymic disorder**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** specific to this condition were found, so the prediction rests on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Brexpiprazole is a serotonin-dopamine activity modulator. It acts as a partial agonist at 5-HT1A and D2/D3 receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description comes from the model's rationale and the retrieved literature.

Dysthymic disorder is a chronic, lower-grade depressive condition. This receptor profile is plausibly relevant to chronic depressive states. Brexpiprazole is also used as adjunctive therapy in major depressive disorder (MDD) in other markets, which gives the prediction some biological plausibility.

However, no trials or publications were found for dysthymia itself. The prediction is therefore a hypothesis, not a demonstrated effect.

**Related lower-ranked predictions:** Two other predictions, neurotic depression (score 97.11%) and melancholia (97.07%), are supported by indirect evidence from the MDD literature. This includes a Phase 3 randomised placebo-controlled trial of adjunctive brexpiprazole in MDD (PMID 26301701) and several network meta-analyses. These are evidence for MDD, not for dysthymia, and they are best viewed as a signal that depressive disorders in general are a reasonable research direction. Adjunctive MDD use is likely already approved in some markets, so it is not a novel repurposing signal.

## Safety Considerations

  - Aprepitant
  - Morphine

  - Clotrimazole
- **Moderate interactions** include several antidiabetic agents (e.g., acarbose, metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin), anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, clidinium), loperamide, and epinephrine.
- **Class-related concern:** Antipsychotics with D2 activity carry a risk of extrapyramidal or dystonic adverse effects. This matters particularly for any paediatric use.

Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for dysthymic disorder is supported only by the model score (L5). There are no trials or publications for this condition. The only EU authorisation is cancelled, and the safety data are incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Dysthymia-specific evidence, such as a literature search and trial registry search for persistent depressive disorder
- A review of whether the MDD adjunctive evidence can be reasonably extended to chronic, lower-grade depression
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC or other label sources, which are currently missing
- Clarification of why the EU authorisation was cancelled, and of the current regulatory status
- A review of the Major drug interactions, particularly with CYP-related interacting drugs, for the intended patient population
