# Perampanel: From Epilepsy to Visual Epilepsy

## One-Sentence Summary

Perampanel is a selective, non-competitive AMPA receptor antagonist originally used as an antiseizure medication for focal-onset (partial-onset) and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures in epilepsy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Visual Epilepsy**, a rare reflex epilepsy subtype triggered by visual/photic stimuli,
with **3 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently available in the evidence pack — but none of these studies were specifically designed for this subtype.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

A formal DrugBank-sourced mechanism-of-action record is not available in this evidence pack (data gap). However, multiple literature sources collected for this candidate consistently and independently describe Perampanel as a **selective, non-competitive antagonist of AMPA (α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid) receptors**, which mediate glutamate-driven postsynaptic excitation in the central nervous system. This mechanism underlies its broad-spectrum anticonvulsant activity across multiple seizure types.

The original indication (epilepsy with focal-onset and generalized tonic-clonic seizures) and the predicted new indication (visual epilepsy, a photosensitive/visually-induced reflex epilepsy subtype) share the same underlying disease category. Visual epilepsy is characterized by cortical hyperexcitability provoked by visual/photic stimuli — the so-called photoparoxysmal response — which, like other seizure types, is thought to involve excessive AMPA-receptor-mediated glutamatergic transmission.

Mechanistically, an AMPA antagonist that already suppresses cortical hyperexcitability in generalized and focal epilepsy could plausibly dampen the specific visual-trigger pathway as well. This is a reasonable extrapolation from known pharmacology, but it remains a **research hypothesis rather than a demonstrated clinical effect**, since no trial or publication in this evidence pack directly studies perampanel in patients with visual/photosensitive reflex epilepsy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Current evidence is limited to L4 (mechanistic/preclinical-level rationale). All 3 registered clinical trials are graded "C" relevance — they study perampanel in general epilepsy populations, not the visual/photosensitive reflex epilepsy subtype — and none of the 19 publications directly addresses this indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A dedicated clinical study (or at minimum case series) of perampanel in visual/photosensitive reflex epilepsy patients
