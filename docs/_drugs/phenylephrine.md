# Phenylephrine: From Intraoperative Mydriasis in Eye Surgery to Nasal Cavity Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Phenylephrine was originally authorised in the EU (as Omidria) to maintain pupil dilation and reduce pain during intraocular lens replacement surgery.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nasal cavity disease**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **5 publications**, mostly indirect, supporting this direction.
The evidence is essentially model prediction plus indirect literature.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Phenylephrine is, however, a well-known alpha-1 adrenergic agonist. In the eye it dilates the pupil, and elsewhere it constricts blood vessels.

In the nose, the same vasoconstriction shrinks swollen, congested mucosa. Phenylephrine is already widely used topically as a nasal decongestant. It also appears in combination sprays (for example Polydexa with phenylephrine) and as a vasoconstrictor to improve the surgical field in endoscopic sinus surgery. The mechanism is therefore plausible and familiar.

The link to the original eye-surgery indication is only that both uses rely on alpha-1 receptor effects on smooth muscle and blood vessels. The supporting literature is mostly combination-product studies, descriptive reports or surgical-adjunct studies, so the evidence for phenylephrine itself is indirect. Several lower-ranked predictions (for example acute laryngopharyngitis, faucial diphtheria, cervical disc degenerative disorder and endobronchial leiomyoma) have no evidence at all, and the model score alone should not be read as proof.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for nasal cavity disease.

For context, one completed trial exists for a related airway indication (bronchial disease, ranked 9th): [NCT00373802](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00373802), a Phase 2, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (50 infants) of nasal phenylephrine in bronchiolitis. No outcome data were available in the compiled data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found, all of the examples shown being of Moderate severity. They include antidiabetic drugs (e.g., acarbose, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, insulin, and the gliflozin and GLP-1 agonist classes) and anticholinergic/antimuscarinic agents (e.g., atropine ophthalmic, hyoscyamine, clidinium).
- **Conditions flagged in the knowledge graph** (drug–disease contraindication links, not label text): hypertension, liver and kidney disease, epilepsy, COPD, hypothyroidism, adrenocortical insufficiency, psychotic disorders, peptic ulcer disease, inflammatory bowel disease, pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia and porphyria, among others.
- **Additional signals from the literature**: the older asthma literature suggests alpha-adrenergic stimulation may cause bronchoconstriction, and one case report describes toxicity with intranasal use. Sympathomimetic use may carry cardiovascular risk.

Please refer to the SmPC for label warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high and the mechanism (mucosal vasoconstriction) is plausible. Phenylephrine is already used as a nasal decongestant. However, there are no registered trials for nasal cavity disease, the literature is indirect (mostly combination products), and the only EU authorisation is cancelled and covers a different, ophthalmic use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC or package insert (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of which specific nasal conditions are targeted, and a review of existing approved nasal phenylephrine products and their evidence
- Route and formulation compatibility assessment (the original product was an ophthalmic irrigation additive, not a nasal product)
- Prospective controlled studies of phenylephrine alone, with cardiovascular safety monitoring in at-risk patients
