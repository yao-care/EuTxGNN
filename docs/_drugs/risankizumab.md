# Risankizumab: From Plaque Psoriasis and Inflammatory Bowel Disease to Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Risankizumab (Skyrizi) is an IL-23 p19 inhibitor originally approved for plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis**, with **2 clinical trials** (1 completed Phase 2 RCT directly testing risankizumab in atopic dermatitis) and **20 publications** currently related to this direction.
The mechanistic fit is only partial, because atopic dermatitis is mainly Th2-driven, so the results of the Phase 2 trial need to be verified.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, risankizumab is a humanised IgG monoclonal antibody that targets the p19 subunit of IL-23. IL-23/Th17 signalling contributes to several inflammatory skin diseases, and its efficacy in plaque psoriasis is established.

Atopic dermatitis, the most common form of dermatitis, is predominantly Th2-driven, although Th22 and possibly Th17 pathways are also involved. This gives a partial rationale for IL-23 and IL-22 blockade, but the mechanistic link is weaker than in psoriasis.

The published literature suggests risankizumab may be most relevant to psoriasis-overlap or paradoxical eczema phenotypes rather than classical atopic dermatitis. One case report describes combined use with dupilumab in concomitant atopic dermatitis and psoriasis. Other reports describe eczematous eruptions arising during risankizumab treatment. This means the drug may also trigger eczema in some patients, which argues for caution.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen identified 78 interacting drugs. Examples of those flagged as **Major** are adalimumab, baricitinib, certolizumab pegol and cladribine. Examples flagged as **Moderate** are tacrolimus, azathioprine, cyclosporine, anakinra, alemtuzumab, denosumab, roflumilast and several vaccines (tetanus toxoid, anthrax, live cholera). Zinc salts are flagged as Minor. Most of the major and moderate interactions involve combined immunosuppression.
- **Paradoxical eczema**: Case reports describe eczematous eruptions during risankizumab treatment, which is relevant when considering use in dermatitis.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 2 RCT (n=172) exists for atopic dermatitis, which supports an L2 evidence level, but its efficacy results are not available in the compiled data. Atopic dermatitis is mainly Th2-driven, and there are reports of eczema arising during risankizumab treatment. The market authorisation is also recorded as cancelled. This is best treated as a research question until the trial results are reviewed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Efficacy and safety results of NCT03706040 and PMID 36588137
- Mechanism of action data
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorisation status
- Assessment of whether the psoriasis-overlap or paradoxical eczema phenotype is a better target than classical atopic dermatitis

**Other predictions:** Predictions ranked 2 to 10 (including neonatal dermatomyositis, amyopathic dermatomyositis, acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans, acne keloid and pityriasis lichenoides) have no clinical trials or literature, rest only on the model score (Evidence Level L5), and are recommended as **Hold**.
