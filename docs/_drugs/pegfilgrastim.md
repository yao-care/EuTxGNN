## Pegfilgrastim: From Chemotherapy-Induced Neutropenia to Severe Nonproliferative Diabetic Retinopathy
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Pegfilgrastim is a long-acting G-CSF (granulocyte colony-stimulating factor) product, originally used to shorten neutropenia and reduce febrile neutropenia in patients receiving cytotoxic chemotherapy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this direction. The prediction is based on the computational model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, pegfilgrastim is a long-acting form of G-CSF that stimulates neutrophil production, and its efficacy in chemotherapy-induced neutropenia is established.

The data compiled for this report do not support a mechanistic link to severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy. The high score (99.89%) is a graph-based prediction only. One speculative hypothesis is that G-CSF-driven mobilization of progenitor cells could play a role in vascular repair, but nothing in the available data demonstrates this.

The same pattern applies to the other predicted indications, such as diabetic retinopathy (99.73%), cataract subtypes, and platelet disorders such as Glanzmann thrombasthenia. None has supporting trials or literature, and for several of them (platelet function disorders, cataract) no plausible biological rationale is apparent.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy rests only on a model score. There are no registered trials, no publications, and no supported mechanistic link (Evidence Level L5). In addition, all 11 EU authorizations are cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and a mechanistic analysis of whether G-CSF activity could plausibly affect retinal vascular disease
- Preclinical studies (for example, in diabetic retinopathy models) and a systematic literature review
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, including safety in diabetic patients and in the eye
- Clarification of the regulatory situation, since the product has no currently valid EU authorization
- Route and dosage form compatibility assessment, for example whether systemic dosing would suit an ocular disease
