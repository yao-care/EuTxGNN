## One-Sentence Summary

Amifampridine was originally used for the symptomatic treatment of Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS) in adults.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Amifampridine is generally known as a voltage-gated potassium channel blocker. It prolongs presynaptic depolarization and enhances acetylcholine release, which is how it improves neuromuscular transmission in LEMS.

No established link between this mechanism and intraocular pressure regulation or retinal ganglion cell protection was identified. The high TxGNN score is a model output only and cannot be verified mechanistically with the available data. Treat this prediction with caution.

Other predictions in the list (ranks 2-10) show the same pattern. Several have no plausible mechanism and are likely graph-based artifacts, such as esophageal varices and varicose disease. A few paraneoplastic neurological conditions (limbic encephalitis, polyneuropathy, cerebellar degeneration) have some indirect biological plausibility because LEMS is often paraneoplastic and autoimmune. They remain hypotheses only, and none has clinical evidence. These are better framed as research questions than as development candidates.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 68 interactions were recorded in the data compiled for this report. The first 20 are summarized here.
  - **Major**: Bupropion
  - **Moderate**: corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, betamethasone, dexamethasone, prednisolone, prednisone), appetite suppressants and stimulants (dexfenfluramine, diethylpropion, fenfluramine, mazindol, phentermine, sibutramine, ephedrine), dronabinol, levofloxacin, metoclopramide, opioids (morphine, liposomal morphine, opium), and polyethylene glycol 3350 with electrolytes
- **Seizure risk**: Amifampridine lowers the seizure threshold. This matters for conditions where seizures can occur, such as porphyria and limbic encephalitis. Several interacting drugs on this list (such as bupropion and levofloxacin) can also lower the seizure threshold.
