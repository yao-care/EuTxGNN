# Decitabine: From Acute Myeloid Leukaemia to Refractory Cytopenia of Childhood

## One-Sentence Summary

Decitabine is a hypomethylating (DNA methyltransferase-inhibiting) agent, originally authorised in the EU for newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) in adults who cannot receive standard induction chemotherapy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **refractory cytopenia of childhood** (a paediatric myelodysplastic syndrome subtype), but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (one cohort study and one case report) supporting this specific direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, decitabine is a hypomethylating agent that inhibits DNA methyltransferase. This can restore normal differentiation in abnormal myeloid clones.

Refractory cytopenia of childhood is a paediatric form of myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS). Decitabine's effect on abnormal myeloid cells is the same type of effect that underlies its use in AML, so the prediction is mechanistically plausible.

Support is currently thin. It consists of a single-centre report (2022) of decitabine combined with a minimally myelosuppressive regimen as a bridge to allogeneic stem cell transplantation in children with MDS, and a 2026 case report of decitabine monotherapy in a child with MDS. No controlled evidence exists for this paediatric population.

**Other predicted indications.** The remaining predictions were also reviewed:
- **Aregenerative anaemia (rank 4):** the best-supported entry. It maps mainly to MDS trials, including a Phase 3 randomised trial (NCT00043134) and a published randomised comparison versus best supportive care in older RAEB patients (PMID 26400023). Evidence for aplastic anaemia itself is weak: the only dedicated Phase 2 trial (NCT04854889) was terminated after 3 patients. MDS evidence should not be extrapolated to aplastic anaemia without caution.
- **Unclassified myelodysplastic syndrome (rank 2) and upper aerodigestive tract neoplasm (rank 8):** only indirect or preclinical literature, with no registered trials (research questions).
- **Partial deletion of 5q, bulbar polio, severe congenital hypochromic anaemia with ringed sideroblasts, 5q35 microduplication syndrome, neuralgic amyotrophy and amyotrophic neuralgia:** no trials or literature; several are likely graph artefacts with no plausible mechanism.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for refractory cytopenia of childhood is mechanistically plausible but rests on one cohort study and one case report, with no registered trials (evidence level L4). The EU authorisation is cancelled, and decitabine's myelosuppressive profile is a concern in already cytopenic children. If a lead is sought among the predictions, the MDS and bone-marrow-failure entry (aregenerative anaemia) has the strongest data, supporting Proceed with Guardrails for MDS-type disease, but that evidence should not be extrapolated to aplastic anaemia or to children.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Prospective paediatric studies (or at least a systematic retrospective series) of decitabine in refractory cytopenia of childhood
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- EU label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, and clarification of why the Dacogen authorisation was cancelled
- A safety monitoring plan for children with pre-existing cytopenias, including management of interacting drugs
- Resolution of the unknown status of the Phase 3 trial NCT00043134 (or confirmation of its results)
