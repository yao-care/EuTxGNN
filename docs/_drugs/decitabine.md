## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Decitabine belongs to the class of hypomethylating agents. These drugs inhibit DNA methyltransferase and can help restore normal differentiation in abnormal blood-cell clones.

Refractory cytopenia of childhood is the most common subtype of myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) in children. Because the disease arises from a dysfunctional blood-forming clone, the hypomethylating mechanism is plausible for it.

The supporting studies are limited. One is a single-centre cohort of children with MDS treated with decitabine plus a minimally myelosuppressive regimen as a bridge to transplant. The other is a case report of decitabine monotherapy in an 8-year-old with MDS. Neither study is dedicated to refractory cytopenia of childhood alone, so the direct evidence for this specific disease remains very thin.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The model score is high, but no cited study is dedicated to refractory cytopenia of childhood, and there are no registered trials. The evidence is limited to one cohort study and one case report in paediatric MDS, so this remains a research question rather than a candidate for development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Prospective or well-designed retrospective studies in children with refractory cytopenia of childhood specifically
- Mechanism of action data and a mechanistic comparison with the approved AML use
- Paediatric safety and dosing data, including myelosuppression management
- Extraction of warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
