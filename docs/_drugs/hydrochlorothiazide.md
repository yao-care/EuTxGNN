# Hydrochlorothiazide: From Essential Hypertension to Malignant Hypertensive Renal Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Hydrochlorothiazide is a thiazide diuretic, used in the EU as a component of fixed-dose combinations (with irbesartan, telmisartan, or amlodipine/valsartan) to treat essential hypertension.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **malignant hypertensive renal disease**, but **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** currently support this specific prediction, so it rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, hydrochlorothiazide is a thiazide diuretic that inhibits the sodium-chloride cotransporter in the kidney, and its efficacy in essential hypertension is established. A nominal link to severe hypertension with kidney involvement is therefore plausible.

However, malignant hypertension is a hypertensive emergency managed with rapid-acting parenteral agents, and thiazides are not a recognised therapy for it. The high score (0.984) most likely reflects the drug's known association with hypertension rather than independent evidence. No trials or literature were found for this indication.

Other predictions in the set were also reviewed:
- **Malignant renovascular hypertension** (98.42%): Renovascular hypertension is driven largely by renin-angiotensin activation, and thiazide-induced volume depletion can further stimulate renin, so monotherapy is mechanistically questionable. No evidence was found. Decision: Hold.
- **Pulmonary hypertension (two forms) and Braddock syndrome** (97.9–98.3%): No credible mechanistic link. The retrieved literature includes a safety signal (hydrochlorothiazide-associated pulmonary edema) and preclinical studies that do not test the drug in this condition. Decision: Hold.
- **Chronic and acute pulmonary heart disease** (97.8% and 93.2%): Diuretics, including thiazides added to loop diuretics, are used for fluid overload in heart failure, giving a symptomatic rationale. The evidence is indirect (extrapolated from left heart failure, including the CLOROTIC trial analysis). Evidence level L4, listed as a research question.
- **Primary hereditary glaucoma, open-angle glaucoma, and hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp** (74.5–90.9%): No plausible mechanism or evidence. Decision: Hold.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug-disease relationships in the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text): liver failure and liver disease, cirrhosis, kidney disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, porphyria, angioedema (including hereditary and RAAS-blocker-induced forms), systemic lupus erythematosus, autoimmune hemolytic anemia, peptic ulcer disease, asthma, bronchitis, epilepsy, anxiety and neurotic disorders, myopathy and familial periodic paralysis.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Doxycycline and atropine are Minor.
- **Literature safety signals**: case reports of hydrochlorothiazide-associated pulmonary edema and drug-induced methemoglobinemia.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top predictions (malignant hypertensive renal disease and malignant renovascular hypertension) are supported only by the model score (L5), with no trials or literature. Thiazides are not a recognised treatment for hypertensive emergencies. In addition, all 13 EU licences are cancelled, and label safety data are missing. The only direction with some indirect support is diuretic use in right-heart congestion (pulmonary heart disease, L4), which is better treated as a research question than as a repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety data from the SmPC (warnings and contraindications), which is currently a blocking gap
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of the cancelled EU licence status and whether a valid hydrochlorothiazide product exists
- Direct clinical evidence (trials or cohort studies) of hydrochlorothiazide in the predicted indications
- For pulmonary heart disease, a focused review of thiazide plus loop diuretic use in right-heart failure
