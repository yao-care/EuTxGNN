# Bictegravir: From HIV-1 Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Bictegravir is an HIV-1 integrase strand transfer inhibitor, originally used to treat HIV-1 infection in adults and children aged 2 years and older (weighing at least 14 kg).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus infection**, but the supporting evidence is only **3 preclinical or structural publications and no clinical trials**. This is a veterinary and animal-model indication with no direct human clinical relevance.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, bictegravir is an HIV-1 integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI). Its efficacy in HIV-1 infection is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV).

SIV integrase is structurally related to HIV integrase, and published intasome structures explain how INSTIs bind. In vitro data show that bictegravir is active against INSTI-resistant SIVmac239. A humanized mouse model has also been proposed for testing antiviral strategies against both SIV and HIV.

This is therefore mainly an animal-model and preclinical rationale. It supports preclinical HIV research, not a human therapeutic use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 81 interactions are recorded. Those listed as Major are mainly polyvalent-cation products that can reduce absorption of integrase inhibitors:
  - Aluminium and magnesium antacids (aluminium hydroxide, magaldrate, magnesium carbonate, chloride and citrate)
  - Calcium salts (acetate, carbonate, citrate, glubionate, gluconate, lactate, phosphate)
  - Iron, kaolin and attapulgite
- Moderate interactions include aprepitant, clarithromycin, dexamethasone, eliglustat and glycerol phenylbutyrate.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The SIV prediction rests on in vitro, structural and animal-model data only, with no clinical trials, and SIV is not a human disease. The product's only EU authorization is cancelled. The other high-scoring predictions are mostly graph-based artifacts or on-label HIV use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- A clear research purpose, such as use in preclinical HIV cure models, rather than a therapeutic claim
- Confirmation of the current regulatory status of Biktarvy in the EU

**Note on other predictions in the data:**
- **AIDS-related complex** (evidence level L1, Proceed with Guardrails) is an on-label HIV-1 use, not true repurposing. It is supported by the ARTISTRY-1 randomized phase 3 trial (PMID [41763229](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41763229/), 2026) of a bictegravir-lenacapavir single-tablet switch. Guardrails: screen for integrase, emtricitabine or tenofovir resistance, and review drug interactions.
- **Congenital HIV** (evidence level L3, Research Question) rests on observational pregnancy-exposure data.
- **Hepatitis A, C and E, and Omsk hemorrhagic fever** have no plausible mechanism, and the related trials do not test bictegravir for these diseases.
