## Cobimetinib: From BRAF V600-Mutant Melanoma to Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Cobimetinib was originally used, in combination with vemurafenib, to treat unresectable or metastatic melanoma with a BRAF V600 mutation.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this direction. The prediction rests on a computational score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, cobimetinib is a MEK1/2 inhibitor that blocks the MAPK/ERK signalling pathway. Its efficacy in BRAF V600-mutant melanoma, where it is combined with the BRAF inhibitor vemurafenib, has been established.

MAPK/ERK pathway activation has been implicated in neuroinflammation and motor neuron stress responses in ALS models. This offers a plausible but unvalidated rationale for the prediction. Melanoma and ALS are very different diseases, and no direct similarity between the original and new indication has been established.

Important caveats:
- The TxGNN score is very high (0.9973), but it is a computational prediction only.
- Brain penetration is a concern, because cobimetinib is thought to be a P-glycoprotein substrate. This has not been assessed here.
- Other top predictions are mostly rare motor neuron or genetic disorders (e.g., Mills syndrome, monomelic amyotrophy, ALS type 22). They share the same speculative rationale and are likely correlated with the ALS prediction. Several others (e.g., polymicrogyria, axial spondylometaphyseal dysplasia) have no supported mechanistic link.

- **Class-related concerns noted in the predictions**: MEK inhibitors carry known ocular toxicity (e.g., serous retinopathy). Other toxicities of concern include dermatologic, hepatic and cardiac effects. These matter especially for any proposed use in retinal or benign, self-limiting conditions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The ALS prediction has a very high model score but no registered trials, no literature and only a speculative mechanistic link (L5).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Preclinical evidence that MEK inhibition is beneficial in ALS models
- Assessment of central nervous system penetration, including P-gp efflux
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, to support safety screening
- A risk-benefit assessment for long-term use in ALS, considering ocular, dermatologic, hepatic and cardiac toxicity
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorization status and of the product's availability
