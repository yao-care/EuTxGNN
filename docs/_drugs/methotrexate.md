## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, methotrexate is an antifolate that inhibits dihydrofolate reductase and so interferes with DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells. Its efficacy in ALL, an oncology indication, shows that it can act on proliferating malignant cells. Mechanistically, it may therefore be applicable to other fast-growing tumours.

Pulmonary blastoma is a rare lung malignancy. No studies were found that test methotrexate in this disease, so the link is an inference from the drug's class and its use in other cancers, not demonstrated evidence. The model assigns a high score, but a model score alone is not clinical proof.

For context, other high-ranking predictions have somewhat more support:
- **Hodgkin lymphoma**: methotrexate has a history in the VBM regimen (vinblastine, bleomycin, methotrexate) for early-stage disease, with reported complete remission rates of 94–100% in small trials. Most registered trials are broad lymphoma, transplant or graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) prophylaxis studies in which methotrexate is a background component.
- **Rhabdomyosarcoma**: a Phase 2 trial of high-dose methotrexate in untreated high-risk children (PMID 9329466) and retrospective regimen data exist, but they are older and the incremental benefit is unclear.
- **Primary pulmonary lymphoma**: methotrexate is established in CNS lymphoma regimens, which gives indirect plausibility, but no controlled study in pulmonary lymphoma was found.
- **Small cell lung carcinoma**: methotrexate appeared in older SCLC combination regimens, but the registered trials are in non-small cell lung cancer.

## Safety Considerations

Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC for the authoritative safety information.

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from a drug-disease knowledge graph, not label text): liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia), interstitial lung disease, pulmonary fibrosis, peptic and gastric ulcer disease, ulcerative colitis and inflammatory bowel disease, stomatitis, pyoureter, substance abuse/dependence, and immune system disease.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified; the first 20 retrieved are summarised here. Major-level interactions include rabeprazole, amoxicillin and acetylsalicylic acid. Moderate-level interactions include doxycycline, tetracycline, clarithromycin, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), mesalazine, balsalazide, amphotericin B, bupropion, pioglitazone, acarbose and chenodeoxycholic acid. Potassium citrate is a minor interaction.
- **Lymphoproliferative signal**: the literature describes methotrexate-associated lymphoproliferative disorders, including cases presenting in the lung. This matters for any lymphoma-related repurposing direction.
- **Pulmonary toxicity**: methotrexate-induced lung disease is described in the literature and is relevant to any use in lung or thoracic tumours.
