# Insulin Aspart: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin aspart is a rapid-acting insulin analogue, originally authorised in the EU for the treatment of diabetes mellitus.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **type 1 diabetes mellitus**, which is in fact an established, on-label use rather than a true repurposing case.
The data compiled for this report include **33 clinical trials** and **20 publications** in this direction, but **all six EU authorizations are cancelled**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, insulin aspart is a rapid-acting insulin analogue. It binds the insulin receptor, promotes peripheral glucose uptake and suppresses hepatic glucose output.

Type 1 diabetes is characterised by an absence of endogenous insulin, so replacement with insulin is the cornerstone of treatment. The original indication (diabetes mellitus) already covers type 1 diabetes, so this prediction confirms an existing use rather than a new one. The very high score is consistent with this.

Other lower-ranked predictions, such as autoimmune oophoritis, opsismodysplasia, stiff person syndrome and the lipodystrophies, have no clinical evidence and no plausible mechanism, and are likely graph artefacts. Drug-induced localized lipodystrophy is a known adverse effect of injected insulin, so it is a safety concern rather than an opportunity. Permanent neonatal diabetes and pancreatic agenesis are biologically plausible, but only indirect evidence is available.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SmPC for safety information. No warnings, contraindications or drug interaction data were available in the data compiled for this report.

One point from the predictions is worth noting: injected insulin is a known cause of localized lipodystrophy at injection sites, so injection-site rotation and monitoring are relevant.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and systematic reviews support insulin aspart in type 1 diabetes, which is an established use, so the evidence level is L1. However, all six EU authorizations are cancelled and no safety label data were available, so the decision carries conditions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarify why all EU authorizations are cancelled (for example, commercial withdrawal versus a safety reason) and confirm the current regulatory status.
- Obtain the current SmPC warnings and contraindications, which are missing and would block safety screening.
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Confirm that the lower-ranked predictions are not pursued without supporting evidence. Only permanent neonatal diabetes and pancreatic agenesis merit further research questions, and genotype-dependent management should be considered for the former.
