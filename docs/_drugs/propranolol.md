## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Propranolol is a non-selective beta-adrenergic blocker, and its efficacy in infantile haemangioma is established.

For the top-ranked prediction, distal myopathy of the Tateyama type, **no plausible mechanistic link** between beta-adrenergic blockade and this rare distal myopathy is established. The high score of 0.994 is a model output only, and there are no trials or publications behind it. The same applies to the second prediction, congenital myopathy with excess of thin filaments.

Several lower-ranked predictions have more indirect support:

- **Haemangioma-related conditions** (intramuscular haemangioma, breast epithelioid haemangioma, breast capillary haemangioma): these are close to the approved indication. Proposed mechanisms include vasoconstriction, inhibition of angiogenic signalling (VEGF, bFGF) and endothelial apoptosis. However, the literature concerns **infantile** haemangioma, not these specific entities, so this is extrapolation (evidence level L4).
- **Cardiomyopathy**: beta-blockade reduces heart rate, contractility and myocardial oxygen demand, and improves diastolic filling. Older studies (1980–1983) examined propranolol in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Many of the records are generic drug monographs, and the designs could not be verified from titles alone (evidence level L3).
- **Cirrhotic cardiomyopathy**: a 2024 report suggests propranolol can correct prolonged QT intervals in cirrhosis. Other work reports blunted cardiovascular responses and possible circulatory and renal impairment in advanced disease, so safety is a concern (evidence level L4).

- **Disease contraindication signals (from a knowledge graph, not label text)**: these include bronchitis, kidney disease, liver disease, pancreatitis, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, gout, myasthenia gravis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and several myopathies and muscle diseases, including familial periodic paralysis. The muscle-disease signals are notable because the top-ranked prediction is itself a myopathy.
- **Literature safety signals**: propranolol-induced hypoglycaemia in infants, and possible circulatory and renal impairment with non-selective beta-blockers in advanced cirrhosis with refractory ascites.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, distal myopathy, Tateyama type, rests on a model score alone (L5), with no trials, no publications and no plausible mechanism. The knowledge graph also flags myopathies and muscle diseases as contraindication signals for propranolol. The EU authorisation for the original indication is cancelled, and warnings and contraindications from the product information were not available.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and any biological rationale linking beta-blockade to distal myopathy, Tateyama type
- Warnings and contraindications from the EMA product information, which are required before safety screening
- Preclinical or case-level evidence in the predicted myopathy
- Resolution of the knowledge-graph contraindication signals for muscle disease
- Consider redirecting evaluation to better-supported candidates (e.g. haemangioma subtypes, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy), each needing disease-specific evidence rather than extrapolation from infantile haemangioma
