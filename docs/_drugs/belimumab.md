## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for belimumab: "BENLYSTA is a BLyS-specific inhibitor that blocks the binding of soluble BLyS, a B-cell survival factor, to its receptors on B cells. BENLYSTA does not bind B cells directly, but by binding BLyS, BENLYSTA inhibits the survival of B cells, including autoreactive B cells, and reduces the differentiation of B cells into immunoglobulin-producing plasma cells." ([DailyMed: Benlysta (belimumab) prescribing information, GlaxoSmithKline LLC](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=2fa3c528-1777-4628-8a55-a69dae2381a3), label version 2026-10-30; accessed 2026-11-19.) Based on known pharmacology, belimumab binds soluble BLyS/BAFF and lowers B-cell survival, which reduces autoantibody-driven immune activity.

Primary release disorder of platelets is a defect in the release of granule contents from platelets. It is an intrinsic platelet function problem rather than a B-cell or BAFF-driven process. No established mechanistic link to belimumab was identified, so the high TxGNN score should be read as a computational signal only, not as biological or clinical support.
