## One-Sentence Summary

Fenofibrate is a lipid-lowering drug, authorised in the European Union in fixed-dose combination products for mixed dyslipidaemia in high cardiovascular risk adults.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **cholesterol-ester transfer protein (CETP) deficiency**, but there are **0 registered clinical trials** and only **3 preclinical or mechanistic publications**, none of which studies the disease itself in patients.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, fenofibrate is a PPAR-alpha agonist. This class raises HDL cholesterol and lowers triglycerides, partly by increasing apoA-I and apoA-II expression and lipoprotein lipase activity.

The link to CETP deficiency is indirect and pathway-based, running through HDL metabolism and lipid remodelling. Mouse studies show that fenofibrate enlarges HDL particles by inducing the phospholipid transfer protein (PLTP) gene. Another mouse study combined fenofibrate with CETP inhibition and observed increased HDL-C and reduced LDL-C.

The rationale is weak, however. CETP deficiency is already characterised by markedly elevated HDL-C, and fenofibrate would not correct the underlying genetic defect. The therapeutic benefit is therefore unclear. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction and is not backed by clinical data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Relations recorded in the knowledge graph (not label text):** The knowledge graph links fenofibrate with a number of conditions as contraindications. These include liver failure, liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease and gallstones (cholelithiasis), muscle disorders (myopathy, rhabdomyolysis), impaired renal function, and blood disorders (thrombocytopenia, anaemia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, neutropenia and several congenital neutropenia syndromes).
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found, and the 20 listed in the data compiled for this report are all of moderate severity. They include sulfonylureas (acetohexamide, chlorpropamide, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide), many insulin products, chenodeoxycholic acid and esomeprazole.

Please refer to the SmPC for authoritative warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No clinical trial or human study directly addresses fenofibrate in CETP deficiency, and the three publications are preclinical or mechanistic, so the evidence level is L5. The mechanistic rationale is also unclear, since the disease already features high HDL-C. The high TxGNN score alone does not justify proceeding.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for fenofibrate
- Evidence on whether lowering triglycerides or modifying lipoproteins offers any clinical benefit in CETP deficiency
- Review of the SmPC warnings and contraindications, which were not available for this report
- Review of the safety signals above (hepatic, biliary, muscle, renal and haematological) if any study is planned
