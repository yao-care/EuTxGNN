## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on the information available in the evidence pack, Melphalan is an alkylating agent that causes DNA cross-linking and double-strand breaks, giving it potent cytotoxic activity against rapidly proliferating tumor cells. This mechanistic profile is what underlies its use across multiple malignancies as a component of high-dose chemotherapy regimens.

Germ cell tumors — particularly platinum-resistant or relapsed cases — are highly sensitive to alkylating-agent cytotoxicity. High-dose melphalan combined with autologous stem cell rescue is already an established salvage chemotherapy strategy in relapsed/refractory germ cell tumors, which gives this prediction a mechanistic and practical foundation beyond a pure model score.

This is corroborated by a directly relevant Phase 2 trial (NCT00936936, n=64) that specifically enrolled patients with poor-prognosis relapsed germ-cell tumors and used melphalan as part of a high-dose combination regimen, together with decades of literature on melphalan-based ("sarcolysin") chemotherapy for testicular seminoma dating back to the 1950s–1970s.
