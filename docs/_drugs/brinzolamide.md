# Brinzolamide: From Ocular Hypertension and Open-Angle Glaucoma to Primary Hereditary Glaucoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Brinzolamide is a topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, originally used to lower raised intraocular pressure in ocular hypertension and open-angle glaucoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary hereditary glaucoma**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this specific prediction.
The stronger evidence sits with the neighbouring predictions (open-angle glaucoma and glaucoma), which are essentially the labelled use rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, brinzolamide inhibits carbonic anhydrase II in the ciliary processes. This reduces aqueous humour secretion and lowers intraocular pressure (IOP). Its efficacy in open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension is well established.

Hereditary glaucoma is also driven by raised IOP, so lowering aqueous production is plausible as a mechanism. However, hereditary and congenital glaucomas are often primarily surgical diseases. Topical IOP-lowering therapy would at best be adjunctive.

The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only. No trials or literature were retrieved for this specific form of glaucoma.

For context, the other top predictions fall into three groups:
- **Already within the labelled use:** glaucoma 1 (open angle), glaucoma, and open angle glaucoma. These have large Phase 3 programmes and are supported by Phase 3 trials, mostly with brinzolamide-containing combinations or generic equivalence designs.
- **Weak or cautionary:** closed-angle glaucoma. One RCT (PMID 35026861) examined brinzolamide in acute primary angle closure, but there are also case reports of brinzolamide-induced ciliary body effusion with secondary angle closure. Hydrophthalmos has no evidence.
- **Likely artifacts:** commissural lip fistula, osteoradionecrosis of the mandible, oral leukoedema and burning mouth syndrome. There is no plausible mechanistic link and no evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 32 interactions are recorded, all with an unclassified severity level. Examples include pantoprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, prednisone, dexamethasone, simvastatin, warfarin and clopidogrel.
- **Class-related cautions:** brinzolamide is a sulfonamide derivative. Sulfonamide hypersensitivity and corneal endothelial effects should be monitored. A published case report describes ciliary body effusion with secondary angle closure.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
For primary hereditary glaucoma, the prediction rests on the model score alone, with no trials or publications. These forms are mainly managed surgically, so topical IOP lowering would only be adjunctive. The well-supported glaucoma predictions are essentially the already labelled use. For those, a "Proceed with Guardrails" decision would apply, but the market status of the single EU authorization is "Cancelled only".

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical or observational data in hereditary or congenital glaucoma, including paediatric safety
- Confirmation of the current EU and per-jurisdiction availability, since the only EU authorization is cancelled
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- SmPC warnings and contraindications for a full safety review
- Review of the 32 recorded drug interactions, whose severity levels are unclassified
