## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available from the data compiled for this report. Lidocaine is generally known as a sodium channel blocker and local anaesthetic. Its authorized use in premature ejaculation relies on reducing penile sensitivity, so it is a symptomatic, sensory-dampening effect rather than a disease-modifying one.

The predicted ocular indication is linked to the same property. As a topical ocular anaesthetic, lidocaine could relieve discomfort in punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis. However, there is no evidence that it changes the course of the disease. Topical anaesthetics also carry a known risk of corneal epithelial toxicity, which is a real concern in a disease that already involves damage to the corneal epithelium. The high TxGNN score therefore should be read as a hypothesis, not as support for efficacy.

For context, the same model produced other predictions with somewhat more related data, but these also mostly reflect existing anaesthetic use:
- **Conjunctival disorder**: trials and publications are about anaesthesia during pterygium and eyelid surgery, not treatment of a conjunctival disease.
- **Atopic conjunctivitis**: only a 2023 mechanistic study of nerve and goblet cell interaction and a 1994 nasal anaesthesia study exist, which is hypothesis-generating at best.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone, with no clinical trials or publications for punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis. The plausible benefit is only symptomatic, and the known corneal toxicity of topical anaesthetics points in an unfavourable direction. The only EU authorization is also cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a clear rationale for a disease-modifying effect on the corneal or conjunctival epithelium
- Preclinical data on corneal epithelial safety
- Review of the official product information (warnings and contraindications), which was not available
- Confirmation of a suitable ocular formulation and route, since no route compatibility has been established
- If an ocular direction is pursued, consider the better-supported symptomatic uses (procedural ocular anaesthesia) or the allergic conjunctivitis neural-mechanism hypothesis as a starting point for research questions
