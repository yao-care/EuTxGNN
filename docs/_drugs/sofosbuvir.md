## One-Sentence Summary

Sofosbuvir is a nucleotide polymerase inhibitor, originally used in combination with other medicines to treat chronic hepatitis C.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**, but most of the **11 related clinical trials** and **20 publications** concern HCV/HBV co-infection rather than HBV treatment. Direct evidence is limited to one small Phase 2 pilot study (n=21).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. From general knowledge, sofosbuvir is a nucleotide analogue inhibitor of the HCV NS5B RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. Its efficacy in chronic hepatitis C is well established.

HBV, however, replicates through a reverse-transcriptase (DNA polymerase) mechanism, so a direct antiviral effect is not expected. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects network proximity between viral hepatitis diseases rather than a true mechanistic effect on HBV.

There is one signal worth noting. Retrospective data showed a modest HBsAg reduction when ledipasvir/sofosbuvir was given to HBV/HCV co-infected patients. A small Phase 2 study (NCT03312023, PMID 36045503) tested whether this could be reproduced in HBV mono-infection. Its HBV-specific activity remains unproven.

The data also rank other candidate indications, such as hepatitis E (rank 2), which are mechanistically more plausible because HEV is an RNA virus with an RNA polymerase. This report focuses on the top-ranked prediction.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Sofosbuvir targets the HCV polymerase and has no expected direct activity against HBV. The only HBV-specific evidence is one small, single-arm Phase 2 study (n=21), and most other trials are HCV or HCV/HBV co-infection studies in which HBV reactivation is a safety concern. The high model score likely reflects network proximity rather than a mechanistic effect.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to clarify any HBV-relevant activity
- Controlled studies of HBV-specific efficacy (HBsAg and HBV DNA) in HBV mono-infection
- Full SmPC warnings and contraindications, since sofosbuvir is currently without a valid EU authorization
- A reactivation-monitoring plan for any patient with HBV exposure
- Consideration of hepatitis E (rank 2, with a Phase 2 pilot and in vitro support) as a mechanistically more plausible research direction
