## Sofosbuvir: Hepatitis B virus infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Sofosbuvir is an antiviral nucleotide analogue that inhibits the hepatitis C virus NS5B RNA polymerase, and it is currently marketed in the EU.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **hepatitis B virus infection**, but only **one small single-arm Phase 2 study** (ledipasvir/sofosbuvir, n=21) and its publication address this disease directly. The evidence level is **L3**.
The other 11 registered trials and most of the 19 publications concern hepatitis C treatment or hepatitis B reactivation in co-infected patients.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Sofosbuvir is a nucleotide analogue that blocks the NS5B RNA-dependent RNA polymerase of the hepatitis C virus.

A direct antiviral effect on hepatitis B is not expected. HBV replicates through a reverse-transcriptase (DNA polymerase) mechanism, which is a different target from NS5B. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects network proximity between the two viral hepatitis conditions rather than a demonstrated mechanistic effect.

One signal does support further study. A retrospective observation in patients with both hepatitis B and C found a modest drop in hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) during ledipasvir/sofosbuvir treatment. This prompted a Phase 2 pilot in people with hepatitis B alone (NCT03312023; PMID 36045503). The study is small, uncontrolled, and its HBV-specific activity remains unproven.

## Safety Considerations

- **Hepatitis B reactivation**: Published reports and cohort data describe HBV reactivation in people with HBV exposure who receive sofosbuvir-based regimens for hepatitis C. This matters directly for any use in hepatitis B.
- **Drug interactions** (8 recorded):
  - Major: apalutamide, enzalutamide, lorlatinib.
  - Moderate: fostamatinib, warfarin, dicoumarol, encorafenib.
  - Minor: cyclosporine.

Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Only one small, single-arm Phase 2 study directly addresses hepatitis B, and its antiviral activity is unproven. The drug's target is not an HBV enzyme, and the wider evidence is dominated by HBV reactivation during hepatitis C treatment. The high model score alone does not justify moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results of NCT03312023 and PMID 36045503: HBsAg and HBV DNA changes and durability of any effect
- Laboratory data showing whether sofosbuvir or its active metabolite has any activity against HBV
- A larger controlled study in HBV mono-infection, with a defined plan for monitoring HBV reactivation
- Mechanism of action data and the full SmPC safety review
