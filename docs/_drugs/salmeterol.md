# Salmeterol: From Asthma to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Salmeterol is a long-acting beta2-agonist, originally used (in the combination product Seffalair Spiromax) for the regular treatment of asthma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, with **9 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this prediction. The evidence mostly concerns COPD with chronic bronchitis, where salmeterol (alone or with fluticasone) is already an established therapy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Salmeterol is a long-acting beta2-adrenoceptor agonist. It relaxes bronchial smooth muscle by raising intracellular cAMP, and it may also improve mucociliary clearance. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description reflects general pharmacology and the mechanistic rationale in the evidence.

Asthma and chronic bronchitis (the chronic bronchitis phenotype of COPD) are both obstructive airway diseases. Both involve airway narrowing, and bronchodilation is a cornerstone of treatment for each. A small study of 14 patients with mild-to-moderate chronic bronchitis examined salmeterol's acute effect on mucociliary and cough clearance. Salmeterol, alone or combined with fluticasone, is already used in COPD. The prediction is therefore a close extension of the approved respiratory use rather than a distant repurposing.

Caveat: most trials enrolled COPD patients in general, not bronchitis specifically. Several of them did not test salmeterol as the main agent, but used it as an active comparator.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph** (derived from drug-disease links, not label text): hypertension, long QT syndrome, epilepsy, hyperglycaemia, herpes simplex virus keratitis, hypopituitarism and other pituitary hormone deficiencies, thymus gland disease and osteoporosis. These should be verified against the product label.
- **Drug interactions**: 600 interactions were recorded. Examples:
  - **Major**: clarithromycin.
  - **Moderate**: epinephrine, several antidiabetic agents (acarbose, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, alogliptin, chlorpropamide, albiglutide, dulaglutide), famotidine, dolasetron, diethylpropion and isometheptene.
  - **Minor**: corticosteroids such as hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, budesonide and beclomethasone.
- **Class warning**: Cochrane reviews of regular LABA use in asthma report a serious-adverse-event signal, particularly without an inhaled corticosteroid. Salmeterol should be used with an inhaled corticosteroid in asthma.

Please refer to the SmPC for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- Several completed Phase 3 trials and RCTs support salmeterol and salmeterol/fluticasone in COPD, including the chronic bronchitis phenotype, and the drug class is already established there. However, the evidence concerns COPD rather than bronchitis as a standalone condition, and the current EU authorization is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data confirmed from DrugBank.
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC.
- Clarification of the exact bronchitis population (chronic bronchitis within COPD vs. acute bronchitis), since the evidence does not address acute bronchitis.
- Confirmation of the regulatory pathway, given that the only EU license is cancelled.
- A safety plan covering LABA use with inhaled corticosteroids, cardiovascular risk, and interactions with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as clarithromycin.
