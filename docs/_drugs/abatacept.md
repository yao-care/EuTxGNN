## Abatacept: Ankylosing spondylitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Abatacept is a T-cell co-stimulation blocker that is currently marketed in the EU.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **ankylosing spondylitis**.
Direct support is limited to **one small open-label Phase 2 pilot trial** and **one open-label 24-week pilot publication**, and several reviews describe abatacept as not effective in axial disease.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Abatacept is a biologic that blocks T-cell co-stimulation, and this pathway is plausibly relevant in spondyloarthritis. That is the basis for the prediction.

Clinical experience does not strongly support the idea, though. Reviews report that biologics targeting T cells, including abatacept, have not been effective in axial spondyloarthritis. Tumour necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors and IL-17 inhibitors perform much better. The mechanistic link is therefore plausible in theory but weakly supported in practice.

The only direct clinical evidence is a small, uncontrolled, open-label pilot programme. There is no randomized trial in ankylosing spondylitis, so the high model score should be read as a hypothesis, not as proof.

## Safety Considerations

Specific warnings and contraindications for this product are not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC.

**Drug Interactions**: 73 interactions were recorded. Main examples:
- **Major**: adalimumab, certolizumab pegol, baricitinib, and live BCG vaccine (Tice strain).
- **Moderate**: anakinra, canakinumab, alemtuzumab, alefacept, azathioprine, roflumilast, and several live or live-attenuated vaccines (including adenovirus type 4 and cholera vaccines).
- **Minor**: zinc salts (acetate, gluconate, sulfate, chloride).

Combining abatacept with other biologics or JAK inhibitors, and using it with live vaccines, needs particular caution.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for ankylosing spondylitis is level L3. It rests on a small, uncontrolled, open-label pilot with no randomized trial, and published reviews describe abatacept as not effective in axial disease. The high TxGNN score (0.9991) is not enough to justify progression.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A randomized, placebo-controlled trial in ankylosing spondylitis, or the full results of the existing pilot trial.
- Comparative data against TNF and IL-17 inhibitors, which are established treatments.
- Mechanism-of-action data and a mechanistic rationale specific to axial disease.
- Safety information from the SmPC, including warnings and contraindications.
