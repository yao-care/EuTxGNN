## Abatacept: selective T-cell co-stimulation modulator → ankylosing spondylitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Abatacept is a biologic that blocks T-cell co-stimulation and is authorised in the EU for several inflammatory joint diseases.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **ankylosing spondylitis**, supported by **1 small Phase 2 open-label pilot trial** and **several reviews, one open-label pilot study and no randomised trials** in this disease. Reviews describe abatacept as not effective in axial disease.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Abatacept is a fusion protein that blocks T-cell co-stimulation, and this mechanism is established in other immune-mediated joint diseases.

Ankylosing spondylitis is also an immune-mediated inflammatory disease, so blocking T-cell activation is biologically plausible. Clinical experience, however, points the other way. Reviews report that abatacept, like other non-TNF biologics targeting IL-1, IL-6 and T cells, has not been effective in axial spondyloarthritis, while TNF and IL-17 inhibitors work well. The prediction is therefore mechanistically plausible but clinically doubtful.

## Safety Considerations

Moderate-level ones include other biologics (anakinra, canakinumab, alemtuzumab), azathioprine, roflumilast and several live vaccines. Zinc salts are Minor.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence in ankylosing spondylitis is a small, uncontrolled open-label pilot, and published reviews describe abatacept as not effective in axial disease. No cited study provides controlled efficacy data, so the evidence rests on a model prediction supported by weak pilot data. A high TxGNN score alone does not justify moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of the NCT00558506 pilot and the published 24-week pilot study, with a critical appraisal of the efficacy signal
- A randomised, placebo-controlled trial in ankylosing spondylitis, if the pilot signal justifies one
- Mechanism-of-action data and a comparison against TNF and IL-17 inhibitors
- Warnings and contraindications taken from the SmPC
