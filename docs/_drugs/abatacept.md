# Abatacept: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Abatacept is a T-cell co-stimulation blocker, originally used to treat rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis and polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid vasculitis**, but support is limited to **1 unrelated clinical trial** and a handful of **case reports** (one of which describes vasculitis appearing during abatacept therapy).
Among the other predictions, the strongest evidence is for inflammatory spondylopathy (driven by psoriatic arthritis data) and for polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis, which is already an approved indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Abatacept is known to be a fusion protein of the Fc region of human IgG1 and the extracellular domain of CTLA-4. It blocks CD80/CD86–CD28 T-cell co-stimulation, and its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis has been established.

Rheumatoid vasculitis is a serious extra-articular complication of rheumatoid arthritis. It is driven by immune-complex and T-cell-mediated vascular inflammation, so blocking T-cell activation is biologically plausible. However, the clinical evidence is inconsistent. One case report (PMID 29930884) and another (PMID 22124545) describe a good response to abatacept. A further report (PMID 27052429) describes new-onset rheumatoid vasculitis during abatacept therapy that improved after rituximab. There are no controlled data.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (73 recorded interactions; the main ones are listed):
- **Major:** adalimumab, baricitinib, certolizumab pegol, BCG live vaccine
- **Moderate:** other immunosuppressive biologics and agents (anakinra, canakinumab, alefacept, alemtuzumab, azathioprine), roflumilast, and several live vaccines (adenovirus type 4, anthrax, cholera, *Candida albicans*)
- **Minor:** zinc salts (acetate, gluconate, sulfate, chloride)

In practice, combining abatacept with other biologic immunosuppressants and giving live vaccines call for particular caution. Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
For rheumatoid vasculitis, the evidence consists only of a few case reports with conflicting directions, and the only registered trial is unrelated, so the evidence level is L4. In addition, the EU authorization is recorded as cancelled, and safety label data could not be retrieved, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety information from the SmPC or package insert (warnings and contraindications), which is currently a blocking gap
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the current EU authorization status, since only a cancelled record was found
- Controlled or at least larger observational data for abatacept in rheumatoid vasculitis, taking into account the report of vasculitis onset during abatacept therapy
- If a broader programme is wanted, a better-supported alternative is to focus on the psoriatic arthritis subset of inflammatory spondylopathy, where a completed Phase 3 RCT exists. This route is already close to the approved psoriatic arthritis label and would be a label-extension question rather than true repurposing.
