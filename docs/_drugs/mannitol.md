## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for mannitol. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, mannitol is an osmotic diuretic that increases free-water excretion by raising plasma osmolality and reducing renal tubular water reabsorption.

NSIAD is a condition in which a gain-of-function mutation in the vasopressin V2 receptor produces an antidiuretic-like state (dilutional hyponatremia) despite low or undetectable ADH levels. The theoretical rationale linking mannitol to NSIAD is that its osmotic diuretic action could, in principle, increase free-water clearance and help correct the hyponatremia seen in NSIAD.

However, this is a mechanistic extrapolation rather than a treatment pathway validated by direct clinical or experimental data. The one supporting publication is a general review on pitfalls in evaluating hyponatremia, which discusses NSIAD as a differential diagnosis but does not evaluate mannitol as a treatment. No clinical trials currently test this drug-disease pairing.
