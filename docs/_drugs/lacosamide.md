## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on the literature, lacosamide enhances slow inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels and binds CRMP2, which may stabilise neuronal hyperexcitability.

Other sodium-channel-active antiseizure drugs, such as lamotrigine and carbamazepine, are used as mood stabilisers. This offers a plausible mechanistic link to mania. However, no prospective trial supports it for this disease, and the link remains a hypothesis.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 25 interactions were identified in the interaction screen. Major-level interactions include dolasetron, ceritinib and siponimod. Moderate-level interactions include clarithromycin, ethanol, cobicistat, chloroquine, hydroxychloroquine, mefloquine, lidocaine, ketoconazole, pasireotide, alectinib, brigatinib, crizotinib, fingolimod, idelalisib, imatinib, ozanimod and sunitinib. Many of these are QT- or conduction-related, which suggests caution with cardiac rhythm effects.
- **Literature safety signal**: one case report describes neutropenia precipitated by lacosamide in a patient with bipolar disorder (PMID 30275630).

For label warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high (0.9996), but no clinical trial studies lacosamide in manic bipolar affective disorder. The available literature consists of small, uncontrolled or retrospective reports, one pilot in bipolar depression, and case reports, and one of these reports a safety signal (neutropenia). The evidence level is therefore L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A prospective, controlled trial of lacosamide in acute mania or bipolar maintenance
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Review of the SmPC warnings and contraindications, with attention to cardiac conduction and haematological monitoring
- Confirmation of the dosage forms for the authorised products
