## Lamivudine: From Chronic Hepatitis B and HIV Infection to Feline Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Lamivudine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor, authorised in the EU for chronic hepatitis B and, as part of combination therapy, for HIV infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome**, which is a veterinary condition rather than a human disease.
Support is limited to **3 publications** (two cohort studies in cats and one in vitro study) and **no registered clinical trials**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Lamivudine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI). Its efficacy in HIV and in chronic hepatitis B is reflected in the EU authorisations.

Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is a lentivirus whose reverse transcriptase is closely related to that of HIV-1. Reverse transcriptase inhibitors that are active against HIV are often active against FIV as well, so the mechanism is plausible. The supporting studies are veterinary, including zidovudine plus lamivudine regimens in naturally infected cats and an in vitro study of zidovudine, lamivudine and abacavir against FIV. They are not human clinical evidence.

The same data also list other predictions that do not look promising. Simian immunodeficiency virus infection is supported only by macaque studies, and the human-relevant indication (HIV) is already authorised, so it adds no new repurposing value. Chronic hepatitis C is supported only by general hepatitis reviews, and lamivudine does not meaningfully inhibit the HCV polymerase. Two other predictions have no trials or literature: a rare neurodevelopmental disorder, and an obsolete familial combined hyperlipidemia term.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is for a veterinary disease. The only support is two feline cohort studies and one in vitro study, with no human trials. Lamivudine is already authorised in the EU for HIV and hepatitis B, so the prediction adds little new value for human medicine.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of whether the target is a human or a veterinary indication. If veterinary, a separate veterinary regulatory and development assessment is needed.
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, which are not yet available in the compiled data
- Controlled studies that directly assess lamivudine in FIV-infected cats, if the veterinary route is pursued
