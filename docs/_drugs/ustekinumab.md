# Ustekinumab: From Plaque Psoriasis to Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Ustekinumab is an IL-12/IL-23 antagonist originally used for moderate to severe plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis**, with **6 related clinical trials** (2 directly testing ustekinumab in atopic dermatitis) and **20 publications** compiled for this direction. The clinical data do not show consistent efficacy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, ustekinumab blocks the shared p40 subunit of IL-12 and IL-23, which inhibits Th1 and Th17 signaling. These pathways contribute to inflammatory skin disease, including psoriasis, its approved indication.

The link to dermatitis is only partial. Atopic dermatitis is driven mainly by Th2 (IL-4/IL-13) signaling, with variable Th17/Th22 involvement. Contact dermatitis has Th1/Th17 components, which gives a plausible but less established link. The very high TxGNN score is supported by dermatologic biology, but clinical studies in atopic dermatitis have shown modest or inconsistent efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Two randomized Phase 2 trials of ustekinumab in atopic dermatitis exist, but their sample sizes are small and the published efficacy is modest or inconsistent. The mechanism only partially fits the Th2-driven disease. In addition, all EU authorizations are currently cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, and the mechanism of action data
- A clear definition of the target dermatitis subtype (atopic vs contact), since the mechanistic rationale differs
- Quantitative review of the efficacy results from NCT01945086, NCT01806662 and the related publications
- Clarification of the EU market status and which authorization would support any new development
