## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the structured record. Based on known information from the supporting literature, Ustekinumab is a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds the shared p40 subunit of interleukin-12 and interleukin-23, thereby suppressing Th1, Th17, and Th22 pathway activation. It is a well-established biologic for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease, and ulcerative colitis.

Atopic dermatitis and psoriasis are both chronic, immune-mediated inflammatory skin diseases with overlapping — though not identical — cytokine pathways. While psoriasis is classically Th17/Th22-dominant, atopic dermatitis has traditionally been considered a Th2-dominant disease; however, more recent immunophenotyping shows meaningful Th17/Th22 activation in atopic dermatitis skin as well, which provides a biological rationale for IL-12/23 blockade.

This mechanistic overlap is directly reflected in the clinical evidence base: two completed Phase 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (including a dedicated Japanese severe atopic dermatitis study) evaluated ustekinumab specifically in atopic dermatitis, and multiple systematic reviews/meta-analyses have since synthesized biologic therapy data — including ustekinumab — in this population. This combination of mechanistic plausibility and early-phase clinical testing is why the TxGNN prediction is considered reasonable rather than speculative.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials plus multiple systematic reviews and a real-world evidence study provide L2-level clinical support for ustekinumab in atopic dermatitis, and the IL-12/23 mechanism has reasonable biological plausibility. However, a Blocking-severity data gap in safety labeling (contraindications, key warnings, DDI) and the absence of confirmed EU marketing authorization data in this pack mean the candidate cannot yet advance to a full safety assessment (S1) without further data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/EU SmPC warnings and contraindications (Blocking gap — required before S1 safety review)
- Confirmed original mechanism of action (MOA) documentation from DrugBank
- Verification of actual EU marketing authorization status, since Ustekinumab is a known marketed biologic and the "Not Marketed / 0 licenses" result likely reflects incomplete data ingestion rather than true absence from the EU market
- Monitoring of ongoing/upcoming trials (NCT05535738, NCT07352566) for maturing Phase 2/3 and Phase 4 evidence in atopic dermatitis
