# Trametinib: From BRAF V600-Mutant Cutaneous Melanoma to Non-Cutaneous Melanoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Trametinib is a MEK1/2 inhibitor established (in combination with the BRAF inhibitor dabrafenib) for the treatment of BRAF V600 mutation-positive cutaneous melanoma. The TxGNN model's evidence pack points most credibly toward **Non-Cutaneous Melanoma** (melanoma arising outside typical skin sites, e.g., mucosal, ocular, acral) as a mechanistically plausible extension of the same BRAF/MEK-driven biology, supported by **50 clinical trials** currently on record, though these trials largely enrolled classic cutaneous BRAF-mutant melanoma populations rather than non-cutaneous subtypes specifically. Note: the model's single highest-scoring prediction (choroideremia, an inherited retinal disorder) is explicitly flagged within the evidence pack itself as a likely knowledge-graph embedding artifact with no mechanistic basis, and is not used as the lead candidate in this report.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action documentation is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on information embedded in the clinical trial records themselves, trametinib is repeatedly described as "a reversible and highly selective allosteric inhibitor of MEK1 and MEK2," used in combination with the BRAF inhibitor dabrafenib to block the MAPK signalling pathway that is constitutively activated by BRAF V600 mutations.

Non-cutaneous melanoma — arising in mucosal, ocular, acral, or other non-skin sites — is histogenetically the same malignancy (malignant transformation of melanocytes) as cutaneous melanoma, and a meaningful proportion of cases, particularly mucosal and acral subtypes, carry the same BRAF V600 mutations or BRAF fusion events that activate the MAPK pathway. The rationale in this evidence pack therefore frames the prediction not as a mechanistic leap, but as an anatomical-site extension of an already-validated molecular target within the same tumor lineage.

Because trametinib/dabrafenib efficacy is driven by BRAF mutation status rather than anatomic origin, the mechanism is plausible across melanoma subtypes; however, most of the 50 supporting trials in this evidence pack enrolled predominantly cutaneous melanoma patients, so trial-level confirmation is indirect rather than subtype-specific. For context, a closely related sub-indication in this same evidence pack — mucosal melanoma — carries direct molecular literature (e.g., BRAF fusion genes shown to activate MAPK signalling and confer MEK-inhibitor sensitivity in mucosal melanoma) and a dedicated retrospective cohort study (NCT06337617) with separate mucosal and non-mucosal analysis arms, reinforcing that the underlying biological hypothesis is credible even though it is not yet confirmed by a prospective trial dedicated to non-cutaneous melanoma as a whole.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale is strong — BRAF/MEK pathway activation is shared across anatomic melanoma subtypes — and is backed by an extensive body of Phase 2/3 clinical evidence (L2), including large completed pivotal trials (COMBI-v, METRIC) for BRAF-mutant melanoma generally. However, these trials predominantly enrolled cutaneous melanoma patients, direct literature support for the "non-cutaneous melanoma" category as a whole is absent, and critical safety/regulatory data for this drug are currently missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/EU product label (SmPC) retrieval to resolve the Blocking-severity safety data gap before any S1 safety evaluation can proceed
- Confirmed mechanism-of-action documentation from DrugBank to formally close the MOA data gap
- Subtype-specific evidence (e.g., BRAF mutation prevalence and treatment outcomes in mucosal, ocular, and acral melanoma populations) to substantiate the "non-cutaneous" grouping beyond mechanistic extrapolation
- Confirmation of current EU marketing authorization status, since this evidence pack records 0 licenses despite trametinib being an established combination therapy in melanoma
