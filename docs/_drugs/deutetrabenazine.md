## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for the original indication is not available in the data compiled for this report. Deutetrabenazine is generally known as a VMAT2 (vesicular monoamine transporter 2) inhibitor. It depletes presynaptic monoamines such as dopamine and reduces involuntary hyperkinetic movements.

Tardive dyskinesia is a hyperkinetic movement disorder, and psychogenic (functional) movement disorders also involve abnormal involuntary movements. A link is plausible, but nothing in the supplied data supports it. The prediction therefore rests on the model alone.

The picture differs for **chronic tic disorder**. Tics are linked to dopaminergic hyperactivity in cortico-striatal-thalamo-cortical circuits, and VMAT2 inhibition is a recognized strategy for tic suppression. The literature includes studies of deutetrabenazine in Tourette syndrome in adults and in children and adolescents. The study designs and results could not be verified from the titles and abstracts supplied.

The other top-10 predictions have no supporting evidence and little mechanistic basis. Examples are tremor-nystagmus-duodenal ulcer syndrome, polycystic kidney disease, acute intermittent porphyria and Hirschsprung disease. Several look like graph-proximity artifacts.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction check returned 100 interacting drugs, of which 20 were reviewed. Major-level interactions include **bupropion**, **cisapride** and **dolasetron**. Moderate-level interactions include clarithromycin, levofloxacin, morphine, granisetron, famotidine, loperamide and dronabinol. Several laxatives and bowel-preparation agents are also flagged as moderate, including bisacodyl, lactulose, lactitol, magnesium citrate, magnesium hydroxide, picosulfuric acid, castor oil, glycerin and polyethylene glycol.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The top prediction, psychogenic movement disorders, is supported only by the model score (L5), with no trials or publications.
- Chronic tic disorder has a body of Tourette syndrome studies (conservatively L2), but the study designs and outcomes are unverified. The meta-analysis also notes mixed results. The EU authorization is cancelled, and the mechanism-of-action and label safety data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full-text review of the Tourette syndrome studies to confirm phase, design and efficacy outcomes, which could support an upgrade of the evidence level
- Registered clinical trial data for the predicted indications
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data (MOA)
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- Clarification of why the EU authorization was cancelled, and of the regulatory pathway for any new indication
- A review of the major interactions (bupropion, cisapride, dolasetron) for the target patient groups
