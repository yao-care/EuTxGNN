# Niraparib: From Ovarian Cancer to Cystic Neoplasm (Gynecologic Serous Carcinoma)

## One-Sentence Summary

Niraparib (Zejula) was originally authorized for maintenance treatment of advanced and platinum-sensitive relapsed high-grade ovarian, fallopian tube and primary peritoneal cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Cystic Neoplasm**, which in the data compiled for this report appears to map to gynecologic serous carcinomas such as endometrial serous carcinoma.
This direction is currently supported by **3 registered clinical trials** (only 1 active and recruiting) and **10 publications**, with no completed efficacy results yet.

**Note on prediction selection:** The top-ranked prediction (epiglottis neoplasm) has no trials or literature at all (evidence level L5). This report therefore focuses on the second-ranked prediction, cystic neoplasm, which is the highest-ranked prediction with actual supporting evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, niraparib is a PARP1/2 inhibitor. It induces synthetic lethality in tumors with homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) or BRCA mutations, which is the basis of its approved ovarian cancer maintenance use.

The "cystic neoplasm" label appears to be mapped to gynecologic serous carcinomas. The ovarian evidence largely restates the original indication and is not true repurposing. The genuinely new angle is **endometrial (uterine) serous carcinoma**, which shares molecular features with high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma, including chromosomal instability, copy-number alterations and somatic mutations. Its treatment already borrows from the ovarian carcinoma model, so the same PARP-inhibition logic may plausibly apply. This link currently rests on a Phase 2 trial and a published Phase 2 protocol, without efficacy results.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (treat as a research question)**

**Rationale:**
- The only trial directly addressing a new indication (NCT04716686, endometrial serous carcinoma) is still recruiting with no results, the other two trials were terminated or withdrawn, and the ovarian literature mostly restates the approved use. The EU authorization is also cancelled, and the safety review cannot proceed because label warnings and contraindications are missing.
- The other top-ranked predictions (epiglottis neoplasm, benign neoplasms of the hypopharynx, tongue and floor of mouth, cervical neuroblastoma, testicular tumor, pre-malignant neoplasm, inner ear neoplasm, jugular foramen schwannoma) have no trials or literature and remain at L5. For benign or pre-malignant conditions, the risk-benefit balance of a DNA-repair-targeting anticancer agent is unfavorable.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from NCT04716686 (Phase 2, endometrial serous carcinoma) or similar completed trials
- EU SmPC warnings and contraindications (blocking data gap), and clarification of why the EU authorization was cancelled
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- HRD/BRCA biomarker stratification data for endometrial serous carcinoma
- A review of hematologic and bleeding risk with the major and moderate interacting drugs
