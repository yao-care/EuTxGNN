# Ledipasvir: From Chronic Hepatitis C to Hepatitis B Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Ledipasvir is an HCV NS5A inhibitor, used in the Harvoni combination (with sofosbuvir) to treat chronic hepatitis C.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**,
with **5 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction. Most of this evidence concerns HCV treatment in HBV co-infected patients, and only a small Phase 2 study tests HBV mono-infection directly.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ledipasvir is part of a combination with sofosbuvir (Harvoni). Its efficacy in chronic hepatitis C is established, and it may be applicable to hepatitis B through indirect effects.

The mechanistic link is weak. HBV has no NS5A homolog, so direct anti-HBV activity of ledipasvir is unclear. A Phase 2 study of ledipasvir/sofosbuvir in HBV-infected subjects (published as PMID 36045503) followed earlier observations that HBsAg fell modestly in patients co-infected with HBV and HCV. Any effect seen may be modest or indirect, for example HCV clearance relieving viral interference. Sofosbuvir, the combination partner, may also contribute to any observed effect, so it cannot be attributed to ledipasvir alone.

Safety is a further concern. HBV reactivation during HCV direct-acting antiviral therapy is a documented signal (PMID 29334502, PMID 33523503).

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 65 interactions were identified. The ones listed here are mostly moderate and mainly involve agents that raise gastric pH or contain polyvalent cations, which can reduce ledipasvir absorption:
  - Antacids: aluminum hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magaldrate, magnesium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium oxide
  - Acid suppressants (H2 blockers and proton pump inhibitors): cimetidine, famotidine, nizatidine, omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole
  - Other moderate interactions: eliglustat, linagliptin, loperamide, naldemedine
  - Minor interactions: fidaxomicin, naloxegol
- **HBV reactivation**: Published cohort studies show HBV reactivation can occur during HCV direct-acting antiviral therapy in co-infected patients. This is a key consideration for any HBV-related use.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There is a completed Phase 2 study in HBV-infected subjects (n=21), but it is small and uncontrolled. The mechanism is unclear, and sofosbuvir may account for any observed effect. HBV reactivation is a documented safety signal. The only EU authorization has been cancelled, which limits near-term regulatory options. The prediction is best treated as a research question for now.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed results of the Phase 2 study (NCT03312023 / PMID 36045503), including HBsAg and HBV DNA changes
- Mechanism of action data and studies separating ledipasvir's contribution from sofosbuvir's
- Larger controlled studies in HBV-infected patients
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, and a monitoring plan for HBV reactivation
- Clarification of the EU regulatory status, since the Harvoni authorization is cancelled
