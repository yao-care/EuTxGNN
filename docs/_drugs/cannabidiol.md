# Cannabidiol: From Seizures in Lennox-Gastaut and Dravet Syndromes to Restless Legs Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Cannabidiol (CBD) was originally authorised in the EU as add-on therapy for seizures associated with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (LGS) and Dravet syndrome (DS).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **restless legs syndrome (RLS)**,
with **1 registered clinical trial (not yet recruiting)** and **10 publications** currently touching on this direction. Most of these are reviews or small studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, cannabidiol is a plant-derived cannabinoid, its efficacy in seizures of LGS and Dravet syndrome has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to restless legs syndrome.

Cannabinoids act on the endocannabinoid system, which can influence sleep-wake regulation, dopaminergic signalling and sensory or pain pathways. These are plausibly relevant to RLS symptoms. Cannabinoids are also thought to inhibit glutamate release in the striatum. Antiseizure activity and RLS are both linked to neuronal excitability, which gives a loose conceptual connection between the original and the new indication.

The clinical evidence is limited. It consists mainly of small studies, reports and reviews, and no completed randomised controlled trial confirms the benefit. The study designs could not be verified from the available titles and abstracts, so the evidence grade is provisional.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The RLS prediction has a high model score, but the clinical support is limited to small or exploratory studies and reviews. The only registered trial has not started, and the EU authorisation is cancelled. Safety documentation is also incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from the planned Phase 2 trial (NCT07224932) or another completed randomised controlled trial in RLS
- Verification of the design and outcomes of the RLS studies (PMID 35749710 and 42387200)
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- Review of the interaction profile for patients likely to have RLS, including those using opioids, sedatives or other CNS-active drugs
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorisation status
