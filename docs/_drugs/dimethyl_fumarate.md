# Dimethyl Fumarate: From Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis to Progressive Multiple Sclerosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Dimethyl fumarate (DMF) was originally authorised in the EU for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS) and for moderate to severe plaque psoriasis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **progressive multiple sclerosis**,
with **3 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently related to this direction. The only completed placebo-controlled trial in primary progressive MS did not meet its primary endpoint.

*Note on evidence level:* The compiled data assigns L1, but none of the progressive-MS trials listed below is a completed Phase 3 RCT. The only Phase 3 study was terminated early with 58 participants, and the completed Phase 2 trial was negative on its primary endpoint. By the stated rules the evidence is closer to L2 at best, and efficacy in progressive disease remains unproven.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on the known pharmacology summarised there, DMF and its active metabolite monomethyl fumarate activate the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway and modulate immune cells (a shift from Th1/Th17 toward Th2, and fewer activated lymphocytes).

Progressive MS has both inflammatory and neurodegenerative components. Because DMF acts on immune modulation and on oxidative-stress protection, it could plausibly address both. The drug is already used in RRMS, so its safety profile in MS patients is well characterised.

However, efficacy specifically in progressive disease is unproven. Existing evidence is small and mostly negative or inconclusive, so the prediction should be seen as a research question rather than a confirmed benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 entries, all of the ones shown being moderate-level. The main groups are:
  - Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisone, prednisolone, methylprednisolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone)
  - Immunosuppressive or cytotoxic agents (mercaptopurine, trimetrexate, levamisole, dexrazoxane)
  - Radiolabelled antibody agents (ibritumomab tiuxetan, tositumomab, iobenguane I-131)
  - Others, including naltrexone, roflumilast and dupilumab
- **Safety signals from the literature**: DMF can cause sustained moderate to severe lymphopenia, which is associated with progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML). The reported PML incidence in DMF-treated patients is low (about 0.02 per 1000 patients).

Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only completed placebo-controlled trial in primary progressive MS did not meet its primary endpoint, and the Phase 3 trial in secondary progressive MS was terminated early with just 58 participants. A plausible mechanism and a well-known safety profile in RRMS are not enough to support moving forward. In addition, all EU authorisations are currently cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of why NCT02430532 was terminated, and retrieval of the results of NCT02959658
- Adequately powered randomised trials in progressive MS with clinically meaningful disability endpoints
- Detailed mechanism of action data and a full review of the SmPC warnings and contraindications
- Clarification of the EU regulatory status, since all licences are cancelled
- A lymphopenia and PML monitoring plan for progressive MS populations
