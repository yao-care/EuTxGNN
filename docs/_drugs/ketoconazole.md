## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on what is known, ketoconazole is an azole antifungal that inhibits CYP17A1 and steroid synthesis, giving it antiandrogenic activity. It is also active against Malassezia (Pityrosporum) yeasts, which can cause acne-like follicular eruptions.

Androgen-driven sebum production is a recognised contributor to acne, and Malassezia folliculitis is often mistaken for acne vulgaris. These two features offer a plausible mechanistic link. Laboratory work also reports that ketoconazole inhibits growth and lipase activity of *Propionibacterium acnes*, the bacterium associated with acne.

These links are indirect. The only trial is a small topical cream study, and the systemic oral form is not what has been tested there. Oral ketoconazole carries serious safety concerns, so the route and formulation matter greatly for any acne use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only study directly on acne is a small, non-phased, still-active topical trial with no results. The remaining support is indirect (laboratory work, Malassezia folliculitis, PCOS-related hyperandrogenism), so the evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from NCT07237763 and larger controlled trials in acne
- Clarification of the intended route (topical versus oral), given the safety profile of systemic ketoconazole
- Mechanism of action data and SmPC warnings and contraindications
- A benefit–risk assessment against established acne therapies
