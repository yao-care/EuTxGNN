## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, thyrotropin alfa is a recombinant form of human thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH). It acts on TSH receptors in thyroid tissue to stimulate iodine uptake and thyroglobulin release. Its use in thyroid cancer follow-up and ablation is established.

For migraine disorder, no plausible mechanistic link has been documented. The score of 99.98% is a graph-based prediction only. The related migraine entities that also appear in the prediction list (migraine with brainstem aura, migraine susceptibility) likely share graph neighbours, so they should not be treated as independent supporting signals. The relationship between the original and new indication is therefore unconfirmed, and this prediction should be treated as a hypothesis only.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 45 interactions were identified in total. The 20 listed in the compiled data are all of moderate severity and mainly involve antidiabetic drugs, including:
  - Oral agents: acarbose, metformin, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, chlorpropamide, acetohexamide
  - DPP-4 and SGLT2 inhibitors: alogliptin, saxagliptin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin
  - GLP-1 agonists: albiglutide, dulaglutide, exenatide, semaglutide
  - Insulins: human insulin and insulin aspart formulations
- **Concern for other predicted indications**: For hyperthyroidism (also on the prediction list), thyrotropin alfa stimulates thyroid hormone production and would be expected to worsen the condition. That prediction is likely a false positive and is a safety concern, not an opportunity. In postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, a transient rise in thyroid hormones could aggravate tachycardia.

Please refer to the SmPC for other safety information (warnings and contraindications).
