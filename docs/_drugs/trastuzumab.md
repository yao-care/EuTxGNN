# Trastuzumab: From HER2-Positive Breast Cancer to Progesterone-Receptor Positive Breast Cancer

## One-Sentence Summary

> Trastuzumab is a HER2-targeted monoclonal antibody originally developed and approved for HER2-positive breast cancer (and later HER2-positive metastatic gastric cancer).
> The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **progesterone-receptor (PR) positive breast cancer**,
> with **34 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently identified in this evidence pack supporting this direction — most of which describe the HR+/HER2+ ("triple-positive") molecular subgroup rather than a biologically new disease target.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Structured mechanism-of-action data was flagged as a data gap in this evidence pack (DG002, High severity). However, the underlying rationale captured alongside the prediction confirms the well-established pharmacology: trastuzumab is a humanized anti-HER2/ERBB2 monoclonal antibody that blocks HER2 dimerization signaling and mediates tumor cell killing through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). This mechanism is the basis of its established use in HER2-overexpressing breast cancer.

Progesterone-receptor positive breast cancer frequently co-occurs with HER2 overexpression — this is the well-recognized "HR+/HER2+" (or "triple-positive" when ER is also positive) molecular subtype, which represents roughly 5–10% of all breast cancers. Importantly, this predicted indication is best understood as a **molecular subgroup within the already-approved HER2-positive breast cancer indication**, rather than an entirely novel disease category. PR positivity itself does not change the treatment decision — HER2 status (IHC3+ or FISH-amplified) remains the determining biomarker for anti-HER2 therapy eligibility, while PR status informs adjunctive endocrine therapy sequencing.

Mechanistically, this makes the prediction biologically coherent but clinically incremental: the evidence largely reflects combination strategies (trastuzumab plus aromatase inhibitors, fulvestrant, or CDK4/6 inhibitors) in patients who are already candidates for HER2-directed therapy under existing labeling, with PR status used to guide combination partner selection rather than trastuzumab eligibility itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials (e.g., NCT01275677, NCT04629846) and Phase 2/3 RCTs (e.g., monarcHER, WSG-TP-II, ExteNET) support the efficacy of trastuzumab in HR+/HER2+ breast cancer, meeting the L1 evidence bar. However, this predicted indication represents a molecular subgroup within the already-approved HER2-positive breast cancer indication rather than a wholly new disease target — PR positivity itself does not change eligibility, which remains defined by HER2 IHC3+/FISH-amplified status. Guardrails are needed to ensure patient selection is not driven by PR status alone.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Resolve Blocking data gap DG001: obtain official product label warnings/contraindications (SmPC) for safety review (S1 gate)
- Resolve High-severity data gap DG002: obtain structured MOA and DrugBank classification data
- Confirm current EU marketing authorization status, since this evidence pack shows 0 active licenses / "Not Marketed"
- Clinical clarification on whether PR-positive status meaningfully changes treatment sequencing (e.g., endocrine partner selection) beyond the existing HER2-positive label
