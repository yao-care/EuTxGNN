# Tenofovir Disoproxil: From HIV-1/Hepatitis B Infection to Feline Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Tenofovir disoproxil is a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor best known as a core component of antiretroviral therapy for HIV-1 infection and as a treatment for chronic hepatitis B virus infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective against **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome** (the AIDS-like disease caused by Feline Immunodeficiency Virus, FIV, in domestic cats), with **4 clinical trials** and **2 publications** currently linked to this candidate.
However, the linked clinical trials are all human HIV-1 studies rather than feline-specific trials, so the actual disease-specific evidence base is limited to two small preclinical animal studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for this candidate is flagged as a data gap in the Evidence Pack (DG002). Based on well-established pharmacological knowledge, tenofovir disoproxil is a prodrug of tenofovir, a nucleotide analogue reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NtRTI). It is administered as part of combination antiretroviral therapy for human HIV-1 infection and is separately approved for chronic hepatitis B virus infection — both indications rely on inhibition of viral reverse transcriptase.

Feline Immunodeficiency Virus (FIV), the cause of feline AIDS, is a lentivirus in the same genus as HIV-1, and it depends on the same reverse-transcriptase-mediated replication mechanism. This shared virology gives the TxGNN prediction a plausible mechanistic basis: a reverse transcriptase inhibitor effective against HIV-1 could, in principle, also suppress FIV replication. This is supported by the Evidence Pack itself — tenofovir already has a validated antiviral track record against HBV (see rank 6, "hepatitis B virus infection", with dozens of completed Phase 3 trials), reinforcing that the drug's core mechanism generalizes across unrelated viral families that share reverse-transcriptase or polymerase machinery.

That said, this is a **veterinary indication in a non-human species**, which falls outside the conventional human drug-repurposing pathway. The mechanistic plausibility does not by itself establish clinical translatability to a regulatory human-indication pipeline, and the specific literature evidence comes from small experimental cat studies rather than controlled clinical trials.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication is a veterinary disease (feline AIDS/FIV infection), which falls outside the standard human drug-repurposing evaluation scope, and the supporting evidence is limited to two small preclinical animal studies with no controlled clinical trials in the target species or population. While the underlying lentivirus-to-lentivirus mechanistic rationale is biologically plausible, it is not sufficient on its own to support progression given the species mismatch and lack of EU marketing status.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm whether veterinary indications are in scope for this repurposing pipeline; if not, this candidate should be deprioritized in favor of the model's human-disease predictions
- Mechanism of action data (DG002) to properly assess cross-species applicability
- TFDA/EMA labeling data on warnings and contraindications (DG001) before any safety pre-assessment (S1) can proceed
- If veterinary development is pursued, dedicated feline pharmacokinetic and controlled efficacy/safety trials, given tenofovir's known nephrotoxicity risk profile in other species
