# Tenofovir Disoproxil: From HIV-1 Infection and Chronic Hepatitis B to Feline Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Tenofovir disoproxil is a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor, originally used to treat HIV-1 infection and chronic hepatitis B.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (FIV infection in cats)**,
but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **1 publication**, a veterinary animal study. This is a veterinary indication outside the scope of human repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Tenofovir disoproxil is known to be a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI). After conversion to its active form, it blocks the viral enzyme that copies viral RNA into DNA. Its efficacy in HIV-1 and hepatitis B is established.

Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is a lentivirus, closely related to HIV, and it also depends on a reverse transcriptase. A drug that blocks this enzyme in HIV could therefore plausibly act on FIV as well. This is the only mechanistic link available.

The sole supporting study is a combination antiretroviral study in cats. Its relevance to human medicine is limited, and the indication is veterinary.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a single veterinary combination-therapy study with no registered clinical trials. The indication is veterinary and outside human repurposing. The drug also currently has no valid EU authorizations.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of whether a veterinary development path is in scope, since otherwise this prediction should be deprioritized
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Safety information from the SmPC (warnings, contraindications, drug interactions)
- Verification of current regional approvals and available formulations, given that all EU licenses are cancelled
- Review of the other predictions in the data, such as hepatitis B virus infection. That one has Phase 3 RCT support, but it is already a labeled indication and so confirms an existing use rather than a new one.
