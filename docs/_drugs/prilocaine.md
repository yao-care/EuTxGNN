## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, prilocaine is an amide-type local anesthetic. Drugs in this class generally block voltage-gated sodium channels in nerve fibres, which reduces the transmission of pain and sensory signals. Its authorized use in premature ejaculation relies on reducing sensitivity of the treated area.

**Papillary conjunctivitis (rank 1).** The model score is very high (0.9978), but no clinical trials or publications were found. Relief of ocular surface discomfort through sodium-channel blockade is conceivable, but it is speculative. Ocular use of a product developed for topical application to the penis would also raise formulation and safety questions.

**Neuralgia (rank 5).** This is the most plausible and best-documented direction. Sodium-channel blockade in nociceptive afferents gives a reasonable mechanism for relieving neuropathic pain, particularly postherpetic neuralgia (PHN). The existing evidence is small and dated, and it relates mainly to the lidocaine/prilocaine combination. Any effect would be symptomatic and topical.

Several other predictions have no plausible mechanistic link or only indirect evidence. These include manic bipolar affective disorder (likely a TxGNN model (deep learning) artifact), bronchitis, migraine, rhinitis, allergic asthma and rosacea conjunctivitis. For atopic eczema, the literature points to safety risks, not treatment benefit.

## Safety Considerations

Package-insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC for safety information.

- **Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph** (derived from knowledge-graph relationships, not label text): hypertension and other cardiovascular disease, myocardial infarction and coronary artery disease, heart conduction disease and atrioventricular block, epilepsy, kidney disease, liver disease, diabetes and diabetic ketoacidosis, respiratory failure, angle-closure glaucoma, myasthenia gravis and neuromuscular junction disease, butyrylcholinesterase deficiency, hemoglobinopathy, and hyperthyroidism and Graves disease.
- **Drug interactions:** 22 interactions were recorded, 20 of them listed in detail. Most are rated Major. Examples include:
  - Methaemoglobinaemia-related agents: dapsone (including topical), primaquine, chloroquine, quinine, tafenoquine, rasburicase, sulfasalazine, amyl nitrite, nitric oxide.
  - Other local anesthetics: lidocaine (including topical), benzocaine (topical), cinchocaine (topical).
  - Others: metoclopramide, silver nitrate (topical), silver sulfadiazine (topical).
  - Moderate: liposomal morphine. Minor: hyaluronidase.
- **Literature safety signals:** In barrier-compromised skin such as atopic dermatitis, absorption increases. Reported events include methemoglobinemia, seizures in a child, contact allergy and purpura after EMLA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (papillary conjunctivitis, and all other predictions except neuralgia). Neuralgia is a research question and not yet ready for a Go.

**Rationale:**
- The top prediction, papillary conjunctivitis, is supported only by a model score, with no trials or literature (L5). Neuralgia is the only direction with a plausible mechanism and clinical reports (L3). That evidence is small, dated, mainly for the lidocaine/prilocaine combination, and symptomatic. The EU authorization is also cancelled, and the safety review is incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications (the missing package-insert data).
- Mechanism of action data for prilocaine.
- A controlled study of prilocaine, or of lidocaine/prilocaine, in neuralgia such as PHN, with a defined route and formulation.
- A route and formulation compatibility assessment, since this has not been completed for any predicted indication.
- A safety plan covering methemoglobinemia risk, the interactions above, and use on compromised skin.
- Clarification of why the Fortacin authorization was cancelled, and whether an alternative authorized topical anesthetic is available.
