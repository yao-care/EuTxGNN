## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism-of-action data for Prilocaine is not available in the evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on the available evidence, Prilocaine is an amide-type local anesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, most commonly used in a 1:1 eutectic mixture with lidocaine (EMLA cream) for topical/regional anesthesia prior to minor procedures.

Neuralgia — particularly postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) — is pathophysiologically driven by abnormal ectopic firing of damaged peripheral nerve fibers. Sodium-channel blockade directly suppresses this aberrant firing, giving a clear mechanistic bridge from Prilocaine's known anesthetic action to a neuropathic-pain indication. This is not a purely theoretical link: lidocaine-prilocaine cream (EMLA) already has a decades-long clinical history of off-label use in PHN, documented across multiple cohort studies, case reports, and at least one completed Phase 2 trial.

The main caveat is that most existing evidence tests the lidocaine-prilocaine combination or prilocaine as an adjunct/pretreatment (e.g., before capsaicin patch application), rather than prilocaine as a standalone therapeutic agent for neuralgia. Component-specific efficacy data for prilocaine alone is therefore limited.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Neuralgia (particularly PHN) is supported by L2 evidence — a completed Phase 2 trial and multiple cohort/RCT publications — showing lidocaine-prilocaine combination cream provides meaningful pain relief, with a clear sodium-channel-blockade mechanism. However, prilocaine-specific (non-combination) efficacy data is limited, and safety/label data is entirely missing.
