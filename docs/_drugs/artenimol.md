## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, artenimol is an artemisinin derivative, its efficacy in uncomplicated *P. falciparum* malaria has been established, and mechanistically it may have some applicability to proliferative or inflammatory conditions. This remains speculative.

Preclinical cancer and inflammatory models have reported that artenimol has anti-proliferative, pro-apoptotic and NF-κB/mTOR-modulating effects. These could loosely relate to mast cell proliferation. However, the data compiled for this report do not support a direct link to KIT-driven mastocytosis. The relationship between malaria and mastocytosis is weak, and the prediction likely reflects patterns in the knowledge graph rather than independent evidence.

Two other mastocytosis predictions (lymphoadenopathic mastocytosis with eosinophilia, and systemic mastocytosis) score similarly (98.4% and 98.3%). They are highly correlated and probably reflect one underlying signal rather than three independent findings. Established KIT-targeted therapies also set a high bar for any repurposing proposal.

The only predicted indication with any supporting publication is *Plasmodium vivax* malaria (rank 4). That publication is [PMID 42419579](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42419579/) (2026, *Travel Medicine and Infectious Disease*), a case report and review of imported *P. knowlesi* malaria in Spain. The patient recovered after three days of artemisinin-based combination therapy with piperaquine and artenimol. It involves a different species and does not directly evaluate artenimol in *P. vivax*, so the evidence is indirect. The biological rationale for this indication is nonetheless much stronger than for the others, since artenimol is active against *Plasmodium* species.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for smouldering systemic mastocytosis is based only on a computational score (L5), with no trials, no publications and no established mechanistic link. The one marketing authorization is cancelled, and the drug's mechanism of action and safety label data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (e.g., from DrugBank)
- EU label warnings and contraindications (from the EMA product information)
- Preclinical evidence testing artenimol in mast cell or KIT-driven models
- Clarification of the cancelled authorization status and the feasibility of any future formulation or route
