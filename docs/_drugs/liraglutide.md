# Liraglutide: From Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (Insulin-Adjunct Therapy)

> **Note on indication selection**: This Evidence Pack contains 10 TxGNN-predicted indications for Liraglutide. Nine of them (opsismodysplasia, thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, stiff person/stiff limb syndrome, autoimmune oophoritis, and four lipodystrophy variants) are rank-1–5 and rank-7–10 candidates scored purely on knowledge-graph proximity, carry **zero** clinical trial or literature support, and are explicitly flagged in their own rationale as lacking mechanistic basis (Evidence Level L5, decision stage S0, recommendation Hold). Only **Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus** (rank 6) is backed by substantive clinical and literature evidence. This report focuses on that candidate as the only one currently actionable for evaluation.

## One-Sentence Summary

Liraglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist originally developed and marketed for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus. The TxGNN model additionally predicts potential benefit as an **adjunct-to-insulin therapy in Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus**, and this direction is already supported by **50 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, including the completed Phase 3 ADJUNCT ONE/TWO program.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this Evidence Pack (MOA: [Data Gap]). Based on known pharmacology, liraglutide is a long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist that glucose-dependently stimulates insulin secretion, suppresses glucagon secretion, and delays gastric emptying — mechanisms established through its approved use in Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.

In Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus, pancreatic β-cells are largely destroyed by autoimmune attack and endogenous insulin secretion is minimal, but residual β-cell function and, more importantly, α-cell (glucagon) regulation may still respond to GLP-1 signaling. This provides a plausible rationale for liraglutide as an **adjunct to insulin** rather than a replacement — aiming to reduce insulin dose, improve glycemic variability, and support weight control, not to restore endogenous insulin production.

This mechanistic plausibility is corroborated by an unusually large and mature evidence base: the completed Phase 3 ADJUNCT ONE and ADJUNCT TWO trials (multinational, insulin-capped, placebo-controlled) directly tested this exact adjunct-therapy hypothesis in Type 1 Diabetes patients, along with multiple smaller Phase 3/4 trials and over a decade of published literature, including systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 RCTs (including the ADJUNCT ONE/TWO program) and a decade of consistent literature support liraglutide as adjunct-to-insulin therapy in Type 1 Diabetes, giving this candidate the highest evidence tier (L1) among all TxGNN predictions for this drug. However, this is an off-label adjunct use in a chronic autoimmune condition, and mandatory safety documentation is currently missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA-equivalent label warnings and contraindications (Blocking gap, DG001)
- Confirmed mechanism-of-action documentation from DrugBank (High priority gap, DG002)
- Drug-drug interaction profile (currently not_found)
- Confirmation of regulatory/marketing status, since current data shows "Not Marketed" with 0 authorizations
- Clarification of dosing/route requirements specific to the Type 1 Diabetes adjunct-therapy population (pediatric vs. adult, insulin-pump users, etc.)

The remaining 9 predicted indications in this pack (opsismodysplasia, thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, stiff person/limb syndrome, autoimmune oophoritis, and the lipodystrophy variants) should remain at **Hold** — they have no clinical or literature evidence and no established mechanistic link, per their own rationale notes.
