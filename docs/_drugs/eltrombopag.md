## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, eltrombopag is a thrombopoietin receptor agonist that raises platelet counts by stimulating megakaryopoiesis (platelet-producing cell growth).

In people living with HIV, low platelet counts can occur as HIV-related immune thrombocytopenia or immune reconstitution thrombocytopenia. The same mechanism that works in its licensed settings could, in principle, help here. The benefit would be supportive, treating the low platelet count rather than the virus.

One in vitro screen (PMID 32977702) reported that eltrombopag may modulate HIV-1 proviral transcription. This is hypothesis-generating only and has not been confirmed in clinical studies.

Note on the evidence level: none of the cited studies directly tests eltrombopag as a treatment for HIV infection itself. They concern thrombocytopenia in people with HIV, hepatitis C or chronic liver disease, so the level is capped at L5 (model prediction without a direct study of the predicted disease).

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in the interaction screen. Most are moderate and involve polyvalent cation products such as calcium, magnesium, aluminium and iron preparations, antacids and kaolin/attapulgite. One major interaction was flagged with eluxadoline, and a moderate one with glyburide.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high TxGNN score (0.9926) is not matched by direct evidence. The only trial concerns thrombocytopenia in chronic liver disease and was terminated, and the HIV-related reports are case reports or small series about thrombocytopenia, not HIV infection itself.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled studies of eltrombopag in HIV-associated thrombocytopenia, with clearly defined populations
- Confirmation of the antiviral or immunomodulatory signal beyond the in vitro screen
- Mechanism of action data and the SmPC warnings and contraindications
- Review of interactions with antiretroviral therapy, and of liver safety in people with HIV co-infections
