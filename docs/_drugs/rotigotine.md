## Rotigotine: From Parkinson's Disease to Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Rotigotine is a dopamine agonist originally authorised in the EU for Parkinson's disease and moderate to severe restless-legs syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**, but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **3 indirect publications** (mostly about restless-legs syndrome and receptor pharmacology) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Rotigotine is a non-ergolinic dopamine agonist. It acts on dopamine receptors D1 to D5, with highest affinity at D3, D2 and D1, and has additional activity at 5-HT1A and alpha2B adrenoceptors. Its mechanism of action data are otherwise incomplete in the data compiled for this report.

Dopaminergic and noradrenergic dysregulation is central to ADHD. A study of dopamine D4 receptor and alpha2A adrenoceptor heteromers supports biological plausibility at the receptor level. Pediatric restless-legs syndrome, which rotigotine treats in adults, is also frequently comorbid with ADHD.

However, the retrieved literature is mostly about restless-legs syndrome and receptor pharmacology, not about rotigotine treating ADHD. The very high TxGNN score reflects a network-based prediction and is not clinical evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The ADHD prediction rests on a network-based score and indirect receptor-level and restless-legs literature. There are no clinical trials and no rotigotine-specific ADHD data. Rotigotine's EU authorisation is also cancelled in the data compiled for this report.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Rotigotine-specific preclinical or clinical studies in ADHD
- Complete mechanism of action data
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, which are required before safety screening
- Confirmation of the current EU authorisation status and available formulations (transdermal use in children has not been addressed)
- Other predicted indications are weaker: schizophrenia has indirect class-level evidence only and carries a risk of worsening psychosis, and the remaining predictions (ranks 3-10) have no trial or literature support.
