# Erenumab: From Migraine Prophylaxis to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Erenumab is a monoclonal antibody against the CGRP receptor, originally used to prevent migraine in adults with at least 4 migraine days per month.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, none of which specifically studied patients with brainstem aura.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Pharmacology data show that erenumab binds the CGRP receptor. In cell-based assays it abolishes CGRP-stimulated cAMP production (IC50 about 2.3 nM) and is about 5000-fold selective for the CGRP receptor over other human calcitonin family receptors.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a migraine subtype that shares the CGRP-mediated trigeminovascular pathophysiology with the already-approved indication, so biological plausibility is strong. The retrieved literature covers erenumab in migraine with and without aura. This includes a secondary analysis of randomized trials in patients with aura, a vascular safety evaluation, and the REFORM study on aura frequency.

However, none of these studies specifically enrolled or analyzed patients with brainstem aura (formerly basilar-type migraine). The evidence is therefore indirect, extrapolated from broader migraine populations. The original indication is already migraine prophylaxis, so this is closer to a refinement of the indication subtype than true repurposing.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Only a pharmacology entry was found, indicating that erenumab acts on the CGRP receptor (its target). No drug–drug interactions with a graded severity were identified.
- **Vascular safety**: The literature includes vascular risk analyses in patients with and without aura. Vascular safety in aura patients, and in subtypes historically associated with brainstem symptoms, warrants attention.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic plausibility is strong, but there are no clinical trials and no studies specifically in brainstem aura, so the evidence is indirect (L4). In addition, the only EU authorization is cancelled, and the label safety information could not be retrieved.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Label warnings and contraindications from the EMA product information, which are required before safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Clinical data or a registry-based analysis specifically in patients with brainstem aura
- A vascular safety assessment for this subtype
- Clarification of the reason for the cancelled EU authorization and the current regulatory status
