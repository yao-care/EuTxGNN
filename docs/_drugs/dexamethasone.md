## Dexamethasone: From Retinal Vein Occlusion Macular Oedema and Multiple Myeloma to Alopecia Areata
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Dexamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid, originally authorised in the EU for macular oedema after retinal vein occlusion, non-infectious posterior uveitis, diabetic macular oedema (Ozurdex) and multiple myeloma (Neofordex).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**, with **no registered clinical trials** but **about 20 publications** retrieved, including one randomised controlled trial in children and several cohort studies.
Evidence is mostly small and heterogeneous, so the prediction is plausible but not yet confirmed by large trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, dexamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid with strong anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive activity. Its efficacy in inflammatory eye conditions (uveitis, macular oedema) and in multiple myeloma is established.

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune, T-cell-mediated attack on hair follicles with breakdown of follicular immune privilege. Glucocorticoids dampen inflammatory cytokines and the immune attack, which provides a mechanistic link to the original anti-inflammatory indications. Oral mini-pulse dexamethasone (for example 5 mg on two consecutive days each week) is an established regimen in clinical practice.

The very high TxGNN score is consistent with the clinical literature. However, JAK inhibitors are stronger comparators in severe disease, and relapse after withdrawal is common.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication signals (knowledge graph, not label text)**: The knowledge graph links dexamethasone to hypertension, congestive heart failure, hyperglycaemia, epilepsy, herpes simplex virus keratitis, measles, urea cycle disorders (carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I deficiency, citrullinemia, hyperargininemia), and a range of pituitary hormone deficiency and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism conditions.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Major-level examples include **fentanyl** and **adalimumab**. Moderate-level examples include paclitaxel, zidovudine, abemaciclib, acalabrutinib, isotretinoin, nifedipine, ibuprofen, ketorolac, acetazolamide, acarbose, acebutolol, codeine and dihydrocodeine. Minor-level examples include dolutegravir, lidocaine and formoterol.
- **Class-related guardrails for systemic use in alopecia areata**: Suppression of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis, metabolic effects, bone loss, and high relapse rates after withdrawal.

Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Oral mini-pulse dexamethasone has a plausible immunological rationale and is supported by one randomised trial, several cohorts and a network meta-analysis in alopecia areata. However, the evidence is small and heterogeneous, there are no registered trials, and JAK inhibitors are stronger options in severe disease.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Larger, adequately powered randomised trials, ideally against JAK inhibitors or standard corticosteroid regimens
- Mechanism of action data and SmPC warnings and contraindications (both currently missing)
- A plan for long-term safety monitoring (HPA axis, glucose, blood pressure, bone health) and for managing relapse after withdrawal
- Clarification of the regulatory position, since both EU authorisations in the data are cancelled and no systemic oral alopecia areata product is listed

**Other predicted indications (all recommended Hold):** The remaining predictions (alopecia mucinosa, telogen effluvium, folliculitis decalvans, alopecia antibody deficiency, rare hereditary hair disorders, alopecia universalis with onychodystrophy and vitiligo, and lacrimal gland prolapse) have no supporting trials or relevant literature, so they are not recommended for further development at this time.
