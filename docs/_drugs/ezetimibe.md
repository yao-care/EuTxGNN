Ezetimibe is a cholesterol absorption inhibitor, used to lower LDL cholesterol in primary hypercholesterolaemia, mixed dyslipidaemia and to reduce cardiovascular risk.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, ezetimibe inhibits NPC1L1-mediated intestinal cholesterol absorption, which lowers LDL-C.

CETP deficiency is a genetic condition in which less cholesteryl ester is transferred from HDL to apoB-containing lipoproteins. This raises HDL-C and tends to lower LDL-C. Both ezetimibe and CETP deficiency act on related lipid pathways, so the model links them.

However, the link is indirect and largely theoretical. CETP deficiency usually does not require LDL-lowering treatment, and a plausible therapeutic rationale for adding ezetimibe has not been established. The high score (0.9909) is a TxGNN model (deep learning) output only. It may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence, and it is not backed by clinical data in the compiled records.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by the model score. No clinical trial or publication directly studies ezetimibe in CETP deficiency (Evidence Level L5), and the therapeutic rationale is weak because CETP deficiency typically does not need LDL-lowering treatment.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarification of a clinical rationale: whether any patients with CETP deficiency have a treatable lipid problem that ezetimibe would address
- Mechanism of action data for ezetimibe
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
