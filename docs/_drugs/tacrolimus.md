# Tacrolimus: From Transplant Rejection Prophylaxis to Seborrheic Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tacrolimus is a calcineurin-inhibitor immunosuppressant, originally used to prevent organ transplant rejection and, as an ointment, to treat atopic dermatitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **seborrheic dermatitis**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, tacrolimus is a calcineurin inhibitor. It blocks NFAT-dependent T-cell activation and the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. This is the basis of its use in transplantation and, topically, in atopic dermatitis.

Seborrheic dermatitis is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin disease of the face and scalp. Its inflammatory component is partly driven by immune responses to *Malassezia* yeast. Topical calcineurin inhibition is therefore mechanistically applicable, in the same way as in atopic dermatitis. Reviews of topical calcineurin inhibitors in seborrheic dermatitis and a maintenance-therapy clinical study with 0.1% tacrolimus ointment support this direction.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related disease associations (knowledge graph, not label text)**: The TxGNN knowledge graph links tacrolimus to contraindication-type edges with hypertension, long QT syndrome and torsades de pointes, liver failure, kidney disease, epilepsy, hyperglycemia and diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, active or latent infections (herpes simplex, herpes zoster, infectious mononucleosis, chronic Epstein-Barr virus infection), cancer and lymphoma (including primary CNS lymphoma), progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, and posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome. These are mostly relevant to systemic use, but the malignancy and infection signals are discussed for topical calcineurin inhibitors as well.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were retrieved. Major-level examples include amphotericin B (including the lipid complex), loperamide, mesalazine, balsalazide and omeprazole. Moderate-level examples include acarbose, famotidine, rabeprazole, systemic corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), metformin, pioglitazone, aprepitant and metronidazole. Interaction relevance is greatest for systemic tacrolimus and is expected to be much lower for topical use with minimal systemic absorption.
- **Malignancy**: A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis (PMID 36370744) examined cancer risk with topical calcineurin inhibitors in atopic dermatitis. Long-term malignancy safety is a standing concern for this class.

Please also refer to the SmPC for the full warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed trials (Phase 3 and Phase 4, 224 patients combined) test tacrolimus ointment directly in facial seborrheic dermatitis. These are supported by a maintenance-therapy clinical study and several reviews, and the mechanism is plausible and matches the approved topical use in atopic dermatitis. However, results from the trials are not provided, the Phase 3 design is unconfirmed, and the only EU topical product (Protopic) is currently cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results and design details (randomization, control arm) of NCT02004860 and NCT01591070
- Detailed mechanism of action data and the full SmPC warnings and contraindications (both missing from the compiled data)
- Clarification of the EU regulatory status of topical tacrolimus products, since all five authorizations are cancelled
- A long-term safety plan covering malignancy, infection and interacting medications, plus a limited-area, short-course use framework for facial skin
