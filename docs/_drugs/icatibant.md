## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is flagged as a data gap in DrugBank for this record. However, the evidence pack's own literature abstracts consistently describe Icatibant as a synthetic decapeptide and selective **bradykinin B2 receptor antagonist** (PMID 24925394, PMID 21284353), used to block the bradykinin-mediated vascular permeability that drives angioedema attacks.

C1 Inhibitor (C1-INH) deficiency causes uncontrolled activation of the kallikrein-kinin system, resulting in excess bradykinin and the swelling attacks characteristic of Hereditary Angioedema. Blocking the bradykinin B2 receptor directly counteracts this downstream mechanism, which is mechanistically coherent regardless of whether C1-INH function is restored.

Importantly, the trial and literature evidence in this pack (e.g., NCT00097695, NCT00912093, PMID 21284353 noting Icatibant "is now authorised in the European Union") indicates that Icatibant is **already an established treatment for HAE due to C1-INH deficiency** in multiple markets. This means the TxGNN "prediction" here largely reflects a true, already-known drug–disease relationship rather than a genuinely novel repurposing candidate. The practical question for this jurisdiction is less "is this mechanistically plausible" and more "why is the drug not yet marketed here, and what local regulatory/safety data is missing."
