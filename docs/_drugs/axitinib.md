## One-Sentence Summary

Axitinib is an oral kinase inhibitor that blocks tumour blood-vessel growth signalling.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **liposarcoma**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **1 preclinical publication** supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Axitinib is generally known as an inhibitor of VEGF and PDGF receptor kinases, which drive tumour angiogenesis.

A preclinical study screened approved targeted drugs and chemotherapeutics against myxoid liposarcoma cell lines. Agents targeting angiogenesis showed antitumour activity, and axitinib showed antiangiogenic and antitumorigenic effects. This is consistent with an antiangiogenic mechanism being relevant in this soft-tissue sarcoma.

This evidence is indirect and limited to laboratory models. It addresses myxoid liposarcoma, one subtype, and no human efficacy data are available. Liposarcoma is also a heterogeneous group of tumours, so the finding may not carry over to other subtypes.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only support is a single preclinical study in one liposarcoma subtype, with no clinical trials and no human efficacy data. The high model score alone is not enough to move forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical data in liposarcoma patients (case series or early-phase trials), ideally by subtype
- Further preclinical confirmation across liposarcoma subtypes
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, and a safety review for this patient population
- Review of the major interactions (clarithromycin, dexamethasone) for use in sarcoma care
