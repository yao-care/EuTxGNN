## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, efmoroctocog alfa is a recombinant factor VIII linked to an Fc fragment. Its efficacy in haemophilia A comes from replacing the missing factor VIII.

Pseudo-von Willebrand disease (platelet-type VWD) is caused by a gain-of-function defect in platelet GPIbα. This increases binding to von Willebrand factor (VWF), leading to loss of high-molecular-weight VWF multimers and thrombocytopenia. Supplying factor VIII does not correct this primary platelet/VWF defect. A secondary reduction in FVIII might occur in severe cases, but that link is speculative.

The high TxGNN score therefore comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related bleeding disorders, not biological evidence of a real mechanistic fit.

The other top-ranked predictions are also weak:
- **Platelet function disorders** (primary release disorder, Glanzmann thrombasthenia, Scott syndrome, collagen receptor defect, constitutional thrombocytopenia): the defect is in platelets, not in factor VIII.
- **Fetal and neonatal alloimmune thrombocytopenia**: this is immune-mediated platelet destruction, and FVIII has no role.
- **Acquired coagulation factor deficiency**: this is the most conceptually plausible, but the category is broad. In acquired haemophilia A, inhibitory autoantibodies make FVIII replacement generally ineffective.
- **Haemophilia A with vascular abnormality**: this is essentially a subset of the approved indication rather than true repurposing.
- **Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura**: FVIII could theoretically raise thrombotic risk, so this prediction should not be acted upon.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a very high model score, but there are no clinical trials or publications, so evidence is at the lowest level (L5). The disease's mechanism (a platelet GPIbα/VWF defect) is not one that FVIII replacement would be expected to correct. The product's only EU authorization is also cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for efmoroctocog alfa
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC, which was not available for this report
- Preclinical or mechanistic studies showing a rationale for FVIII in platelet-type VWD
- Any case reports or clinical literature in pseudo-von Willebrand disease
- Confirmation of current marketing and supply status in the EU
- If any alternative is pursued, review of **acquired coagulation factor deficiency**, restricted to specific subtypes and with attention to inhibitor status, as the most plausible of the ten predictions
