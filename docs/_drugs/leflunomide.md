# Leflunomide: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Plasma Cell Myeloma

## One-Sentence Summary

Leflunomide is a disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD), originally used to treat active rheumatoid arthritis and active psoriatic arthritis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Plasma Cell Myeloma**, with **5 clinical trials** (all early-phase, none with efficacy results yet) and **10 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From the literature, leflunomide's active metabolite (A771726, also called teriflunomide) inhibits dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH), an enzyme needed to make pyrimidines, which are building blocks for DNA and RNA. Fast-growing immune cells, which drive rheumatoid arthritis, depend on this pathway.

Myeloma cells also depend on rapid nucleotide production. Laboratory studies show that DHODH is expressed in myeloma cell lines and primary myeloma cells, and that blocking it reduces growth and induces apoptosis (PMID 19174558). Leflunomide has also been reported to lower c-Myc expression, an important growth driver in myeloma, by targeting PIM kinases (PMID 30940637). A further study found cytotoxicity that does not depend on mitochondria (PMID 34577124). A Phase 1 study in heavily pretreated relapsed/refractory myeloma showed that the drug could be given safely (PMID 32268821).

All of this is preclinical or early-phase. No efficacy results from clinical trials are available yet.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph** (these come from drug-disease links in a knowledge graph, not from label text): liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, chronic hepatitis B and C infection, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia and agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, anemia, interstitial lung disease, peripheral neuropathy, pneumocystosis and aspergillosis. These matter in myeloma, where blood counts, kidney function and infection risk are often already compromised.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found, and the list reviewed here shows many rated Major. Examples include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), acetylsalicylic acid, pioglitazone, bupropion, clarithromycin, minocycline, naltrexone, acarbose and the anabolic steroids oxandrolone and oxymetholone. Dexamethasone is a standard part of myeloma regimens, including the trial combination with pomalidomide, so this interaction deserves particular attention.
- **Tolerability in myeloma**: In the Phase 1 study, no dose-limiting toxicities appeared at 20 and 40 mg. The abstract available here is truncated, so findings at 60 mg cannot be described.

Please refer to the SmPC for the full warnings and contraindications, as these were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale is plausible and the Phase 1 study showed feasibility. However, the Phase 2 trials have no efficacy results, one was withdrawn and another enrolled only 1 patient. In addition, all current EU authorizations are cancelled, and label-based safety data is missing. This is a promising research question rather than a case ready for a decision to advance.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Efficacy results from the Phase 2 trials (NCT04508790, NCT05014646)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- EU SmPC warnings and contraindications, to complete safety screening
- A review of drug interactions and blood-count risks in myeloma patients, especially with dexamethasone
- A review of how the cancelled EU status affects availability and the regulatory pathway
