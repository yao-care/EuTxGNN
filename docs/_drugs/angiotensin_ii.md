## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Pharmacology records show that angiotensin II is a peptide that acts on the AT1 and AT2 receptors (and, in rat data, the K2P3.1 channel). Its efficacy in raising blood pressure in distributive shock is established, and it is used as a vasopressor.

No mechanistic link to the predicted indication is supported by the available data. Angiotensin II acts on AT1 receptors to constrict blood vessels, and no pathway connecting this action to oxidative phosphorylation defects caused by nuclear DNA anomalies is evident. The high model score (92.47%) is therefore a statistical prediction only, with no clinical or literature corroboration.

Of note, the model's other top-ranked predictions are mostly diabetic eye diseases (diabetic retinopathy, diabetic cataract). The literature there consistently describes angiotensin II as a pathogenic driver and supports blocking the pathway (ARBs, ACE inhibitors) rather than giving angiotensin II. This suggests the predictions should not be read as therapeutic proposals.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The data compiled for this report contain no drug-drug interaction entries with severity levels. Three pharmacological target records were returned: the AT1 receptor, the AT2 receptor and the K2P3.1 channel (rat data). These describe targets, not interacting drugs.
- **Class-related consideration (from the literature reviewed)**: In diabetic eye disease, angiotensin II signalling is linked to oxidative stress, inflammation and angiogenesis in the retina. Retinal risk of angiotensin II in diabetic patients is an open safety question rather than an established finding.

For full safety information, including warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.
