# Oxybutynin: From Urge Incontinence to Restless Legs Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Oxybutynin is an antimuscarinic drug originally used to treat urge incontinence and increased urinary frequency and urgency in adults with unstable bladder.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, oxybutynin is an antimuscarinic agent with direct smooth-muscle relaxant activity. Its efficacy in overactive bladder and urge incontinence is established.

The link to restless legs syndrome is speculative. A possible connection is central anticholinergic modulation, but no clinical or literature evidence supports it. Anticholinergic drugs may even worsen restless legs symptoms in some patients. The high TxGNN score (0.997) reflects a graph-based prediction only.

Other predicted indications were also reviewed:
- **Low compliance bladder** (score 97.9%, evidence level L3) is the best-supported prediction. It includes a systematic review of intravesical oxybutynin in children with poorly compliant neurogenic bladder, plus long-term intravesical studies. However, it sits close to the original urological indication, so it is closer to an indication extension than true repurposing.
- **Peptic ulcer disease** (L4) rests only on 1960s clinical evaluations and a 1990 case report of oxybutynin-induced reflux esophagitis.
- **Attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder** (L4) has only indirect links, such as enuresis treatment in affected children.
- Most other predictions have no supporting evidence.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions found; main ones listed):
- **Major:** potassium citrate, potassium chloride
- **Moderate:** other anticholinergics and antispasmodics (hyoscyamine, atropine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine, trospium, propantheline, and others), opioids (morphine, opium, loperamide, eluxadoline), metoclopramide, aprepitant, pramlintide

Anticholinergic burden is also associated with cognitive and sleep-related adverse effects. A 1990 case report described oxybutynin-induced reflux esophagitis.
