## Bimekizumab: From Plaque Psoriasis to Diabetic Cataract
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Bimekizumab (Bimzelx) is an antibody originally used to treat moderate to severe plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis, and hidradenitis suppurativa.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **diabetic cataract**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction. It is a model-only signal.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Bimekizumab is known to be a monoclonal antibody that neutralizes IL-17A, IL-17F and IL-17AF. Its efficacy in immune-mediated inflammatory skin and joint diseases is the basis of its approved indications.

A possible link to diabetic cataract is that chronic low-grade inflammation and oxidative stress in the diabetic lens may involve IL-17 signaling. This is speculative. No study found in the compiled data shows that blocking IL-17 affects lens opacification.

The other top predictions are all cataract types (tetanic, type 2 diabetes-associated, immature, mature, craniostenosis, cortical, nuclear senile and senile cataract) plus antithrombin deficiency type 2. They have similar scores (about 98%) and similarly lack supporting evidence. Cataract is mainly a degenerative process of oxidative damage and protein aggregation, and mature cataract is treated surgically. The clustering of cataract terms suggests a knowledge-graph artifact rather than a true biological signal.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score, with no clinical trials, no literature, and a speculative mechanism. The marketing authorization is listed as cancelled, and safety data are missing. Systemic immunosuppression is hard to justify for a condition with no established inflammatory IL-17 driver.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, to assess whether IL-17A/F plays any role in diabetic lens injury
- Preclinical evidence (for example, lens or diabetic animal models with IL-17 blockade)
- A literature search for IL-17 and cataract or diabetic lens pathology
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications, and a check of the current EU authorization status
- Route compatibility assessment, since an ocular condition may need a different delivery approach than a systemic antibody
- A risk-benefit review of systemic immunosuppression in diabetic and elderly patients
