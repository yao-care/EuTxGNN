# Treprostinil: From Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension to Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension Associated with Congenital Heart Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Treprostinil is a prostacyclin analogue, authorised in the EU for inoperable or persistent/recurrent chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary arterial hypertension associated with congenital heart disease (CHD-PAH)**, the strongest-supported of the model's top predictions.
There are currently **no registered clinical trials** and **19 publications** (mostly reviews, plus two small cohort studies on prostacyclin therapy in CHD-PAH), so the evidence is indirect and preliminary.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, treprostinil is a prostacyclin analogue that dilates the pulmonary blood vessels and has antiproliferative effects on the vessel wall. Its efficacy in pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) is the basis of its EU authorisation.

CTEPH and CHD-PAH are both forms of pulmonary hypertension with raised pulmonary vascular resistance and risk of right heart failure. Prostacyclin-pathway therapy targets the vasoconstriction and vascular remodelling common to PAH of different causes, including congenital heart disease. Two small observational studies directly address this setting: subcutaneous treprostinil in CHD-PAH over 12 months (PMID 29436381) and long-term continuous prostacyclin therapy in eight adults with CHD-associated pulmonary hypertension (PMID 23890862). One concern is the theoretical risk that vasodilation could worsen right-to-left shunting in patients with intracardiac shunts.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Others, with level unknown, include pantoprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, prednisone, simvastatin and potassium chloride.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and there are small observational studies in CHD-PAH, but there are no registered trials, the evidence is indirect, and the only EU authorisation is cancelled. Safety review is also incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC or package insert (currently missing and blocking the safety screen)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Review of the two CHD-PAH cohort studies for design and outcomes, with particular attention to shunt-related risk
- Controlled or registry-based evidence specific to CHD-PAH
- Assessment of route of administration and formulation, since none was specified in the available data
- Clarification of current regulatory status, given the cancelled authorisation
