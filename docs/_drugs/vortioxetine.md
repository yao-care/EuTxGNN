# Vortioxetine: From Major Depressive Disorder to Neurotic Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Vortioxetine is a multimodal serotonergic antidepressant whose approved use, according to the literature evidence collected here, is major depressive disorder (MDD). The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Neurotic Disorder**, but this direction is currently supported only by **1 real-world clinical study** and **1 case-based review**, with no dedicated randomized trial for this specific diagnostic label.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for vortioxetine is not available in the drug-level fields of this Evidence Pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). However, supporting literature within the predicted-indication evidence describes vortioxetine as a multimodal serotonergic agent: it inhibits the serotonin transporter (SERT), acts as a 5-HT1A receptor agonist and 5-HT1B partial agonist, and antagonizes 5-HT3 and 5-HT7 receptors. This combined receptor profile increases serotonergic, noradrenergic, dopaminergic, and cholinergic neurotransmission in brain regions implicated in mood and anxiety regulation.

"Neurotic disorder" is a broad, largely obsolete ICD-9-era category encompassing anxiety–depressive spectrum conditions. Because vortioxetine's serotonergic mechanism directly targets mood and anxiety circuitry, extrapolation to this category is mechanistically plausible. However, the repurposing rationale in the Evidence Pack explicitly notes that "neurotic disorder" substantially overlaps with the already-established MDD indication rather than representing a clearly distinct, novel therapeutic target — meaning this candidate sits closer to a labeling/nomenclature question than a true new indication.

The single supporting clinical study (NCT04446039) is a large real-world retrospective cohort comparing antidepressant utilization patterns across 370,212 patients, but it was not designed around this specific diagnostic label and provides only indirect supportive signal (relevance grade B). The one literature reference is a case-based review of neurotic depression treatment approaches, further reinforcing plausibility but not establishing direct efficacy evidence for this precise disease label.

**Decision: Hold**

Evidence is limited to a single indirect real-world cohort study and one case-based review (Evidence Level L3), with no trial specifically designed around the "neurotic disorder" diagnostic label. Additionally, this candidate largely overlaps with vortioxetine's already-recognized MDD indication rather than representing a clearly distinct repurposing opportunity, and a Blocking-severity safety data gap (missing label warnings/contraindications) prevents even an initial (S1) safety assessment.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
