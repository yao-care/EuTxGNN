# Gemtuzumab ozogamicin: From Acute Myeloid Leukaemia to Chronic Myelogenous Leukemia, BCR-ABL1 Positive

## One-Sentence Summary

Gemtuzumab ozogamicin (Mylotarg) is a CD33-targeted antibody-drug conjugate, originally authorised in the EU for newly diagnosed CD33-positive acute myeloid leukaemia (AML).
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction with any real supporting data is **chronic myelogenous leukemia, BCR-ABL1 positive** (CML), where evidence is limited to **1 indirectly related clinical trial** and **10 publications**, mostly preclinical work, case reports and reviews.
Of the 10 predictions, only this one and a few AML-adjacent ones have any plausible biology. Many of the others, such as bulbar polio and 5q35 microduplication syndrome, look like model artifacts.

Note: the top-ranked prediction (rank 1) is Richter syndrome, but it has no supporting trials or literature and no plausible CD33 rationale. This report focuses on rank 3 (CML), the highest-ranked prediction with a credible mechanistic link and some evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on what is known, gemtuzumab ozogamicin is an antibody-drug conjugate: a humanised anti-CD33 antibody carries the cytotoxic agent calicheamicin into cells that express CD33. Its efficacy in CD33-positive AML is established, and the literature describes how CD33 expression level and internalisation influence cell killing (PMID 15454492).

The link to CML is plausible but not proven. Preclinical work shows that CD34+/CD38- leukaemic stem cells in CML express CD33 (Siglec-3) and respond to gemtuzumab ozogamicin (PMID 21993666). A case report describes the drug combined with blinatumomab in a patient with refractory mixed-phenotype blast crisis of CML (PMID 34764108). Blast-phase CML behaves like an acute leukaemia, which makes the extension from AML biologically sensible.

However, no prospective trial of gemtuzumab ozogamicin in CML was provided. The one listed trial (NCT03589729) tests dexrazoxane for heart protection during chemotherapy and does not evaluate the efficacy of the drug in CML. The evidence therefore supports a hypothesis, not a treatment recommendation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Hepatic toxicity (from published literature):** Hepatic sinusoidal obstruction / veno-occlusive disease has been reported after treatment, both in patients after stem cell transplantation and in those without transplantation. About 20% of patients in one report had grade 3-4 hyperbilirubinaemia and raised liver enzymes (PMIDs 11895761, 11466696).
- **Drug Interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 entries; the first 20 reviewed include these **Major** interactions:
  - Deferiprone
  - Samarium (153Sm) lexidronam
  - Adalimumab
  - Baricitinib

  Moderate interactions include naltrexone, palifermin, roflumilast, chloramphenicol, zidovudine, alemtuzumab, anakinra, azathioprine and asparaginase products. Several vaccines (tetanus toxoid, anthrax) also appear as moderate interactions, and levofloxacin is a minor one. Several of these likely reflect additive immunosuppression or myelosuppression.

Please refer to the SmPC for the official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The CML prediction has a credible biological basis (CD33 on CML stem cells, plus a case report in blast crisis), but there is no prospective trial of gemtuzumab ozogamicin in CML, and the only listed trial is about heart protection. The drug's only EU authorisation is cancelled, and known liver toxicity raises the bar for any new use. Other predictions are weaker: most have no data, and several (such as bulbar polio, malignant spiradenoma and 5q35 microduplication syndrome) appear to be artifacts. The metastatic neoplasm hit reflects AML trials mapped to a non-specific label. Therapy-related AML/MDS is a coherent extension of the original indication but has no subtype-specific data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Prospective clinical data (at least a well-designed Phase 2 study) of gemtuzumab ozogamicin in CD33-positive blast-phase CML
- Confirmation of CD33 expression in the target patient population
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- The full EU SmPC warnings and contraindications, which could not be retrieved and currently block safety screening
- A liver toxicity (veno-occlusive disease) monitoring and risk-management plan
- Clarification of why the EU authorisation was cancelled, and of the regulatory path for any new use
