# Pemetrexed: From Pleural Mesothelioma and Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer to Malignant Peritoneal Mesothelioma

## One-Sentence Summary

Pemetrexed is an antifolate chemotherapy drug, originally used for malignant pleural mesothelioma and non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **malignant peritoneal mesothelioma**,
with **7 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Pemetrexed inhibits thymidylate synthase, DHFR and GARFT, which disrupts folate-dependent nucleotide synthesis. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the compiled data, so this description comes from the repurposing analysis.

Peritoneal mesothelioma shares histology and biology with pleural mesothelioma, the approved indication. The cisplatin plus pemetrexed combination is the standard treatment for pleural mesothelioma and is already commonly used for the peritoneal form when surgery is not possible. A Phase 2 trial and several retrospective series support this use. Guidelines extrapolate from pleural trials because peritoneal disease is too rare for dedicated large trials.

Other predictions in the list fall into three groups. Pleural mesothelioma and its epithelioid, sarcomatoid and biphasic subtypes are already covered by the approved indication, so they are on-label use rather than true repurposing. Pericardial cancer rests on case reports only. Several low-ranked predictions (pleural adenomatoid tumor, iminoglycinuria, lymphohistiocytoid mesothelioma, well-differentiated papillary mesothelioma) have no trials or only case-level support, and for the benign or indolent conditions cytotoxic therapy is not justified.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication signals (knowledge graph, not label text)**: kidney disease, impaired renal function, pyoureter, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anemia, macrocytic anemia. These come from knowledge-graph drug-disease edges and should be confirmed against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query returned 100 interactions, all of which appear in the listed sample as moderate level. Examples include:
  - NSAID-type and renally cleared agents: acetylsalicylic acid, mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine
  - Acid suppressants: famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine
  - Nephrotoxic or renally cleared agents: amphotericin B (several formulations), vancomycin, kanamycin, neomycin, polymyxin B
  - Others: metformin, amoxicillin, levofloxacin, sapropterin

Label warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the SmPC for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Cisplatin plus pemetrexed is a well-established regimen for pleural mesothelioma, and a completed Phase 2 trial that included peritoneal disease, together with several retrospective series, supports its use in peritoneal mesothelioma. Evidence is at the Phase 2/retrospective level (L2), no peritoneal-specific Phase 3 data exist, and treatment is moving toward immunotherapy-containing regimens. For surgical candidates, cytoreductive surgery with HIPEC remains the preferred approach.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from ongoing randomized Phase 2 trials in peritoneal mesothelioma (NCT05001880, NCT03875144)
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, since this data is currently missing
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A renal function and haematological monitoring plan, given the knowledge-graph contraindication signals
- Clarification of the current EU regulatory status, since all listed authorizations are cancelled
- A comparison with immunotherapy-based first-line options (nivolumab plus ipilimumab)
