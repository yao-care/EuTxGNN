# Loxapine: From Agitation in Schizophrenia and Bipolar Disorder to Manic Bipolar Affective Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Loxapine is a first-generation antipsychotic, authorised in the EU as the inhaled product Adasuve for rapid control of mild-to-moderate agitation in adults with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **manic bipolar affective disorder**, with **no registered clinical trials** but **20 publications** (mostly reviews) discussing this direction.
This is largely an on-label or near-label indication rather than true repurposing, and the evidence for treating mania itself, as opposed to acute agitation, is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, loxapine is a dibenzoxazepine antipsychotic that antagonises dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors. This profile is consistent with the control of manic and agitated states.

Agitation is a common feature of bipolar mania and schizophrenia. The inhaled formulation (Adasuve) is already approved in the EU for agitation associated with bipolar disorder and schizophrenia. The predicted indication therefore overlaps heavily with the existing one. However, the literature mainly addresses acute agitation; evidence for treating a manic episode itself is indirect.

The market status is "Cancelled only", so product availability should be verified before any development plan.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug–disease relationships in the TxGNN knowledge graph, not from label text): epilepsy, benign prostatic hyperplasia, substance abuse/dependence, female breast carcinoma, cardiovascular and heart disease, glaucoma, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, Alzheimer disease, tardive dyskinesia, lingual-facial-buccal dyskinesia, and hyperprolactinaemia (including familial and acquired forms and prolactin-producing pituitary tumour).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major-level ones include **bupropion**, **morphine** and **potassium citrate**. Many moderate-level ones involve anticholinergic or gastrointestinal agents (e.g. atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine, trospium, loperamide), as well as epinephrine, dronabinol, nabilone, lorcaserin and eluxadoline.

For warnings and contraindications from the label, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication overlaps substantially with the existing approved use (agitation in bipolar disorder and schizophrenia), and the literature supports the drug class in this setting. However, there are no registered trials for mania itself, the evidence is mainly narrative reviews (L3), and the EU authorisation is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of product availability and regulatory status in the EU, given the cancelled authorisation
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Direct evidence in manic episodes (not only agitation), such as controlled trials with mania rating scale endpoints
- A safety plan addressing the knowledge-graph contraindication signals and the major drug interactions
