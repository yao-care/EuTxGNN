## Tenofovir alafenamide: From Chronic Hepatitis B to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) is originally used to treat chronic hepatitis B in adults and in children aged 6 years and older weighing at least 25 kg.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, a condition of non-human primates.
Support consists of **0 clinical trials** and **8 publications**, all preclinical (mostly macaque or mouse studies).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, TAF is a prodrug of tenofovir. Its active form, tenofovir diphosphate, inhibits reverse transcriptase and terminates the growing viral DNA chain. This is the basis of its activity in hepatitis B.

SIV and SHIV (a simian/human hybrid virus) are lentiviruses that also replicate through reverse transcriptase, so the mechanism is biologically plausible. Macaque studies of prevention (PrEP), post-exposure protection and treatment report protective or suppressive effects of TAF, alone or combined with emtricitabine, elvitegravir or other agents.

SIV infection is a veterinary and preclinical model condition. Its human relevance is as a surrogate for HIV. This evidence is therefore indirect and is not new human clinical evidence for repurposing. The other two predictions (feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome and a rare neurodevelopmental disorder) have no supporting studies. The neurodevelopmental one has no identifiable mechanistic link.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is preclinical only, with no registered clinical trials. SIV infection is an animal model condition, so the findings have no direct human repurposing use. The score (0.9989) is a computational prediction and not proof of efficacy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarify whether the real goal is a human HIV indication, which should be assessed with human HIV-1 data and existing combination approvals rather than SIV data
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Warnings and contraindications from the EU product information (SmPC)
