## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Tasimelteon is known to be a dual MT1/MT2 melatonin receptor agonist that acts on circadian regulation, which is why it was developed for Non-24.

No identifiable mechanistic link connects this mechanism to the predicted condition. Polymicrogyria is a malformation of the cerebral cortex with a developmental or genetic origin. It is not a disorder of circadian rhythm or melatonin signalling.

The high TxGNN score (99.48%) most likely reflects proximity in the underlying knowledge graph rather than biological or clinical evidence. It should not be read as a sign of efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a graph-based score. There are no trials or publications, and no plausible mechanistic link between melatonin receptor agonism and a cortical malformation. The only EU authorisation is cancelled, which further limits near-term development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanistic or preclinical evidence connecting MT1/MT2 signalling to the pathology of polymicrogyria
- Mechanism of action data for tasimelteon
- Parsed safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the EU product information
- Clarification of the regulatory status of the cancelled authorisation

**Note on other predictions:** Among the other predicted indications, **insomnia** is far better supported. It has a Phase 3 randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of tasimelteon in paediatric insomnia ([NCT06953869](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06953869), n=420, recruiting, no results yet). It also has supporting review literature and a clear mechanistic rationale. Prioritising insomnia for further evaluation would be more productive than pursuing polymicrogyria.
