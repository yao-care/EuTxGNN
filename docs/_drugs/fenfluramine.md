## One-Sentence Summary

Fenfluramine is an add-on antiepileptic medicine originally used to treat seizures associated with Dravet syndrome and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **proximal 16p11.2 microdeletion syndrome**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, so it rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, fenfluramine is an add-on antiepileptic medicine, its efficacy in seizures of Dravet syndrome and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome is the basis of its authorization, and it may mechanistically be applicable to other neurodevelopmental conditions with epilepsy.

The 16p11.2 deletion is associated with neurodevelopmental phenotypes, epilepsy and obesity. Fenfluramine is known to have serotonergic and sigma-1 receptor activity that is relevant to seizure control, which could form a loose link. This reasoning is speculative. No retrieved trial or publication supports it, and the very high score (0.9993) comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning). It may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** hypertension and hypertensive disorder, pulmonary hypertension, type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, glaucoma, and substance abuse/dependence. Fenfluramine was historically withdrawn from the market as an appetite suppressant because of valvular heart disease and pulmonary hypertension, so cardiac and pulmonary monitoring is a key concern.
- **Drug Interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs. Major-level interactions include fentanyl, dextromethorphan, tramadol, alfentanil and almotriptan. These are mostly opioids and serotonergic agents, which raises a serotonin-related concern. Moderate-level interactions include abciximab, abiraterone, acalabrutinib, acarbose, codeine, dihydrocodeine, formoterol, ketorolac, ibuprofen, salbutamol, ethanol, alogliptin and pioglitazone.

Please also refer to the SmPC for the full safety information.
