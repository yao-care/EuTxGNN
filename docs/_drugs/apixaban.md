# Apixaban: From Venous Thromboembolism and Stroke Prevention to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Apixaban is an oral factor Xa inhibitor (anticoagulant), originally used to prevent stroke in non-valvular atrial fibrillation and to treat and prevent venous thromboembolism.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but support is weak: **1 clinical trial** (indirect, not migraine-focused) and **3 publications** (two case reports with conflicting results and one small retrospective study of antithrombotic therapy in general).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, apixaban is a direct factor Xa inhibitor. Its efficacy in preventing thromboembolism is established, and mechanistically it could be relevant to conditions with a thrombotic or hypercoagulable component.

The link to migraine is indirect and speculative. Paradoxical embolism through a patent foramen ovale, and hypercoagulability associated with antiphospholipid antibodies, have been proposed as contributors to migraine, especially with aura. In theory, anticoagulation might help selected subgroups. However, nothing suggests that apixaban acts on core migraine pathophysiology such as CGRP signalling or cortical spreading depression.

The published apixaban reports also conflict. One describes migraine with aura worsening after starting apixaban. Another describes a patient whose aura resolved on warfarin but not on apixaban. The high model score therefore should be read as a hypothesis-generating signal, not as evidence of benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs. Examples of **Major** interactions include acetylsalicylic acid, cobicistat, deferasirox, ibritumomab tiuxetan and tositumomab. Examples of **Moderate** interactions include aprepitant, clarithromycin, clotrimazole, dexamethasone, miconazole, fenfluramine and sugammadex.
- As an anticoagulant, apixaban carries a bleeding risk, and the case report of migraine worsening suggests that tolerability in migraine patients is uncertain.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only evidence for migraine is a model prediction, one indirect Phase 3 trial, and conflicting case reports, including one reporting worsening on apixaban. The mechanistic link is speculative, and no currently valid EU authorization appears in the data compiled for this report.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to assess a plausible link to migraine
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC
- Prospective controlled data in a defined subgroup (e.g., migraine with aura plus PFO or antiphospholipid antibodies) before any further consideration
- Confirmation of current EU regulatory status, since both authorizations are listed as cancelled

**Other predictions in the ranking (for context only):**
- **Rheumatoid arthritis** and **pulmonary hypertension** are better framed as research questions. For rheumatoid arthritis there is preclinical evidence that apixaban inhibits FXa-mediated JAK2/STAT3 and MAPK signalling. For pulmonary hypertension there is a plausible link only in thromboembolic subtypes such as CTEPH, with mixed evidence elsewhere.
- **Leprosy**, **Prinzmetal angina**, **brachydactyly-syndactyly syndrome**, **colobomatous microphthalmia-rhizomelic dysplasia syndrome** and **kyphoscoliotic heart disease** have no supporting evidence and are likely model artifacts.
