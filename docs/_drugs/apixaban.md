## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Apixaban is known as a direct oral factor Xa inhibitor, which reduces thrombin generation and clot formation.

The link to migraine is indirect and speculative. Thromboembolic mechanisms, such as paradoxical embolism through a patent foramen ovale, and hypercoagulability associated with antiphospholipid antibodies have been proposed as contributors to migraine. Anticoagulation could therefore help in selected subgroups.

However, nothing shows that apixaban acts on core migraine pathophysiology such as CGRP signalling or cortical spreading depression. The published apixaban case reports contradict each other. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis, not a supported use.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions**: The interaction database lists 100 interacting drugs. Among those shown, the following are classed as **Major**:
- Acetylsalicylic acid
- Cobicistat
- Deferasirox
- Ibritumomab tiuxetan
- Tositumomab / Tositumomab (I-131)

Many other agents are classed as Moderate, including aprepitant, clarithromycin, clotrimazole, dexamethasone, fenfluramine, miconazole and rolapitant.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC. As an anticoagulant, apixaban carries a bleeding risk that matters especially when it is combined with antiplatelet drugs such as acetylsalicylic acid.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only linked trial does not study migraine, and the literature consists of two contradictory case reports and one retrospective study in which apixaban was not clearly the tested agent. No cited study directly shows benefit in migraine, so the evidence level is L5, and the high TxGNN score (0.9902) is not backed by clinical data. Bleeding risk also argues against use without stronger evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for apixaban and a plausible pathway to migraine
- A prospective controlled study in a defined subgroup, such as migraine with PFO or antiphospholipid antibodies
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, for a benefit-risk assessment in migraine patients
