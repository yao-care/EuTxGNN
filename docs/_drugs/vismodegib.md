## One-Sentence Summary

Vismodegib is an oral Hedgehog pathway (SMO) inhibitor, originally used to treat symptomatic metastatic or locally advanced basal cell carcinoma that cannot be treated with surgery or radiotherapy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **medulloblastoma with extensive nodularity**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Vismodegib blocks Smoothened (SMO), a key protein in the Hedgehog signaling pathway. In basal cell carcinoma, this pathway is constantly switched on, usually through PTCH1 loss or SMO activating mutations. Blocking SMO removes the growth signal that drives these tumors.

Medulloblastoma, especially the SHH-activated subgroup, is also driven by abnormal Hedgehog signaling (PTCH1, SMO or SUFU alterations). The "extensive nodularity" subtype is strongly associated with SHH activation. The shared pathway makes the prediction biologically plausible.

Several caveats apply:
- No trials or literature for this specific entity were found in the data compiled for this report.
- SMO inhibitors carry known concerns about premature growth plate fusion in children. This matters because medulloblastoma often affects pediatric patients.
- The support is purely a model prediction.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 56 interactions were identified. The only moderate-level interaction in the listed results is **clarithromycin**. The rest are minor and mostly involve acid-reducing agents and antacids, such as H2 blockers (famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, nizatidine), proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole, dexlansoprazole), and magnesium, aluminum, calcium and sodium-based antacids.
- **Class-related concerns**: Premature growth plate fusion in pediatric patients is a known concern for SMO inhibitors. Teratogenicity precautions are required. Commonly reported adverse effects include muscle spasms, alopecia, dysgeusia and weight loss.

Please refer to the SmPC for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link between SMO inhibition and SHH-driven medulloblastoma is plausible. However, there are no trials or publications for this specific entity, the evidence level is L5, and pediatric growth-plate toxicity is a significant concern. The only EU authorization is also cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Preclinical or clinical evidence in SHH-activated medulloblastoma, particularly the extensive nodularity subtype
- A pediatric safety assessment, especially for growth plate effects
- Confirmation of the current EU authorization status
- Detailed mechanism of action and SmPC warnings and contraindications to complete the safety screening

**Note on other predictions:** Among the other model predictions, the broader "skin cancer" category has the strongest support (L2, Phase 2 trials, European guidelines). However, it largely overlaps with vismodegib's original basal cell carcinoma indication, so it is not a true repurposing. Xeroderma pigmentosum has case-report-level evidence (L4) of vismodegib clearing basal cell carcinomas in affected patients.
