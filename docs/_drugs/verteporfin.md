## One-Sentence Summary

Verteporfin is a photosensitizer used in photodynamic therapy, originally approved for choroidal neovascularisation in wet age-related macular degeneration and in pathological myopia.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Verteporfin is generally known as a photosensitizer. When activated by light, it generates reactive oxygen species that occlude abnormal new blood vessels in the eye. It has also been reported to inhibit YAP-TEAD signaling.

Neither action has an established connection to oxidative phosphorylation deficiency caused by nuclear DNA defects. The reactive oxygen species generated by photosensitizers could plausibly worsen mitochondrial dysfunction rather than help it. The high TxGNN score reflects proximity in a knowledge graph, not biological or clinical evidence, so the prediction should be treated with caution.

Other predicted indications in the data (ranks 2–10) are also supported only by model scores or indirect evidence. These include esophageal varices, exocrine pancreatic insufficiency, varicose disease and several rare retinal conditions. The most notable are exocrine pancreatic insufficiency, with one preclinical paper on YAP and pancreatic fibrosis (a pathway verteporfin is known to inhibit), and retinal dystrophy in lipidoses, with one 2007 review of ocular photodynamic therapy. Both remain research questions rather than actionable leads.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score. No clinical trials or publications support it, and there is no plausible mechanistic link. The photosensitizer's reactive oxygen species may even be harmful in mitochondrial disease. The EU authorization has also been cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, to assess biological plausibility
- Preclinical evidence in mitochondrial disease models, including the effect of reactive oxygen species generation
- Label-based safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC
- Clarification of why the EU authorization was cancelled and of the available routes of administration, including light delivery
- If an alternative lead is wanted, a targeted evidence review of exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (YAP pathway) or ocular conditions with a neovascular component
