## One-Sentence Summary

Verteporfin is a photosensitizer used in photodynamic therapy, originally approved for choroidal neovascularisation in wet age-related macular degeneration and in pathological myopia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation disorder due to nuclear DNA anomalies**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction. It is a computational prediction only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Verteporfin is generally known as a photosensitizer. When activated by light, it generates reactive oxygen species that occlude abnormal new blood vessels in the eye. It has also been reported to inhibit YAP-TEAD signaling.

Neither action has an established connection to oxidative phosphorylation deficiency caused by nuclear DNA defects. The reactive oxygen species generated by photosensitizers could plausibly worsen mitochondrial dysfunction rather than help it.

Other predicted indications in the data (ranks 2–10) are also supported only by model scores or indirect evidence. Both remain research questions rather than actionable leads.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not label text): inherited porphyria, porphyrin metabolism disease, porphyria, liver disease, gallbladder disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified in total. The 20 listed in the data are mostly of moderate severity, and one (polymyxin B) is minor. Main examples:
  - Tetracycline antibiotics (doxycycline, tetracycline, minocycline), which may increase photosensitivity
  - Sulfonylurea antidiabetics (glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, chlorpropamide, tolbutamide, tolazamide, acetohexamide)
  - Other: acetylsalicylic acid, levofloxacin, sulfasalazine, mannitol, papaverine, beta carotene
