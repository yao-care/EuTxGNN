## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Tralokinumab is known to be an anti-IL-13 monoclonal antibody, and its efficacy in atopic dermatitis is tied to type 2 inflammation.

A speculative link to diabetic cataract could run through IL-13 and type 2 inflammation. However, cataract in diabetes is driven mainly by polyol pathway activity, oxidative stress, and glycation of lens proteins. Atopic dermatitis is associated with cataract (atopic cataract), but that is a disease association, not evidence of a therapeutic effect.

The high score likely reflects proximity among cataract-related conditions in the knowledge graph rather than independent evidence. Nine other cataract subtypes ranked with similar scores (98.5%–98.6%), including craniostenosis, immature, tetanic, mature, type 2 diabetes-associated, cortical, nuclear senile, and senile cataract. This pattern supports that interpretation. Diabetic retinopathy (98.4%) is the most biologically plausible of the ten candidates, because inflammation and vascular dysfunction contribute to it. IL-13's role there is still unestablished and would need preclinical validation.
