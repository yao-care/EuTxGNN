# Vernakalant: From Atrial Fibrillation Cardioversion to Stroke Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Vernakalant is an atrial-selective antiarrhythmic agent used for the acute pharmacological cardioversion of atrial fibrillation (AF). The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Stroke Disorder**, but the supporting evidence — **3 clinical trials** and **7 publications** — addresses AF cardioversion outcomes rather than stroke prevention directly, so this is best framed as an indirect, mechanistically plausible hypothesis rather than a validated new indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed, formally sourced mechanism-of-action data is not available for vernakalant in this Evidence Pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). However, based on the trial and literature context collected, vernakalant is known to act as an atrial-selective sodium/potassium ion channel blocker, used to convert atrial fibrillation to sinus rhythm ("pharmacological cardioversion").

The link between AF and stroke is well established: AF is one of the most important risk factors for ischemic stroke, since disorganized atrial contraction promotes thrombus formation and embolization. Restoring sinus rhythm is therefore hypothesized to reduce thromboembolic risk over time, which is the biological rationale the TxGNN model appears to be capturing.

However, this is an **indirect** mechanistic link — vernakalant treats the upstream arrhythmia (AF), not stroke itself, and none of the identified trials used stroke incidence, thromboembolic events, or neurological outcomes as a primary or secondary endpoint. All three related trials were graded "B" relevance (AF cardioversion efficacy vs. comparator drugs), meaning the connection to stroke prevention remains theoretical rather than clinically demonstrated.
