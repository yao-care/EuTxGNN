## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, vernakalant is an atrial-selective antiarrhythmic agent. Its efficacy in converting recent-onset atrial fibrillation to sinus rhythm is the basis of its original approval.

The link between the original and the new indication is indirect. Stroke is a major consequence of atrial fibrillation, so restoring sinus rhythm could in theory reduce thromboembolic risk. However, cardioversion itself carries a peri-cardioversion thromboembolic risk. No evidence shows that vernakalant prevents or treats stroke.

The high TxGNN score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases (such as AF and stroke), not biological evidence of a direct drug effect. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis only.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SmPC for safety information.

For context, the compiled rationale notes that vernakalant's labeling cautions against use in recent acute coronary syndrome and severe heart failure. It also notes that the drug could worsen bradycardia or sinus node dysfunction and is generally cautioned against in conduction disorders. No drug interaction data were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The stroke prediction rests on an indirect link through atrial fibrillation. There are no registered clinical trials and no publication showing a vernakalant effect on stroke. The only EU marketing authorization has been cancelled, and the cardioversion-related thromboembolic risk is an additional concern.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Safety information from the EMA product information (warnings and contraindications), which is currently missing and blocks safety screening
- Clinical or observational evidence on whether vernakalant affects stroke or thromboembolic outcomes
- Clarification of why the EU authorization was cancelled and whether the drug is available in the EU
