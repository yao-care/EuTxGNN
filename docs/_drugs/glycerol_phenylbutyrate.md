## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From what is known about the compound, glycerol phenylbutyrate is metabolized to phenylacetate, which conjugates with glutamine to form phenylacetylglutamine. This is excreted in urine and provides a route for nitrogen removal that bypasses the urea cycle.

Citrullinemia type II (citrin deficiency) causes hyperammonemia through impaired urea cycle flux and disrupted aspartate supply. An alternative nitrogen-excretion pathway could therefore plausibly lower ammonia, which is the basis for the model's prediction.

There are important caveats. Dietary management in citrin deficiency (low carbohydrate, higher protein and fat) differs from that in classic UCDs, so applicability is uncertain. No clinical trial or publication was retrieved to support the prediction.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by the model score, with no clinical trials or literature (Evidence Level L5). The EU authorization is cancelled, and safety and mechanism data are incomplete. The mechanistic rationale for citrin deficiency is plausible but unproven, and applicability is uncertain because its dietary management differs from that of classic UCDs.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the product information, which is required before any safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A systematic literature search, including case reports, on nitrogen scavengers in citrin deficiency
- Comparison with established treatments and with current clinical practice for citrin deficiency
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorization status and of formulation and route compatibility
- If a repurposing study is pursued, consider hyperammonemic encephalopathy due to carbonic anhydrase VA deficiency (rank 4) in parallel, as it is the most mechanistically plausible candidate
