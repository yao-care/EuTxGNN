# Bupropion: From Weight Management to Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Bupropion was authorized in the EU as Mysimba, for weight management in adults with obesity or overweight and weight-related comorbidities.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
with **6 clinical trials** (including one completed Phase 3 trial) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

*Note on evidence level:* Only one completed Phase 3 randomized trial is identified (NCT00048360), and one completed Phase 2/3 trial (NCT00061087) exists in a specialized population. Under the stated rules (≥2 completed Phase 3 RCTs for L1), this falls to L2, although a Cochrane review of bupropion in adult ADHD is also available.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, bupropion inhibits the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine (an NDRI). This overlaps with the catecholaminergic mechanism of established ADHD treatments such as methylphenidate and atomoxetine.

The original indication (weight management) and the predicted indication (ADHD) are not closely related clinically. The link is mechanistic: dopaminergic and noradrenergic modulation in prefrontal circuits is a plausible basis for improved attention and impulse control. Bupropion is generally considered a second-line or off-label option for ADHD rather than first-line therapy.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (these come from drug-disease links in the TxGNN knowledge graph, not from product label text): epilepsy, hypertension, liver disease and liver failure, cirrhosis, kidney disease, bipolar disorder (manic), psychotic and schizophrenia-spectrum disorders, anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, substance and opiate dependence, anxiety disorder, myocardial infarction, and central nervous system neoplasm.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major-level examples include fentanyl, lidocaine, codeine, dihydrocodeine, tramadol, methylphenidate, hydrocortisone, aldesleukin, alfentanil, and amantadine. Moderate-level examples include ethanol, alprazolam, nifedipine, hydrochlorothiazide, and cetirizine.
- **Additional guardrails for ADHD use**: dose-dependent seizure risk, blood pressure and heart rate monitoring, extensive CYP2D6 interactions, and caution in patients with comorbid substance use or eating disorders.

Please refer to the SmPC for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial, additional trials in comorbid populations, and a Cochrane review support bupropion in adult ADHD. However, it is a second-line option, the marketing authorization in the EU is cancelled, and the safety profile (seizures, blood pressure, many interactions) requires careful control.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The current product label warnings and contraindications (not yet available, and currently blocking formal safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Verification of the bupropion arm and comparator in NCT00048360 and NCT00061087
- A regulatory pathway, since there is no valid EU authorization for the original product
- A monitoring plan for seizure risk, blood pressure, and heart rate, and a strategy for patients with comorbid substance use or eating disorders
