# Thiotepa: From High-Dose Conditioning Chemotherapy to Adult Germ Cell Tumor

## One-Sentence Summary

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for thiotepa is not available in this Evidence Pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on the information that is available, thiotepa is an alkylating agent that inhibits tumour cell replication via DNA cross-linking. It is a core component of high-dose chemotherapy (HDCT) conditioning regimens used before autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT), typically combined with agents such as carboplatin and etoposide (e.g., the TVCa or BET regimens).

Germ cell tumours are highly chemosensitive, and salvage HDCT with ASCT is an established treatment strategy for relapsed or refractory disease. The clinical trial and literature evidence collected here show that thiotepa-based regimens have already been used in real-world practice for germ cell tumours, including both testicular/ovarian and central nervous system (CNS) germ cell tumours, following relapse after conventional platinum-based chemotherapy. This means the TxGNN prediction is not proposing an entirely novel mechanism, but rather reinforces and consolidates existing off-label clinical experience into a structured indication signal.

Because the original indication and formal MOA are not documented in this pack, the mechanistic rationale above relies on the model's own supporting evidence (clinical trials and literature) rather than an approved label reference.
