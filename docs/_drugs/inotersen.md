# Inotersen: From Hereditary Transthyretin Amyloidosis Polyneuropathy to Acute Intermittent Porphyria

## One-Sentence Summary

Inotersen was originally used to treat polyneuropathy in adults with hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (hATTR).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acute intermittent porphyria**, but there are currently **0 registered clinical trials** and only **1 review article** behind this prediction. The evidence is essentially a computational prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the data compiled for this report. Inotersen is an antisense oligonucleotide that targets transthyretin (TTR) messenger RNA, reducing production of the TTR protein. This is the mechanism through which it treats hATTR polyneuropathy.

The link to acute intermittent porphyria (AIP) is only indirect. Both conditions can cause peripheral neuropathy, and the one retrieved publication is a review of genetic neuromuscular disorders and oligonucleotide-based therapies. Nothing in the data shows that lowering TTR affects heme biosynthesis or porphyria attacks. The very high TxGNN score is a computational output and should not be read as evidence of efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no clinical trials, and the only publication is a general review with no direct link to acute intermittent porphyria. The mechanism is not supported, and the only EU authorization has been cancelled. The prediction should stay at the research-question stage.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Label warnings and contraindications from the EMA package insert, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Preclinical evidence on whether TTR knockdown affects heme biosynthesis or porphyria attacks
- A safety review for thrombocytopenia and glomerulonephritis, which are flagged as contraindicated in the knowledge graph
- Clarification of why the EU authorization was cancelled
