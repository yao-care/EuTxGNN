## Nivolumab: From Advanced Melanoma and Multiple Cancers to Non-Cutaneous Melanoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Nivolumab is a PD-1 immune checkpoint inhibitor originally authorised in the EU for melanoma, lung cancer, kidney cancer, Hodgkin lymphoma and several other cancers.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **non-cutaneous melanoma**, with **40 registered clinical trials** and **9 publications** compiled for this prediction. Most of these are early-phase studies or case-level reports.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Nivolumab blocks PD-1 (encoded by *PDCD1*), a receptor on T cells. Blocking it helps restore the T-cell antitumour response. This mechanism does not depend on where the melanoma arises, so it can plausibly apply to melanomas outside the skin.

The approved melanoma indication already covers advanced melanoma broadly. Non-cutaneous subtypes (mucosal, acral, uveal-type sites) are generally less responsive than cutaneous disease. They have less UV-driven neoantigen load, so efficacy is expected to be lower. Case reports and cohorts nonetheless describe responses in Asian patients and in mucosal and anorectal melanoma.

Other predicted melanoma subtypes in the same list include epithelioid cell, superficial spreading, acral lentiginous, nodular, lentigo maligna, amelanotic, mucosal, CDK4-linked and balloon cell melanoma. Their evidence varies widely, from a completed Phase 2 trial and pooled analyses for mucosal melanoma to no studies at all for CDK4-linked melanoma.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 14 interaction records were found.
  - **Major**: lenalidomide, pomalidomide, thalidomide.
  - **Moderate**: corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisolone, prednisone, methylprednisolone, deflazacort) and idelalisib.
- **Immune-related adverse events (from the literature)**: Case reports describe fatal or fulminant myocarditis with checkpoint inhibitors, capillary leak syndrome, bullous pemphigoid, pulmonary sarcoid-like granulomatosis, and vitiligo-like depigmentation. A series of 79 cases reported severe late-onset grade III-IV adverse events.

Please refer to the SmPC for formal warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Nivolumab is already authorised in the EU for advanced melanoma, and the PD-1 mechanism is not site-specific. Prospective and retrospective data exist for mucosal, acral and uveal melanoma, but responses are generally lower than in cutaneous disease and the trials are small or terminated. The EU authorisation record shows "cancelled only", and the safety data for this report are incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the current EU authorisation status of Opdivo, and download the SmPC for warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank (only the PD-1 target is currently documented)
- Subtype-specific efficacy data (mucosal, acral, uveal), including results from NCT05384496 and NCT05089370
- Clarify the benefit of adjuvant nivolumab in acral melanoma, given the negative retrospective signal
- A monitoring plan for immune-related adverse events, especially myocarditis, and review of corticosteroid and immunomodulator co-medication
- For subtypes with no or only case-level evidence (CDK4-linked, balloon cell, lentigo maligna, nodular, amelanotic, epithelioid cell), treat as research questions rather than candidates for repurposing
