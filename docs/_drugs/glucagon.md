## Glucagon: From Severe Hypoglycaemia to Irritable Bowel Syndrome
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Glucagon was originally authorised in the EU for treating severe hypoglycaemia in people with diabetes mellitus.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)**.
Support is indirect: **2 early-phase clinical trials** (both of GLP-1-type drugs, not glucagon) and **about 20 publications** are linked to this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, glucagon is a pancreatic peptide hormone, its efficacy in severe hypoglycaemia has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to IBS.

Glucagon and the related GLP-1 family peptides inhibit gastrointestinal motility and may modulate visceral sensation through the gut-brain axis. Glucagon is also known as a short-acting smooth muscle relaxant in the gut, which is plausible for spasm-related IBS symptoms.

However, the link is indirect. The two trials of IBS-related motility used GLP-1 or the GLP-1 analogue ROSE-010, not glucagon. No trial in the data compiled for this report tests glucagon itself in IBS.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 25 interactions were identified, all graded Minor. They include warfarin and a broad group of beta-blockers (for example atenolol, bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol, propranolol, labetalol, sotalol, and ophthalmic timolol).

No warnings or contraindications were available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but the supporting trials and publications concern GLP-1 analogues, not glucagon, and none tests glucagon in IBS. In addition, the EU authorisations are all cancelled, and mechanism and label safety data are missing. This is currently a research question rather than an actionable repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct evidence for glucagon in IBS, such as a small proof-of-concept study on symptoms or motility
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorisation status and available formulations and routes
