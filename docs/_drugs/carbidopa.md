# Carbidopa: From Parkinson's Disease to Rasmussen Subacute Encephalitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Carbidopa is a dopa decarboxylase inhibitor used together with levodopa (and entacapone in combination products) to treat Parkinson's disease with end-of-dose motor fluctuations.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Rasmussen subacute encephalitis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** support this prediction, and no plausible mechanism has been identified.
Among the other predicted diseases, Lewy body dementia has the most supporting material (1 trial, 15 publications), though the evidence is mostly indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, carbidopa inhibits peripheral DOPA decarboxylase (DDC). It has no antiparkinson effect by itself, but it prevents levodopa from being converted to dopamine outside the brain, so more levodopa reaches the central nervous system.

Rasmussen subacute encephalitis is an immune-mediated, unilateral hemispheric brain inflammation. Peripheral DDC inhibition has no established role in this process, so the prediction is not supported by a mechanistic link. It should be treated as a model-generated hypothesis only.

Other predictions are more plausible. In Lewy body dementia, nigrostriatal dopaminergic loss produces parkinsonism that levodopa/carbidopa can treat. In MCT8 deficiency (Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome), which is mapped to the X-linked intellectual disability predictions, reports describe parkinsonian features that responded to levodopa/carbidopa. Both rest on extrapolation from Parkinson's disease or on small uncontrolled reports.

## Safety Considerations

Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC for safety information.

- **Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph (not label text):** The TxGNN knowledge graph links carbidopa to contraindication-type relationships with several disease groups. These include psychiatric disorders (psychotic, schizoaffective, anxiety, and dysthymic disorders), cardiovascular conditions (myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, orthostatic hypotension), and hepatic, renal, and gastrointestinal diseases (liver failure, kidney disease, peptic and gastric ulcer, gallbladder disease). They also include epilepsy, open-angle glaucoma, diabetes and diabetic ketoacidosis, and melanoma. These signals should be verified against the official label.
- **Drug interactions:** The only interaction record is pharmacological. Carbidopa targets L-aromatic amino-acid decarboxylase (DDC), which is its intended mechanism and not a drug-drug interaction.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, Rasmussen subacute encephalitis, has a high model score but no trials, no literature, and no plausible mechanism (evidence level L5). In addition, all EU authorisations for carbidopa-containing products in the data are cancelled. Lewy body dementia (L3) and MCT8 deficiency-related syndromes (L4) are better-supported research questions, but their evidence is indirect or limited to small uncontrolled reports.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The mechanism of action (MOA) and the SmPC warnings and contraindications for carbidopa, to complete the safety screen
- A mechanistic rationale and preclinical or clinical data for Rasmussen subacute encephalitis, or a decision to prioritise Lewy body dementia or MCT8 deficiency instead
- For Lewy body dementia, a controlled therapeutic study of levodopa/carbidopa in that population
- For MCT8 deficiency, a prospective controlled study, with confirmation of how the disease maps to the predicted X-linked syndromes
- Clarification of the current EU regulatory pathway, since the existing authorisations are cancelled
