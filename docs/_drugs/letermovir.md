# Letermovir: From CMV Prophylaxis in Transplant Recipients to Vulvovaginal Candidiasis

## One-Sentence Summary

Letermovir (Prevymis) is an antiviral originally used to prevent cytomegalovirus (CMV) reactivation and disease in transplant recipients.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **vulvovaginal candidiasis**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this prediction.
The very high score appears to be a knowledge-graph artifact of the drug's anti-infective class rather than a real mechanistic signal.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, letermovir is a CMV-specific antiviral that inhibits the viral terminase complex (pUL56/UL51/UL89), which is needed to cut and package viral DNA.

That target has no known counterpart in fungi. *Candida*, the cause of vulvovaginal candidiasis, is a fungus and does not use a herpesvirus-type terminase. There is therefore no plausible mechanistic link between the original indication and the predicted one. The 99.88% score most likely reflects the drug's general anti-infective class in the knowledge graph, not a real biological signal.

In short, this prediction should be treated as a model artifact. It should not be treated as a repurposing lead.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

No trials or literature support letermovir for vulvovaginal candidiasis. The drug's target, a viral terminase, is absent in fungi, so the high TxGNN score is not backed by mechanism or evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A credible mechanistic hypothesis for antifungal activity, with in vitro data against *Candida* species
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
