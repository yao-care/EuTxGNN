# Insulin Detemir: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin detemir is a long-acting basal insulin analogue, originally authorised in the EU for treating diabetes mellitus in adults, adolescents and children aged 1 year and above.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **type 1 diabetes mellitus**, with **37 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
This is essentially an on-label use rather than a true repurposing case.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data was not available in the data compiled for this report. From the published literature, insulin detemir is a soluble, long-acting insulin analogue. It binds the insulin receptor and lowers blood glucose. A fatty acid (myristic acid) attached to the molecule lets it bind reversibly to albumin, which slows absorption and prolongs its effect.

Type 1 diabetes is characterised by absolute insulin deficiency, so basal insulin replacement is a core part of treatment. The predicted indication falls within the drug's already approved indication of diabetes mellitus. The high TxGNN score is therefore consistent with the established mechanism, and this should be read as confirmation of an existing use rather than a newly discovered one.

Other predicted conditions (for example autoimmune oophoritis, opsismodysplasia, stiff person syndrome and lipodystrophies) had no clinical trials or literature. They remain graph-based predictions with speculative or indirect links, such as comorbid diabetes. Lipodystrophy is also a known adverse effect of injected insulin, so those predictions may be safety signals rather than therapeutic ones. Pancreatic agenesis is mechanistically plausible as insulin-deficient diabetes, but evidence is indirect, and detemir is not approved for infants under 1 year.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified in the data compiled for this report. Most of the examples listed are of moderate severity, and a few are minor (topical corticosteroids). Moderate examples include:
  - Other glucose-lowering drugs: metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, albiglutide.
  - Sympathomimetics: epinephrine, phenylephrine, pseudoephedrine, salbutamol, formoterol.
  - Beta-blocker: acebutolol.
  - Hormones and corticosteroids: ethinylestradiol, estradiol, hydrocortisone.
  - Diuretics: acetazolamide, hydrochlorothiazide.
  - Others: alpelisib, doxycycline, ethanol.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC for safety information. In particular, hypoglycaemia monitoring and standard basal-bolus titration remain necessary.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The type 1 diabetes prediction is supported by multiple completed Phase 3 trials and several systematic reviews, which gives Level L1 evidence. However, this is the drug's established on-label use rather than a new indication. The only EU authorisation is cancelled, so availability is a practical limit. The other nine predictions have little or no supporting evidence and should remain on hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm product availability, since the EU authorisation is cancelled and no valid licence is currently recorded
- Retrieve the SmPC warnings and contraindications
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Set up a hypoglycaemia monitoring and dose-titration plan
- For pancreatic agenesis, a dedicated evaluation would be needed, as detemir is not approved for infants under 1 year
