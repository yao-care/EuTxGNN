# Mercaptopurine: From Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia to Myeloid Leukaemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite chemotherapy drug, originally approved in the EU (as Xaluprine) for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL) in adults, adolescents and children.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukaemia**. The data compiled for this report list **15 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, but in most trials the contribution of mercaptopurine itself cannot be separated from the multi-drug regimens used.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for mercaptopurine are not available in the data compiled for this report. Mercaptopurine is known to be a purine antimetabolite. Its thioguanine nucleotide metabolites are incorporated into DNA and RNA and inhibit de novo purine synthesis. This is toxic to rapidly dividing cells, including leukaemic blasts.

ALL and myeloid leukaemia are both blood cancers of fast-dividing blasts, so a drug that disrupts nucleotide synthesis could plausibly act in both. In ALL, mercaptopurine plus methotrexate is a core maintenance backbone. In acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), the available evidence is mostly for mercaptopurine as part of multi-drug regimens or oral maintenance with methotrexate. There is little evidence that it works as a stand-alone treatment. How much mercaptopurine contributes in these studies remains uncertain.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** The TxGNN knowledge graph links mercaptopurine with contraindication edges to kidney disease, liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia (including Gilbert syndrome), cholestasis and hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma. These come from a research database and must be checked against the official SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions:** 296 interactions are recorded. Those with a known severity grade include the following.
  - Moderate: mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine, sulfasalazine and naltrexone.
  - Minor: levofloxacin.
  - Many others have an unspecified severity.
- The literature also describes allopurinol altering mercaptopurine metabolite levels, so co-prescription needs review.

For label warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence for myeloid leukaemia is moderate (L2) but indirect. Most trials are multi-agent regimens in which mercaptopurine's contribution is unclear, and the AML-specific literature consists of small cohorts, pilot studies and older reports. The only registered studies that name mercaptopurine directly are one APL maintenance trial and two small early-phase studies. The listed EU product is also cancelled. By contrast, the predictions for ALL and precursor lymphoblastic lymphoma/leukaemia are L1 and are effectively the existing approved indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank to support the mechanistic link analysis.
- The SmPC safety information (warnings and contraindications), which is currently missing and blocks safety screening.
- Trial data that isolate mercaptopurine's contribution in AML or APL, for example the results and arm details of NCT00003934 and the ongoing NCT06199557.
- A prospective or randomized comparison of mercaptopurine-based maintenance in AML.
- Confirmation of current EU product availability.
- A TPMT/NUDT15 genotyping, blood count, liver monitoring and drug interaction plan.
