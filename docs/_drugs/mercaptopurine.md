## One-Sentence Summary

Mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite chemotherapy drug that is already marketed in the EU.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **Myeloid leukemia**.
The data compiled for this report include **15 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, but only a few of them test mercaptopurine directly in myeloid disease. Those few are observational or pilot studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the compiled data. Mercaptopurine belongs to the purine antimetabolite class. Its thioguanine nucleotide metabolites are incorporated into DNA and RNA and block de novo purine synthesis. This is toxic to rapidly dividing cells such as leukaemic blasts, which are also found in myeloid leukaemias.

The evidence for myeloid disease comes mostly from two uses:
- Mercaptopurine as one component of multi-drug regimens.
- Oral maintenance with mercaptopurine plus methotrexate.

A Korean cohort studied maintenance in patients with acute myeloid leukaemia who were ineligible for transplantation. A small Indian prospective pilot studied metronomic oral mercaptopurine in elderly patients. The mercaptopurine contribution inside multi-agent regimens is hard to separate, so the prediction is plausible but not yet shown to be independent of the other drugs.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L3. Direct support for myeloid leukaemia is limited to a cohort study and a small prospective pilot of oral mercaptopurine maintenance in AML (PMID 26425037, 27275453). No randomized trial tests mercaptopurine itself. In the larger Phase 3 trials, mercaptopurine's contribution is unclear or confined to maintenance. The model score is very high (0.9994), but the clinical case is not yet strong enough to advance.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Trials or analyses that isolate mercaptopurine's contribution in myeloid leukaemia, particularly maintenance, such as a randomized comparison.
- Review of the Phase 1/2 trials NCT06199557 and NCT05506332 as they report.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data.
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC.
- A safety monitoring plan, including TPMT/NUDT15 testing and blood count monitoring.
