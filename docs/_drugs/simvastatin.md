# Simvastatin: From Mixed Dyslipidaemia to Familial Hypercholesterolemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Simvastatin is a statin that was originally authorised in the EU, as part of the Cholib combination, for mixed dyslipidaemia in high cardiovascular risk adults.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **familial hypercholesterolemia**, with **9 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
This is closer to an established lipid-lowering use than to true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Simvastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis. This reduces hepatic cholesterol production and upregulates LDL receptors, which lowers LDL-C. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description reflects the well-established statin class mechanism.

The original indication (mixed dyslipidaemia) and familial hypercholesterolemia are both disorders of lipid metabolism. Familial hypercholesterolemia is caused by impaired LDL clearance, which is the pathway statins target. The prediction is therefore mechanistically straightforward. Benefit is expected to be limited in homozygous FH with absent LDL receptor function.

Other predictions in the list vary widely in support. Autosomal dominant hypercholesterolemia overlaps heavily with heterozygous FH and carries the same evidence. By contrast, brain stem infarction, CETP deficiency, ABri amyloidosis, and several others rest on model prediction only (L5) and should be held. HIV infection has mostly interaction and safety literature, and one pilot study found no anti-HIV activity for simvastatin.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, myopathy and other muscle diseases, rhabdomyolysis, thrombocytopenia, diabetes-related conditions (hyperglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis), gout, peptic ulcer disease, blood coagulation disease, and several coronary or myocardial infarction conditions. These should be verified against the official product information.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in total. Major-level interactions include **amiodarone**, **amlodipine**, and **fenofibrate**. Moderate interactions include nifedipine, paclitaxel, omeprazole, aprepitant, and ethanol, among others.
- **HIV-related caution**: In HIV treatment, protease inhibitors and boosters inhibit CYP3A4 and raise simvastatin exposure, increasing myopathy and rhabdomyolysis risk. A case report documents myopathy with simvastatin and a cobicistat-containing regimen.

Label warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials (including the ENHANCE trial and an adolescent heterozygous FH trial) evaluate simvastatin in FH, and Cochrane reviews and guidelines support statin use. However, this is essentially an established lipid-lowering use, the EU authorisation is cancelled, and ENHANCE did not show an added imaging benefit from ezetimibe over simvastatin alone.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Official SmPC warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A safety plan covering myopathy, hepatic risk, and interactions (especially amiodarone, amlodipine, fenofibrate, and CYP3A4 inhibitors)
- Clarification of dosing and efficacy in homozygous FH, where benefit is limited
- Confirmation of current EU regulatory status, since the only licence found is cancelled
