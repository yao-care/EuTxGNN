## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this Evidence Pack. Based on known pharmacology, Simvastatin is a member of the statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) class. It inhibits the rate-limiting enzyme in hepatic cholesterol biosynthesis, which upregulates LDL receptor expression on hepatocytes and accelerates clearance of LDL cholesterol from the circulation. Its efficacy in reducing LDL-C in general hypercholesterolemia is well established.

Familial hypercholesterolemia is a genetic disorder in which reduced or dysfunctional LDL receptors lead to markedly elevated LDL-C from birth. Because statins act by upregulating the *remaining* functional LDL receptors, they remain pharmacologically relevant even in heterozygous FH, where receptor function is only partially impaired — this is why simvastatin (and other statins) appear repeatedly as **background/backbone therapy** in nearly every major FH trial identified in the evidence, including trials of add-on agents such as ezetimibe (ENHANCE trial) and alirocumab (PCSK9 inhibitor programs).

In effect, the TxGNN prediction reflects a mechanistic relationship that is already reflected in real-world clinical practice: statins including simvastatin are long-standing first-line/background therapy for FH, even though this Evidence Pack does not show a corresponding formal EU marketing authorization or local labeling data for that specific indication.
