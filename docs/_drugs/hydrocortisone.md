## Hydrocortisone: From Adrenal Insufficiency to Alopecia Areata
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid originally authorized in the EU for replacement therapy in adrenal insufficiency and for congenital adrenal hyperplasia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**, with **1 clinical trial** (in which hydrocortisone served only as the weak comparator) and **20 publications**, most of them old or low-quality case reports, currently linked to this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid. Its efficacy in adrenal insufficiency, as replacement therapy, is established. Glucocorticoids are also known to have anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease in which the hair follicle is attacked, so suppressing local immune activity is mechanistically plausible. Topical and intralesional corticosteroids are an established treatment class for this condition.

However, the direct evidence supports class plausibility more than hydrocortisone efficacy. In the Phase 3 trial, hydrocortisone 1% was the low-potency comparator, not the intended active therapy, and the results suggest it is less effective than a potent corticosteroid (clobetasol). The prediction score is high, but it is a model output and not proof of benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only controlled evidence used hydrocortisone 1% as the weak comparator, and it appears inferior to a potent corticosteroid. The remaining literature is mostly historical case reports. The data support the class plausibility of corticosteroids in alopecia areata, but not hydrocortisone specifically. All three EU authorizations are valid, and the dosage forms are not specified.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The full results of NCT01453686 and PMID 24226568, to quantify hydrocortisone's own response
- Mechanism of action data
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- A comparison against other corticosteroids and approved alopecia areata treatments
- Route and formulation compatibility assessment (topical or intralesional use is not covered by the cancelled EU products)
