## One-Sentence Summary

Hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid with anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **alopecia areata**, and the data compiled for this report include **1 Phase 3 clinical trial** and several publications, one of them a randomized trial, in which hydrocortisone 1% cream served as the low-potency comparator. Evidence level is **L2**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid that suppresses inflammation and immune activation.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks the hair follicle and causes patchy, non-scarring hair loss. Dampening local immune activity is therefore mechanistically plausible, and topical and intralesional corticosteroids are an established treatment class for this condition.

An important caveat: in the one randomized trial, hydrocortisone 1% was the weak-potency comparator rather than the intended active therapy, and the results suggest it is less effective than a potent corticosteroid (clobetasol). The direct evidence supports the plausibility of the corticosteroid class more than the efficacy of hydrocortisone itself.

## Safety Considerations

Many others are rated Moderate, including zidovudine, isotretinoin, acarbose, acebutolol, acetazolamide, ketorolac, nifedipine, ibuprofen, ethinylestradiol, aldesleukin, alefacept and alemtuzumab. ## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed randomized Phase 3 trial, together with a published RCT and older case series of intralesional hydrocortisone, supports corticosteroid use in alopecia areata (evidence level L2). Hydrocortisone served only as the weak comparator and appeared less effective than a potent corticosteroid, so its own efficacy is not established.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Studies in which hydrocortisone is the intended active treatment, ideally against placebo or other corticosteroids
- Choice of route and formulation (topical or intralesional) and dose
- Review of the SmPC warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data
