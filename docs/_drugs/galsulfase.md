## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general knowledge, galsulfase is a recombinant N-acetylgalactosamine-4-sulfatase (ARSB). It is given intravenously to replace the enzyme that is deficient in MPS VI, so that dermatan sulfate and chondroitin-4-sulfate can be broken down in lysosomes.

The predicted condition is a rare congenital ocular and neurodevelopmental syndrome with no known glycosaminoglycan storage pathology. No mechanistic link to MPS VI was identified. The high score (0.979) is most likely driven by shared ophthalmic phenotype features in the knowledge graph rather than biology relevant to the drug.

Other top-ranked predictions show the same pattern. They are mostly congenital eyelid, ocular-muscle and cranial-nerve disorders: camptodactyly-myopia-medial rectus fibrosis syndrome, congenital Horner syndrome, ptosis-vocal cord paralysis syndrome, jaw-winking syndrome, congenital entropion, epiblepharon and congenital ectropion. None has a supporting mechanism, trial or publication. Scheie syndrome (ranked 10th, score 94.79%) is biologically closer, as it is another mucopolysaccharidosis. However, it is caused by alpha-L-iduronidase (IDUA) deficiency, an enzyme that acts upstream of ARSB, so galsulfase would not correct the primary defect. Laronidase is the established therapy for it.
