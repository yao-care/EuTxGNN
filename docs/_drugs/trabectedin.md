## Trabectedin: From Soft-Tissue Sarcoma to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Trabectedin is a marine-derived cytotoxic agent, originally used for advanced soft-tissue sarcoma and, in combination with pegylated liposomal doxorubicin, for relapsed platinum-sensitive ovarian cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the compiled drug record. From the published literature, trabectedin binds the DNA minor groove, causes DNA damage, and interferes with transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair. It also modulates the tumour microenvironment by reducing tumour-associated macrophages.

Tumours with BRCA deficiency or homologous recombination defects appear particularly sensitive to trabectedin. This is relevant to breast cancer, where germline BRCA1/2 mutations occur. Trabectedin is already used in ovarian cancer, which shares a BRCA-related biology with some breast cancers. Preclinical work also suggests that trabectedin may enhance the effect of IL-12 immunotherapy in triple-negative breast cancer.

The link remains plausible rather than proven. Clinical activity in breast cancer has been explored mainly in Phase 2 studies, and no registered efficacy trial is included in the data compiled for this report.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 potential interactions, of which the first 20 are available here. Most are Moderate. One is Major: **clarithromycin**. Other Moderate examples include dexamethasone, aprepitant, cimetidine, metronidazole, levofloxacin, simvastatin and rosuvastatin. Concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers should be reviewed against the SmPC.
- **QT interval**: A dedicated QT study (NCT00786838; published as PMID 21739119) evaluated the effect of trabectedin on cardiac repolarisation in patients with advanced solid tumours.

Label warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the SmPC for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The biological rationale is credible (DNA-repair interference, BRCA-deficiency sensitivity), and Phase 2 data exist in advanced breast cancer, so the evidence level is L2. However, the only registered trial is a safety study, there is no completed Phase 3 evidence, and both EU authorizations are cancelled. Label safety data are also missing, so the safety review cannot yet proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Phase 2 results (PMIDs 25239225, 24692579, 27266804) to judge response rates and benefit in breast cancer, especially in BRCA-mutated and XPG-stratified subgroups
- SmPC warnings and contraindications from the EMA product information, which are currently missing
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of the cancelled EU status, including whether any product remains available
- A defined target population (for example BRCA-mutated or triple-negative breast cancer) and a plan for a controlled efficacy trial
- A safety monitoring plan covering myelosuppression, liver toxicity, and interactions with CYP3A4 modulators
