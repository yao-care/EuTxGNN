## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, secukinumab is a monoclonal antibody that inhibits interleukin-17A (IL-17A). Its efficacy in plaque psoriasis, hidradenitis suppurativa, psoriatic arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis and juvenile idiopathic arthritis is well established.

For primary release disorder of platelets, however, no clear mechanistic link was found. Platelet secretion defects are not known to be driven by IL-17A signalling. The high TxGNN score (0.98) is a computational prediction only, with no supporting trials or literature. The same applies to the other top-ranked predictions: the next two (pseudo-von Willebrand disease and Glanzmann thrombasthenia) are inherited platelet disorders unrelated to IL-17A, and the breast cancer predictions have only indirect, preclinical discussion of IL-17 in the tumour microenvironment. Drug-induced osteoporosis has a weak biological rationale, since IL-17A contributes to inflammatory bone loss, but no clinical evidence was provided.

The high score should therefore be read as a hypothesis for further investigation, not as evidence of benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query completed with 100 recorded interactions. The only Major-level interaction among those listed is **adalimumab**, so combining secukinumab with another immunomodulatory biologic needs careful review. Moderate-level interactions include simvastatin, warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dicoumarol, tacrolimus, theophylline, aminophylline, oxtriphylline, roflumilast, lidocaine, nifedipine, hydrocodone, fentanyl and a tetanus toxoid vaccine. Zinc salts (sulfate, acetate, gluconate, chloride) are Minor.
- **Related literature note**: A review on IL-17 blockers describes paradoxical gastrointestinal effects, with disease exacerbation in Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, which is relevant to any new-use safety planning.

Please refer to the SmPC for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by the TxGNN model score. There are no registered trials, no relevant publications and no plausible link between IL-17A blockade and platelet release disorders. The EU authorization is also listed as cancelled, which adds uncertainty about the product's current availability.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a mechanistic rationale connecting IL-17A inhibition to platelet granule secretion
- Preclinical or in vitro evidence in platelet function models
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, which were not available for this report
- Clarification of the current EU authorization status
