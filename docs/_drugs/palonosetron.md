## Palonosetron: Migraine disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Palonosetron is a 5-HT3 receptor antagonist originally used to prevent nausea and vomiting caused by cancer chemotherapy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a case report) on this direction, and that report describes palonosetron *causing* migraine-type headache rather than treating it.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, palonosetron belongs to the 5-HT3 receptor antagonist class. Its efficacy in chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting is established, and the 5-HT3 receptor is a plausible, though unproven, participant in trigeminovascular signalling relevant to migraine.

The high TxGNN score (0.9974) comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. It should not be read as clinical evidence. Nausea and vomiting are also core migraine symptoms, which may partly explain the learned association, but symptom control is different from treating the underlying disorder.

The only literature linked to this prediction points the opposite way. The case report (PMID 21132477) describes migraine-type headache induced by palonosetron, and headache is a recognised adverse effect of this drug class. No evidence shows that palonosetron prevents or treats migraine.

Other predicted indications in the ranking (migraine with brainstem aura, migraine susceptibility, headache disorder, trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia, glaucoma, sciatic neuropathy, and rare skin conditions) are also supported by no clinical evidence, or only by safety-related papers.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified in total. Examples of those rated **Major** include:
  - Opioids and analgesics: fentanyl, alfentanil, tramadol
  - Serotonergic agents: dextromethorphan, almotriptan, sumatriptan
  - QT-prolonging or cardiac agents: amiodarone, amisulpride, amitriptyline, anagrelide
- Examples rated **Moderate** include abiraterone, dihydrocodeine, codeine, famotidine, formoterol, adenosine, salbutamol, propofol, loperamide and alfuzosin.
- **Headache** is a known adverse effect of palonosetron and of the 5-HT3 antagonist class, which is directly relevant to any use in headache disorders.
- A 2026 review (PMID 42748473) discusses QT prolongation with antiemetics, and this should be considered given the number of QT-related interactions.

Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score. The single linked publication is a case report of palonosetron causing migraine-type headache, which points toward harm rather than benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (MOA) and a mechanistic analysis of 5-HT3 involvement in migraine
- Preclinical or early clinical studies showing a therapeutic effect in migraine
- A review of whether palonosetron-associated headache outweighs any possible benefit
- Safety information from the SmPC (warnings and contraindications) for safety screening
- Clarification of EU regulatory status, since both authorizations are cancelled, and of route and formulation compatibility
