## Afatinib: From EGFR-Mutated Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer to HER2-Positive Breast Carcinoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Afatinib is an oral, irreversible ErbB-family blocker, originally authorized in the EU for EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **HER2-positive breast carcinoma**,
with **4 clinical trials** (3 completed Phase 2 studies) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Phase 2 efficacy was modest and no Phase 3 evidence is included in the data compiled for this report.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the literature, afatinib is an irreversible ErbB-family blocker that inhibits EGFR (HER1), HER2 and HER4. Its efficacy in EGFR-mutated NSCLC is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to HER2-driven breast cancer.

In HER2-positive breast cancer, HER2 is the oncogenic driver, so the target rationale is direct. Resistance to trastuzumab may be mediated by other ErbB-family members, which is the rationale for broader pan-ErbB blockade. Phase 2 data exist for afatinib alone, with vinorelbine or paclitaxel, and combined with trastuzumab. Efficacy was modest and limited by diarrhea and rash. Afatinib has not shown superiority over established HER2 therapies (trastuzumab, T-DM1, T-DXd).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
Mechanistic rationale is direct and three Phase 2 trials have been completed, but efficacy was modest, afatinib has not shown superiority over established HER2 therapies, and no Phase 3 evidence is included in the data compiled for this report. The only EU authorization is cancelled, and the safety label data are missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Review of the LUX-Breast 1, 2 and 3 results to define whether any subgroup (for example, patients with brain metastases or after trastuzumab failure) benefits
- A comparison against current standards of care (trastuzumab, T-DM1, T-DXd)
- A tolerability and diarrhea/rash management plan, plus a review of the interaction list for combination regimens
