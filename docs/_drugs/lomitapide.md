# Lomitapide: From Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia to Macrothrombocytopenia with Mitral Valve Insufficiency

## One-Sentence Summary

Lomitapide is an oral lipid-lowering drug, originally used as an add-on treatment for adult patients with homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Lomitapide inhibits microsomal triglyceride transfer protein (MTP) in liver and intestinal cells. This blocks the assembly and secretion of apoB-containing lipoproteins (VLDL and chylomicrons) and lowers LDL cholesterol. This mechanism is well established for HoFH.

For the predicted indication, **no plausible mechanistic link was identified**. Lomitapide has no known role in platelet biology or mitral valve disease. The very high score (0.999) most likely reflects closeness within the knowledge graph, shared with several related platelet disorders. It should not be read as biological support.

The same pattern holds for the other top-ranked predictions (hereditary thrombocytopenia, dense granule disease, transient neonatal thrombocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, primary platelet release disorder, pseudo-von Willebrand disease, Glanzmann thrombasthenia and platelet storage pool deficiency). All are L5, with no trials or literature and no mechanistic rationale.

One prediction in the list, **hyperlipoproteinemia** (rank 9, score 99.74%), is different. It is essentially a re-discovery of the approved use rather than true repurposing. It is supported by several completed Phase 3 studies in HoFH, including the pivotal study NCT00730236 (n=29), a long-term extension NCT00943306 (n=19), a Japanese study NCT02173158 (n=9) and a paediatric study NCT04681170 (n=46). Recent publications include the paediatric Phase 3 efficacy results (Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol, 2024) and the 2023 European Atherosclerosis Society consensus update on HoFH (Eur Heart J). This confirms the original indication and does not support the top-ranked prediction.

**Decision: Hold**

The top-ranked prediction rests only on a graph-based model score. It has no clinical trials, no literature and no plausible mechanistic link to MTP inhibition. The EU authorization is also cancelled. The only well-supported prediction in the list (hyperlipoproteinemia) is the existing HoFH indication, not new repurposing.
