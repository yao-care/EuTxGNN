# Toripalimab: From Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma to Mixed-Type Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Toripalimab is a PD-1 blocking antibody, originally used for first-line treatment of nasopharyngeal carcinoma and oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **mixed-type autoimmune hemolytic anemia**, but **no clinical trials and no related publications** support this prediction, and the known biology points in the opposite direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From the pharmacology data, toripalimab is an antibody that blocks PD-1 (CD279) from binding its ligands PD-L1 and PD-L2, with similar potency (IC50 about 3.0 nM). This releases the brake on T cells and strengthens anti-tumour immunity.

The TxGNN score is high (0.938), but it comes from a network-based prediction only. The mechanism argues against the prediction. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia is driven by antibodies attacking red blood cells, so further immune activation through PD-1 blockade would be expected to worsen it, not treat it. Immune checkpoint inhibitors are in fact reported to cause autoimmune hemolytic anemia as an immune-related adverse event. The predicted direction of benefit is therefore biologically implausible.

The other top-ranked predictions show the same pattern. Idiopathic aplastic anemia (T-cell-mediated marrow failure), drug-induced and neonatal autoimmune hemolytic anemia, and amyopathic dermatomyositis are all immune-mediated conditions that PD-1 blockade could aggravate. Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria and primary CD59 deficiency are complement-driven disorders with no link to the PD-1 pathway. For dermatitis and proteinuria, the published reports describe adverse events, not treatment benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Immune-related adverse events**: Published reports link PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, including toripalimab, to severe skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis) and lichenoid drug eruption. Immune checkpoint inhibitors have also been reported to cause autoimmune hemolytic anemia, immune-mediated marrow toxicity, and myositis or dermatomyositis-like syndromes. These adverse events overlap directly with several predicted indications.
- **Combination-related findings**: Proteinuria in the retrieved oncology studies is likely linked mainly to VEGF-pathway partner drugs (axitinib, bevacizumab, lucitanib, pazopanib).
- **Neonatal population**: No pediatric or neonatal data are available for toripalimab.

For the full label warnings, contraindications and interactions, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a network score alone, with no trials or publications, and the mechanism and adverse-event data point to possible harm rather than benefit. The EU authorization is also cancelled.

- Label warnings and contraindications (SmPC) for formal safety screening

- Clarification of the EU authorization status
