# Travoprost: From Ocular Hypertension and Open-Angle Glaucoma to Visceral Calciphylaxis

## One-Sentence Summary

Travoprost is a topical prostaglandin F2-alpha analogue originally used to lower elevated intraocular pressure in ocular hypertension and open-angle glaucoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **visceral calciphylaxis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this specific prediction.
The prediction rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Travoprost is known to be a prostaglandin F2-alpha analogue (FP receptor agonist) given as an eye drop, and it lowers eye pressure by increasing fluid outflow from the eye. Its efficacy in glaucoma is well established.

No plausible link between this mechanism and the vascular calcification pathways of visceral calciphylaxis is documented in the data. Eye drops also give very low systemic exposure, so reaching the vascular beds involved at effective concentrations is doubtful. The high TxGNN score reflects a pattern found by the model in its knowledge graph, not confirmed biology.

Other top-ranked predictions (thoracic outlet syndromes, angiodysplasia of stomach, blue toe syndrome, spontaneous coronary artery dissection, lymphangiectasis, hemangioendothelioma) are likewise supported by prediction only. The only evidence found for any prediction is a set of ophthalmic studies and one case report linked to the "vascular disease" and "hemangioendothelioma" predictions, discussed below.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level contraindication signals (knowledge graph, not label text)**: uveitis, iris disease, ciliary body disease, panuveitis, iritis, cystoid macular edema, macular retinal edema.
- **Drug Interactions**: 49 potential interactions were found in the DDI database, all with an unknown severity level. Examples include calcitriol, pantoprazole, sucralfate, morphine, metformin, omeprazole, rosiglitazone, prednisone, simvastatin and acetylsalicylic acid. Their clinical significance is not established.
- **Case-report signal**: uveal effusion has been reported with topical travoprost in a patient with an abnormal vascular malformation (Sturge-Weber-Krabbe syndrome), suggesting caution in patients with abnormal ocular vasculature.
- **Common ocular effect**: conjunctival hyperemia is frequently reported with travoprost.

Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for visceral calciphylaxis is supported only by the model score (L5), with no trials or publications, no documented mechanistic link, and minimal systemic exposure from the eye-drop formulation. The ophthalmic evidence found supports only the original glaucoma indication. In addition, both EU authorizations are cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and a mechanistic rationale connecting FP receptor agonism to calcification or vascular pathways
- Preclinical studies (for example, in models of vascular calcification) testing relevance
- Evaluation of route and formulation, since a topical ophthalmic product is unlikely to reach the target tissues
- Safety information from the SmPC, which is currently missing
- A review of whether any other lower-ranked prediction has a more plausible mechanism
