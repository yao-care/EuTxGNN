# Trastuzumab deruxtecan: From HER2-Positive Breast Cancer to Drug-Induced Osteoporosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Trastuzumab deruxtecan is a HER2-directed antibody-drug conjugate, originally used to treat HER2-positive and HER2-low breast cancer, HER2-mutant non-small cell lung cancer and HER2-positive gastric cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **drug-induced osteoporosis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, so it is a computational signal only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, trastuzumab deruxtecan is an antibody-drug conjugate. A HER2-targeting antibody delivers a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload (deruxtecan) into HER2-expressing tumour cells. Its efficacy in HER2-positive and HER2-low cancers has been established.

No plausible mechanistic link to drug-induced osteoporosis was identified. This is a non-malignant bone disease, and it is not driven by HER2 biology. The high TxGNN score is a computational output of the knowledge graph and should not be read as evidence of efficacy. Giving a cytotoxic agent to treat a chronic metabolic bone condition would also raise obvious safety concerns.

Other predictions for this drug have the same weakness. They include diabetic retinopathy, bronchitis, diabetic cataract, benign lesions and several tumour types without an established HER2 role. Bronchitis is a particular concern because interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis is a known serious toxicity of this drug.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query found 42 interacting drugs. Examples of Major interactions include deferiprone, samarium (153Sm) lexidronam, adalimumab, baricitinib, certolizumab pegol, cladribine, leflunomide and natalizumab. Examples of Moderate interactions include palifermin, strontium chloride Sr-89, roflumilast, chloramphenicol, alefacept, alemtuzumab, anakinra, azathioprine, canakinumab, dimethyl fumarate and niraparib. Many of these are immunosuppressive or myelosuppressive agents.
- **Reported adverse events in the literature**: Interstitial lung disease, reversible Fanconi syndrome and a possible association with dermatomyositis have been described in case reports.

Please refer to the SmPC for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score. There are no clinical trials or publications, and no plausible mechanism links a HER2-directed cytotoxic ADC to drug-induced osteoporosis. The EU license is also cancelled, and the safety profile is substantial.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A mechanistic rationale and preclinical evidence linking the drug to bone metabolism
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Label warnings and contraindications (SmPC) for a formal safety screen
- Clarification of the cancelled EU license status
