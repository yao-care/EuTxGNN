# Eptinezumab: From Migraine Prophylaxis to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Eptinezumab (Vyepti) is an anti-CGRP monoclonal antibody, originally used to prevent migraine in adults with at least 4 migraine days per month.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **indirect literature support** (10 publications, mostly reviews, case reports and subgroup analyses).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, eptinezumab is an anti-CGRP (calcitonin gene-related peptide) monoclonal antibody. CGRP is a key signaling molecule in migraine, and blocking it is effective for migraine prevention.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a migraine subtype that shares the underlying pathophysiology of the approved indication, so a mechanistic overlap is plausible. Indirect support includes a post hoc analysis of two pivotal trials (PROMISE-1 and PROMISE-2) in patients with self-reported aura, case reports and a review of anti-CGRP antibodies in aura, and a report of genetic migraine disorders that responded to CGRP antagonists.

There are important limits. A randomized trial found that PACAP38-induced migraine attacks are independent of CGRP signaling, which suggests CGRP blockade may not cover all migraine pathways. No study has directly targeted brainstem aura, and the aura evidence is based on self-reported aura in general migraine populations. The very high TxGNN score is consistent with the overlap with the approved migraine indication but is not clinical proof.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: No interaction records were found in the data compiled for this report.
- **Emerging safety signals (class-level, from recent cohort studies)**: Studies of CGRP inhibitors have examined glaucoma risk and cerebrovascular risk (ischemic stroke/TIA) in people with migraine. These are observational safety questions that deserve attention when considering use in patients with aura, and they are not a basis for conclusions here.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is mechanistically plausible, because eptinezumab is already approved for migraine prevention and aura subgroup data exist. However, there are no trials directly targeting brainstem aura, the evidence is indirect, and the only EU authorization is cancelled. Safety review cannot proceed without the label warnings and contraindications.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Warnings and contraindications from the EMA product information, which is a blocking gap for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Clarification of why the EU authorization was cancelled and whether the product is currently available
- Evidence specific to brainstem aura, such as a dedicated subgroup analysis, registry data or a pilot trial
- Review of cerebrovascular and other class-level safety signals in patients with aura, particularly given the brainstem involvement
