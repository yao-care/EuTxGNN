Spironolactone is a well-established aldosterone (mineralocorticoid) receptor antagonist, classically used as a potassium-sparing diuretic for hypertension, oedema, and heart failure. ## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed structured mechanism-of-action data for spironolactone is currently marked as a data gap in this record. Based on well-established pharmacology, spironolactone is a synthetic aldosterone receptor antagonist that also exhibits meaningful anti-androgenic activity — it blocks androgen receptors and reduces androgen synthesis. This dual hormonal action is the basis for its long-standing off-label use in dermatology for androgen-driven conditions such as acne, hirsutism, and hair loss.

Androgenetic alopecia (AGA), also known as female pattern hair loss, is directly driven by androgen (dihydrotestosterone) signalling at the hair follicle, causing progressive miniaturisation of terminal hairs into vellus hairs. Because spironolactone antagonises this signalling pathway, it targets the pathology of AGA directly — a mechanistic fit that is notably stronger than several other TxGNN-predicted candidates in this pack (e.g., hypotrichosis simplex, Braddock syndrome), which involve non-androgenic genetic mechanisms with no plausible pharmacological link to spironolactone.

This mechanistic plausibility is reinforced by real-world clinical practice: spironolactone has been used off-label by dermatologists for decades as anti-androgen therapy for female AGA. The repurposing candidate is further supported by a completed Phase 2 RCT directly comparing spironolactone to topical minoxidil, a Cochrane systematic review on interventions for female pattern hair loss, and multiple systematic reviews — including one in the *Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology* — specifically evaluating its efficacy and safety in this indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 2 RCT directly testing spironolactone against topical minoxidil in female pattern hair loss, combined with a Cochrane systematic review and multiple dermatology-society-level systematic reviews, establishes a credible evidence base (L2) and a clear, well-understood mechanistic rationale (androgen receptor antagonism). However, no large confirmatory Phase 3 RCT specific to this drug-indication pairing exists, and formal regulatory safety documentation is currently unavailable.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/EMA-labelled warnings, contraindications, and drug-drug interaction data (currently a Blocking data gap, DG001)
- Confirmed DrugBank mechanism-of-action record (High-priority data gap, DG002)
- Verification of actual EU marketing status via national (non-centralised) authorisation registries, since this dataset only captures centrally-authorised EMA products
- A larger, adequately powered confirmatory trial specifically evaluating spironolactone for AGA, given the existing Phase 2 RCT's small sample size (n=40)
- Route/dosage-form compatibility assessment for the alopecia indication (currently marked pending)
