# Spironolactone: From Refractory Oedema and Hypertension to Alopecia

## One-Sentence Summary

Spironolactone is an aldosterone receptor antagonist, originally authorised for refractory oedema (heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome), primary aldosteronism and essential hypertension.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia** (the highest-ranked prediction with meaningful supporting evidence), with **2 clinical trials** (1 completed, 1 withdrawn) and **20 publications** currently linked to this direction.
The two top-scoring predictions (hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp and congenital hypotrichosis milia) have no supporting evidence at all, so this report focuses on alopecia.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, spironolactone blocks the mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) receptor, which explains its use in oedema and hypertension. It also has anti-androgen activity: it antagonises the androgen receptor and weakly inhibits androgen synthesis.

This second property links the original indications to hair loss. Androgenetic alopecia (female pattern hair loss) is driven by excessive follicular sensitivity to androgens, which gradually shrinks terminal scalp hair into fine vellus hair. Reducing androgen signalling in the follicle is therefore a plausible way to slow or reverse this process. A Phase 2 trial compared spironolactone with topical minoxidil in female pattern hair loss, and a systematic review has examined oral and topical spironolactone in androgenetic alopecia.

The evidence applies mainly to androgenetic alopecia in women. It should not be extended to other types of alopecia, such as autoimmune alopecia areata, or to rare hereditary hypotrichosis, where a link to androgen signalling is speculative. Use in men is limited by feminising effects.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged by the knowledge graph** (derived from drug-disease relationships, not from label text): kidney disease, anuria, potassium deficiency disease, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperuricaemia, gout, hyperparathyroidism, osteitis fibrosa, hypercalcaemia, nephrocalcinosis, phosphorus metabolism disease, hepatic coma, hepatic encephalopathy, pancreatitis, pyoureter, systemic lupus erythematosus (including paediatric), neonatal jaundice.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. One is rated Major (loperamide). Many are Moderate, including corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin), metformin, bupropion, morphine, exenatide, bisacodyl, castor oil and dronabinol. Minor interactions include doxycycline, acetylsalicylic acid and fidaxomicin.
- **Risks specific to the hair-loss use** (from the evidence review): hyperkalaemia, menstrual irregularity, teratogenicity (risk of feminisation of a male fetus), and feminising effects in men. A published case report describes drug-induced hepatitis in a woman treated for androgenetic alopecia.

Label warnings from the package insert were not available. Please refer to the SmPC for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Alopecia, specifically androgenetic alopecia in women, has a plausible anti-androgen mechanism, a completed Phase 2 trial against minoxidil, and several systematic reviews addressing spironolactone. The evidence is still modest (one small trial of 40 participants, results not confirmed) and the use is off-label. The two higher-scoring predictions (hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp and congenital hypotrichosis milia) and other predictions (alopecia areata, malignant hypertension types, pulmonary hypertension, Braddock syndrome) lack supporting evidence and should remain on Hold. Chronic pulmonary heart disease (L3, one small Phase 4 trial with 15 participants) is a research question rather than a candidate for action.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of NCT00175617 and a review of the spironolactone-specific systematic review (PMID 36923692) to confirm efficacy
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- A defined target population (women with androgenetic alopecia) with exclusion of pregnancy and of patients with the flagged contraindications
- A monitoring plan for potassium and renal function, and a contraception requirement
- Confirmation of current EU availability, since the only authorisation is cancelled
