## Ketorolac: From Intraocular Surgery Support to Headache Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ketorolac is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID). Its only EU authorization (as Omidria) was for maintaining intraoperative mydriasis, preventing intraoperative miosis and reducing acute postoperative ocular pain in intraocular lens replacement surgery.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **headache disorder**, with **17 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, ketorolac is a non-selective COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor that reduces prostaglandin synthesis. Its use in ocular surgery relies on this anti-inflammatory and analgesic effect.

Prostaglandins contribute to neurogenic inflammation, trigeminovascular sensitization and pain signalling in migraine and tension-type headache. The analgesic effect proven for postoperative ocular pain is therefore mechanistically plausible for headache. Guideline-level and meta-analytic evidence already exists for acute migraine treatment in the emergency department.

Note that the original indication is a topical ocular use, whereas the headache evidence is almost entirely for parenteral (IV/IM) or intranasal ketorolac. The route of administration differs, and the EU authorization is cancelled, so any development would need a suitable formulation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug-disease contraindication signals** (from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text): hypertension, gastric and peptic ulcer disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, asthma, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, blood coagulation disorders and hemophilia, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, myocardial infarction and other cardiovascular or heart disease, anaemia and haemoglobinopathy, impaired renal function, and dry eye syndrome.
- **Drug interactions** (100 recorded; examples):
  - **Major:** acetylsalicylic acid.
  - **Moderate:** corticosteroids (betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone), sulfonylureas (glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, chlorpropamide, acetohexamide), exenatide, levofloxacin, kanamycin and balsalazide.
  - **Minor:** H2 blockers (famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine) and linaclotide.
- **Case-report signals:** ketorolac-induced lithium toxicity (reported in a cluster headache patient) and rare severe hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis.

Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Headache disorder, mainly acute migraine and tension-type headache, has the strongest support among the predictions. Several completed Phase 3/4 trials, systematic reviews and guideline-level assessments exist. However, the trials are mostly small, and the evidence concerns parenteral or intranasal use rather than the original ophthalmic indication. The EU authorization is cancelled, and NSAID class risks are significant.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, and mechanism of action data
- Selection of a route and formulation, since the cancelled ophthalmic product does not apply to headache
- Guardrails: short-course limits, and avoidance in patients with GI bleeding or ulcer risk, renal impairment, cardiovascular disease, asthma or bleeding disorders
- Confirmation of the current regulatory status of systemic ketorolac products in the EU

**Other predicted indications (for reference):**
- **Viral conjunctivitis** (Phase 2/3 trial and several small RCTs; symptom relief only, mixed benefit): a research question.
- **Trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia:** indirect evidence only; the sole trial was withdrawn. Hold.
- **Rosacea conjunctivitis, nasopharyngitis, cap polyposis, cutaneous photosensitivity-lethal colitis syndrome:** model prediction only. Hold.
- **Ulcerative proctosigmoiditis and undetermined colitis:** NSAIDs may worsen inflammatory bowel disease, so use is not supported. Hold.
- **Allergic urticaria:** the literature describes ketorolac as a cause of hypersensitivity, so the prediction is likely an adverse-event association and a contraindication signal. Hold.
