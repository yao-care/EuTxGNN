## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Ketorolac is a non-selective COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor, a class that reduces prostaglandin synthesis. Prostaglandins contribute to neurogenic inflammation, trigeminovascular sensitization and pain signalling in migraine and tension-type headache, so analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects in headache are mechanistically plausible.

The clinical literature supports this link. Guideline-level assessments and several systematic reviews and meta-analyses address parenteral ketorolac for acute migraine in the emergency department, and trials have tested it in migraine and tension-type headache, in both adults and children.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Interaction screening returned 100 interacting drugs. The most notable is a **major** interaction with acetylsalicylic acid. Moderate interactions include corticosteroids (betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone), sulfonylureas (acetohexamide, chlorpropamide, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide), exenatide, kanamycin and levofloxacin. Interactions with H2 blockers (famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine) are minor.
- A published case report describes lithium neurotoxicity when ketorolac was co-administered in a patient with cluster headache.
- NSAID class risks (gastrointestinal bleeding, renal injury, cardiovascular events) call for short-course use and avoidance in at-risk patients. Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.
