# Ketorolac: From Acute Pain Management to Headache Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Ketorolac is a potent nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) established for short-term management of moderate-to-severe acute pain, but it currently holds **no marketing authorization on file** in this dataset. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Headache Disorder**, and this direction is already strongly supported by **36 clinical trials** and **19 publications**, including multiple completed Phase 3/4 RCTs and society guidelines on migraine management.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not returned in this evidence pack. Based on well-established pharmacology, Ketorolac is a non-selective cyclooxygenase (COX-1/COX-2) inhibitor that blocks prostaglandin synthesis, producing potent systemic analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects. It is typically administered IV, IM, or intranasally for fast onset in acute, moderate-to-severe pain.

Although no licensed original indication is recorded in this dataset, Ketorolac's global clinical use for acute pain control (post-surgical pain, renal colic, orthopedic trauma) shares a common pharmacological basis with headache/migraine pain: prostaglandin-mediated sensitization of trigeminal nociceptors and neurogenic inflammation are both implicated in migraine and tension-type headache pathophysiology.

This mechanistic overlap explains why Ketorolac has, in practice, already become a first-line parenteral abortive therapy for acute migraine and tension-type headache in emergency department settings for over three decades — a pattern reflected directly in the clinical trial and literature evidence below, which substantially predates and corroborates the TxGNN prediction rather than merely being consistent with it.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The clinical evidence for Ketorolac in headache disorder is strong (L1: multiple completed Phase 3/4 RCTs plus current society guidelines), and this use is already well established in emergency-medicine practice. However, this candidate has **no EU marketing authorization on file** (0 licenses, not marketed) and is missing essential label safety data — a **blocking** gap (DG001) that prevents entry into the S1 safety pre-screen.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Official label warnings/contraindications document (SmPC or equivalent) — resolves blocking gap DG001
- Confirmed mechanism-of-action data via DrugBank API — resolves gap DG002
- Clarification of Ketorolac's EU regulatory status, since no marketing authorization currently exists in this jurisdiction
- A completed drug-drug interaction (DDI) query, as the current query returned no results
