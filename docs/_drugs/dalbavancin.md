# Dalbavancin: From Acute Bacterial Skin Infections to Postinfectious Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Dalbavancin is a lipoglycopeptide antibiotic originally used to treat acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections (ABSSSI).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **postinfectious vasculitis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this direction. The prediction rests on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on its drug class, dalbavancin is a lipoglycopeptide that inhibits cell wall synthesis in Gram-positive bacteria. Its efficacy has been established in ABSSSI.

Postinfectious vasculitis is largely immune-mediated. Antibacterial activity offers no clear mechanistic rationale beyond eradicating a possible triggering Gram-positive infection. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only and should not be read as evidence of efficacy.

Other predictions in the same list are mostly weak. Several involve viruses (ophthalmic herpes zoster, infectious mononucleosis) or a protozoan parasite (Chagas cardiomyopathy), which dalbavancin does not target. Infection-related hemolytic uremic syndrome is typically caused by Gram-negative Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli*, which dalbavancin does not cover, and antibiotics are generally avoided there. The most biologically plausible alternative is **orbital cellulitis** (score 99.02%). It is commonly caused by *S. aureus* (including MRSA) and streptococci, which dalbavancin covers, and it is close to the approved ABSSSI indication. It is flagged as a research question, but it also has no clinical evidence in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no supporting trials or literature (evidence level L5), and the mechanism is weak because postinfectious vasculitis is largely immune-mediated rather than driven by active Gram-positive infection. The product's only EU authorization is also cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for dalbavancin
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, to allow safety screening
- A literature and trial search for postinfectious vasculitis, and a documented link to a Gram-positive trigger
- Consideration of prioritizing orbital cellulitis, with studies of orbital tissue penetration and paediatric use
- Clarification of the regulatory status of the cancelled EU authorization
