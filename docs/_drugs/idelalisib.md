# Idelalisib: From Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia and Follicular Lymphoma to Mantle Cell Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Idelalisib (Zydelig) is an oral PI3Kδ inhibitor, originally authorised in the EU for chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL) and refractory follicular lymphoma (FL).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **mantle cell lymphoma (MCL)**, with **5 clinical trials** (all early-phase or terminated) and **20 publications** (mostly reviews and preclinical work) supporting this direction.
The EU authorisation is now cancelled, and serious toxicities limit any further development.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From the published literature, idelalisib is a selective inhibitor of the delta isoform of PI3K, a key node in B-cell receptor signalling and B-cell survival. Its efficacy in CLL and follicular lymphoma has been shown in clinical studies.

MCL is also a B-cell malignancy that depends on B-cell receptor and PI3Kδ signalling, so the mechanistic link is plausible. Laboratory studies show idelalisib reduces translation-regulatory signalling in MCL cells and acts synergistically with a P300/CBP inhibitor (A-485) and with the CDK4/6 inhibitor palbociclib. Resistance mechanisms, such as CBX5 loss, have also been described, suggesting that MCL can show intrinsic resistance to idelalisib alone.

Clinical signals come from a large Phase 1 combination study (NCT01088048, n=241, including MCL cohorts) and a Phase 1/2 study with lenalidomide (NCT01838434, n=106). A 2014 *Cancer Discovery* report noted activity in heavily pretreated MCL patients. However, mature efficacy and safety data in MCL are lacking.

## Safety Considerations

- **Known toxicities (from the trial and literature data):** serious and sometimes fatal colitis, hepatotoxicity, pneumonitis and infections. An independent monitoring committee observed more deaths and serious adverse events in front-line CLL and early-line indolent NHL when idelalisib was combined with standard therapies, and several Phase 3 trials were terminated. The drug was later withdrawn from the market for its accelerated-approval indication.
- **Drug Interactions:** the interaction screen returned 100 entries. Major-level interactions include loperamide, triamcinolone, budesonide and eliglustat. Moderate-level interactions include acarbose, doxycycline, hydrocortisone, pioglitazone, bupropion, aprepitant, dexamethasone, betamethasone, clarithromycin, saxagliptin and minocycline, among others.

Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link in MCL is plausible and early-phase combination studies exist, but there are no completed late-phase trials in MCL, and the preclinical data point to intrinsic resistance when idelalisib is used alone. The drug carries serious toxicity, its EU authorisation is cancelled, and newer agents are available for B-cell malignancies.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results from the MCL studies (especially NCT01838434 and NCT01088048) to establish efficacy and safety in MCL
- Detailed mechanism of action data and SmPC warnings and contraindications
- A risk-benefit comparison against current MCL options, and a monitoring plan for colitis, hepatotoxicity, pneumonitis and infection
- A review of the other predicted indications: B-cell neoplasm (rank 3) largely overlaps with the existing CLL/FL indications and is not a new repurposing signal; the remaining predictions (Hodgkin lymphoma, myeloid leukaemia, CML, colon adenocarcinoma and others) rest on weak or preclinical evidence only
