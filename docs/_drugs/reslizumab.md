## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Reslizumab is known to be an anti-IL-5 monoclonal antibody. By blocking IL-5 signaling it reduces eosinophil numbers and activity, which is why it works in severe eosinophilic asthma.

The link to immune-mediated thrombocytopenia is weak. This condition is mainly driven by autoantibody-mediated platelet destruction and impaired platelet production, and eosinophil or IL-5 pathways are not an established driver. No trials or publications back the prediction.

The same applies to the other platelet-related predictions, such as primary release disorder of platelets, pseudo-von Willebrand disease, autoimmune thrombocytopenia, Glanzmann thrombasthenia, neonatal thrombocytopenia, and Evans syndrome. All of them are model-only signals with no supporting mechanism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data to assess whether any IL-5/eosinophil role exists in immune thrombocytopenia
- Preclinical or observational evidence linking IL-5 blockade to platelet destruction
- Safety information from the SmPC
- A completed Phase 2 randomized, placebo-controlled pilot ([NCT01111305](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01111305), n=31) tested single-dose reslizumab for reducing eosinophilia after diethylcarbamazine treatment of Loa loa infection. It was published as an RCT ([PMID 32910141](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32910141/), 2021). A related review is also available ([PMID 33326992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33326992/), 2021). The study is small and a pilot, so efficacy as a therapeutic indication is not established.
