# Icosapent Ethyl: From Cardiovascular Risk Reduction to Myocardial Infarction

## One-Sentence Summary

Icosapent ethyl is a highly purified form of eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), originally authorized in the EU to reduce cardiovascular risk as an add-on to statin therapy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Myocardial Infarction**, with **1 registered clinical trial** (withdrawn, no participants enrolled) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction, including several analyses of the large REDUCE-IT trial.

Note: the predicted indication analysed here is the highest-ranked prediction that has real supporting evidence (rank 4). The three top-ranked predictions (hemoglobinopathy, partial deletion of the short arm of chromosome 16, beta-thalassemia with other manifestations) have no clinical evidence and are not assessed in detail.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the supplied literature, icosapent ethyl lowers triglycerides and remnant lipoproteins, and is also described as having anti-inflammatory, plaque-stabilizing and antithrombotic effects. A 2026 mouse study (PMID 42628912) reports that it activates the omega-3 receptor GPR120 and improves cardiac remodeling after myocardial infarction by suppressing inflammatory cells.

The original indication (cardiovascular risk reduction) and the predicted indication (myocardial infarction) are closely related. Myocardial infarction is one of the key events that cardiovascular risk reduction aims to prevent. This is therefore essentially an on-label or closely adjacent use rather than a true repurposing. REDUCE-IT analyses show reduced ischemic events, including in patients with prior MI and across MI types and sizes.

The evidence is not uniformly positive. A meta-analysis of 13 omega-3 trials (PMID 31567003) is a reminder that results for omega-3 products as a class have been debated, and the choice of placebo in REDUCE-IT has been questioned.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: No interaction records were found in the queried database.
- **Atrial fibrillation**: REDUCE-IT reported more atrial fibrillation/flutter hospitalizations with icosapent ethyl (3.1% vs 2.1%, P=0.004), as described in the supplied literature (PMID 36802845). Monitoring for atrial fibrillation and bleeding is advised.

For full warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple REDUCE-IT publications, including a large randomized trial and analyses in prior-MI patients, support cardiovascular and MI-related risk reduction. This is largely consistent with the existing indication. However, the only registered trial is withdrawn, the EU authorization is cancelled, and results for omega-3 products as a class are mixed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm product availability, since the only EU authorization is cancelled
- Obtain the SmPC warnings and contraindications (currently missing)
- Obtain mechanism of action data
- Restrict use to statin-treated patients with elevated triglycerides and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (or diabetes with additional risk factors)
- Put an atrial fibrillation and bleeding monitoring plan in place
- Weigh the neutral results of other omega-3 trials and the placebo debate when interpreting the evidence
