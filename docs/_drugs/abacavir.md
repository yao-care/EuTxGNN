## Abacavir: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Abacavir is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor, originally used in combination antiretroviral therapy for HIV infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, but only **1 publication** (a 2004 in vitro susceptibility study) and **no clinical trials** support this. SIV infection occurs in non-human primates and is not a human clinical indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Abacavir is known to be an NRTI active against HIV-1 reverse transcriptase, and its efficacy in HIV infection is established. SIV is a closely related lentivirus with a similar reverse transcriptase, so activity is mechanistically plausible.

The only supporting study is a 2004 laboratory paper. It tested 16 approved anti-HIV drugs and one experimental drug against HIV-2, SIV and SHIV strains. This is early, in vitro evidence. It does not show that abacavir works in a living host.

SIV infection occurs in non-human primates, so the translational value is mainly as a preclinical model, not as a new treatment for patients. The high TxGNN score therefore mostly reflects how biologically close SIV is to HIV.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only evidence for SIV infection is a single in vitro study, with no clinical trials. SIV is a non-human primate infection, so it is not a meaningful new indication for patients. The EU authorization for the reference product is also cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Treat this as a preclinical or model-use question only, with in vivo data in a primate model if it is pursued.
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA).
- EU label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC.
- Clarification of current EU availability of abacavir products, since the only record found is cancelled.

For reference, two other predictions for this drug have stronger support, but both fall within or near the approved HIV indication. **Congenital HIV** has a completed Phase 3 trial (NCT00102960, n=377), and **AIDS related complex** has a completed Phase 2 study (NCT00002364, n=40). If the goal is practical value, these are better candidates to review than SIV infection.
