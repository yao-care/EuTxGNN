## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Peginterferon alfa-2a is a pegylated form of interferon-alpha. Interferon-alpha is known to have antiviral and immunomodulatory effects, including induction of interferon-stimulated genes and enhancement of HBV-specific T-cell responses.

Chronic hepatitis B is already listed in the EU authorisation for adults with compensated liver disease and, for HBeAg-positive disease, for children aged 3 years and older. The high prediction score therefore mainly reflects a known use, and this should not be read as a new discovery. Large randomised and post-marketing studies directly support its efficacy in this setting.

Other predicted diseases (hepatitis E, hepatitis A, animal viral hepatitis, Omsk haemorrhagic fever, Kyasanur forest disease and several cardiac conditions) were also scored by the model. For these, the compiled data show no direct supporting evidence. The cardiac predictions in particular lack a plausible mechanism. They are not recommended for further development at this stage.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Chronic hepatitis B is an already approved indication, and Phase 3/4 trials plus published RCTs and meta-analyses directly support it. This is label confirmation rather than new repurposing, and the EU authorisation is currently cancelled, so market availability needs clarification.

The other predicted indications (hepatitis E, hepatitis A, animal viral hepatitis, Omsk haemorrhagic fever, Kyasanur forest disease and the cardiac conditions) have no direct evidence and are recommended as **Hold**.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarification of the reason for the cancelled EU authorisation and confirmation of current availability
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC (currently missing, and a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Patient selection criteria (compensated liver disease, raised ALT, viral replication), stopping rules and a monitoring plan for interferon-related toxicity
- Review of the major drug interactions listed above before clinical use
