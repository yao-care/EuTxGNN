# Regorafenib: From Colorectal Cancer to Liposarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Regorafenib (Stivarga) is an oral multikinase inhibitor originally approved for metastatic colorectal cancer, GIST, and hepatocellular carcinoma. TxGNN predicts high relevance for **Liposarcoma** (score 99.76%), but the two completed Phase 2 RCTs directly testing this indication (REGOSARC, SARC024) both showed **no benefit specifically in liposarcoma**, even though the drug worked in other sarcoma subtypes — this is a case where mechanistic plausibility is high but direct trial evidence is negative.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Per the drug monograph literature included in this pack (PMID 30069758, 24756792), regorafenib is an oral diphenylurea multi-kinase inhibitor targeting angiogenic receptors (VEGFR1-3, TIE2), stromal receptors (PDGFR-β, FGFR), and oncogenic kinases (KIT, RET, RAF). This broad antiangiogenic/multikinase profile is the basis for its approval in colorectal cancer and GIST, and mechanistically extends to soft tissue sarcomas, which are highly dependent on angiogenic signaling.

This mechanistic rationale is exactly why liposarcoma was investigated directly: the REGOSARC trial (NCT01900743, PMID 27751846) tested regorafenib against placebo across five sarcoma cohorts, including a dedicated liposarcoma cohort. The results showed clear efficacy in leiomyosarcoma and synovial sarcoma, **but the liposarcoma cohort did not show benefit** and was closed early on interim analysis (PMID 29902612). This finding was independently confirmed by the SARC024 trial's liposarcoma-specific Phase 2 RCT (PMID 32701199), which explicitly concluded that results "do not support the routine use of regorafenib in this patient population."

In other words: the TxGNN prediction is mechanistically sound (angiogenesis-driven tumor, multikinase inhibitor target match), and it correctly identified that regorafenib and liposarcoma are closely connected in the literature — but the connection reflects **two negative Phase 2 RCTs**, not a validated new indication. This is a useful example of why high TxGNN scores must be checked against actual trial outcomes rather than mechanism alone.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic case for regorafenib in liposarcoma is sound, but the two most relevant completed Phase 2 RCTs (REGOSARC, SARC024) both specifically found no benefit in the liposarcoma subgroup, even as they demonstrated benefit in other sarcoma subtypes. Combined with a Blocking data gap on official safety labeling (DG001), this indication does not meet the bar to proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Resolve DG001 (official EMA/TFDA label warnings and contraindications) before any safety evaluation can begin
- Resolve DG002 (formal DrugBank MOA record) — the MOA used in this report was reconstructed from literature abstracts within the pack, not an authoritative source
- If pursuing regorafenib repurposing further, redirect priority toward **clear cell renal cell carcinoma / renal cell carcinoma (disease)** (ranks 3 & 10), which carry the same L2 evidence level but are at decision stage S2 ("Research Question") with a supportive single-arm Phase 2 trial (NCT00664326) and no contradicting negative RCT — a materially stronger evidence position than liposarcoma
