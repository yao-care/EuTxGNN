## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Mycophenolic acid, the active form of mycophenolate mofetil, inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH). This depletes intracellular guanosine nucleotide pools and suppresses lymphocyte proliferation. Activated lymphocytes support HIV replication, so fewer of them could reduce the reservoir available to the virus. The depleted nucleotide pool also shifts dNTP ratios, which can strengthen the activity of nucleoside analogues such as abacavir and didanosine.

Laboratory work and early clinical reports support this logic. In a small series, adding the drug to abacavir-containing regimens was associated with lower intracellular deoxyguanosine triphosphate and lower plasma HIV-1 RNA. However, controlled clinical studies have not shown durable virologic benefit. Immunosuppression is also a concern in people with HIV. The rationale is biologically plausible but remains a research question rather than an established treatment.

Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available beyond the IMPDH-based reasoning above.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 92 interactions are recorded in total. The main ones are:
  - **Major**: activated charcoal.
  - **Moderate**:
    - Proton pump inhibitors: omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, rabeprazole.
    - Antibiotics: amoxicillin, clarithromycin, doxycycline, minocycline, tetracycline, vancomycin, kanamycin, paromomycin.
    - Antacids and minerals: magnesium oxide, aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate.
  - **Minor**: zinc sulfate, zinc acetate, iron.
- **Setting-specific concern**: Immunosuppression is a particular concern in people with HIV. Because the drug acts on lymphocytes, any use in HIV would need careful monitoring of immune status and infection risk.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L2. It rests on one randomized trial of antiretroviral therapy with or without mycophenolate mofetil in treatment-naive patients (PMID 15353978) and a randomized phase 2 pilot (NCT00038272); there are no phase 3 trials in HIV. The mechanism is plausible, but the clinical studies show no durable virologic benefit, and immunosuppression adds risk in this population.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A review of the full results of the randomized trial (PMID 15353978) and the phase 2 pilot, to judge whether any virologic or reservoir signal justifies further study.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data on guanosine depletion and nucleoside-analogue synergy in HIV.
- A safety and infection-monitoring plan for immunosuppression in people with HIV, including interactions with antiretroviral regimens.
- SmPC warnings and contraindications for formal safety screening.
