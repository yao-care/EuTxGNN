## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Mycophenolic acid, the active form of mycophenolate mofetil, inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH). This depletes intracellular guanosine nucleotide pools and suppresses lymphocyte proliferation. Activated lymphocytes support HIV replication, so reducing them is a plausible way to limit the virus. Guanosine depletion also shifts dNTP ratios, which can boost nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors such as abacavir and didanosine.

Published work is consistent with this idea. A small case series (PMID 12352149) reported depleted dGTP and a drop in plasma HIV-1 RNA when mycophenolate was added to abacavir-based regimens. A randomised study in treatment-naive patients (PMID 15353978) examined its effect on viral decay and the latent reservoir. However, the data compiled for this report show no durable virologic benefit, and immunosuppression is a real concern in people with HIV. No study retrieved shows a clear clinical benefit of mycophenolate itself in HIV.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is biologically plausible, but no cited trial directly shows efficacy of mycophenolate in HIV: the one directly relevant trial was withdrawn with no participants, and the others are indirect. Immunosuppression in people with HIV is an added concern, so the evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A completed, adequately powered controlled trial of mycophenolate in HIV with virologic and immunologic endpoints
- Safety analysis of immunosuppression and opportunistic infection risk in people with HIV
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications for a full safety screen
- Detailed mechanism of action data from drug databases
