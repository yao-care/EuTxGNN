## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Lecanemab is known to be an anti-amyloid-beta protofibril monoclonal antibody. Its efficacy in early Alzheimer's disease rests on targeting amyloid pathology in the brain.

The only conceivable link to diabetic cataract is a hypothesis that amyloid-beta deposition or protein aggregation occurs in lens tissue. This is speculative and has not been established as a cause of cataract. Cataract in diabetes is driven mainly by high blood sugar (polyol pathway, oxidative stress, glycation), not by amyloid-beta.

There are also practical concerns. Large IgG antibodies are expected to penetrate the lens poorly after systemic dosing. The risk of amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) would weigh against use in a non-fatal condition that is treatable by surgery. The score of 0.985 reflects graph proximity in the model rather than demonstrated biology.

Other top predictions (ranks 2–10) are also cataract-related terms or diabetic retinopathy. They share the same weak rationale, and all remain at evidence level L5 with a Hold recommendation. Diabetic retinopathy has a slightly more plausible preclinical rationale (reported amyloid-beta accumulation and retinal neurodegeneration), but it is still hypothetical.
