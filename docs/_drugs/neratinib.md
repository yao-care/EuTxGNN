# Neratinib: From HER2-Positive Breast Cancer to Progesterone-Receptor Positive Breast Cancer

## One-Sentence Summary

Neratinib is an irreversible pan-HER tyrosine kinase inhibitor, originally authorised in the EU for extended adjuvant treatment of early-stage HR-positive, HER2-positive breast cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **progesterone-receptor positive breast cancer**, with **2 clinical trials** and **12 publications** currently linked to this direction.
Most of this evidence overlaps with the already-approved population, so the truly new signal (HER2-negative or HER2-mutant disease) rests on early-phase data.

*Note on selection:* The top-ranked prediction (normal breast-like subtype of breast carcinoma) has no trials or literature and a weak mechanistic link. This report therefore focuses on the highest-ranked prediction with actual evidence, progesterone-receptor positive breast cancer (rank 2, same TxGNN score).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, neratinib is an irreversible inhibitor of EGFR (HER1), HER2 and HER4 tyrosine kinases. Its efficacy in HR-positive, HER2-positive early breast cancer has been shown in the ExteNET phase 3 trial.

In HR-positive/HER2-positive disease, crosstalk between HER2 and estrogen receptor signalling is a recognised route of resistance. This supports combining neratinib with endocrine therapy. However, for the HR+/HER2+ population this is largely on-label use, not true repurposing.

The genuinely new signal lies in HR-positive, HER2-negative or HER2-mutant disease. There, activating ERBB2 mutations are associated with endocrine resistance. The SUMMIT basket study (neratinib + fulvestrant + trastuzumab) and preclinical work in HR+/HER2-low models point in this direction, but the evidence is early-phase and restricted to biomarker-defined subgroups.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screening returned 100 interacting drugs. Examples of the ones flagged as Major:
  - Acid-suppressing agents: famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, and the proton pump inhibitors omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole and rabeprazole
  - Aprepitant
  - Dexamethasone
  - Clarithromycin
  - Clotrimazole

  Moderate-level interactions include loperamide (relevant because it is used for neratinib-associated diarrhoea), magnesium oxide, aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, empagliflozin and naldemedine. Minor interactions include naloxegol and fidaxomicin.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L2 at most. The only trials for this indication are one small recruiting Phase 2 study without results and one withdrawn study with no patients. Most supportive data (ExteNET) concern the already-approved HR+/HER2+ population. The EU authorisation is also cancelled, and warnings and contraindications are not available. The higher-ranked predictions have no supporting evidence at all. The more promising repurposing direction is HER2-mutant HR+/HER2-negative breast cancer, with the SUMMIT study as the main reference.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC, which is currently missing and blocks safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Results from the recruiting Phase 2 trial (NCT04886531), or a new trial in HER2-mutant HR+/HER2-negative disease
- Clarification of why the EU authorisation was cancelled and the current availability of the product
- A plan to manage diarrhoea and the many acid-suppressant interactions, which affect practical use
