## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the structured DrugBank record for this drug (Data Gap DG002). Based on the literature evidence collected in this pack for ESL's other predicted indications, eslicarbazepine acetate is metabolized to its active moiety eslicarbazepine, which blocks voltage-gated sodium channels by enhancing their slow inactivation — a broad-spectrum antiseizure mechanism with a well-documented efficacy record in focal-onset seizures.

ADEAF (also known as ADLTE, autosomal dominant lateral temporal epilepsy) is a genetic focal epilepsy syndrome caused by *LGI1* mutations, presenting with auditory/aphasic focal seizures. Since it is fundamentally a focal epilepsy, a sodium-channel-blocking antiepileptic is mechanistically plausible as a symptomatic treatment. However, no study in this pack — or in the wider evidence pool for this drug — specifically enrolls or analyzes ADEAF patients; the link is a class-level extrapolation from "ESL treats focal epilepsy in general" rather than syndrome-specific evidence.

It is also worth noting that other TxGNN-predicted indications for this same drug (status epilepticus, secondary generalized tonic-clonic epilepsy) already have L3-level supporting evidence with real trials and literature, whereas ADEAF sits at the bottom of the evidence hierarchy (L5, model-only).
