# Sirolimus: From Facial Angiofibroma in Tuberous Sclerosis Complex to Liposarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Sirolimus is an mTOR inhibitor, originally used for facial angiofibroma in tuberous sclerosis complex, prevention of kidney transplant rejection, and sporadic lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM). The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **liposarcoma**, but support is thin: **1 completed single-arm Phase 2 trial** and **7 publications**, mostly reviews, which give only indirect support.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, sirolimus inhibits mTOR (mammalian target of rapamycin), a kinase that controls cell growth and proliferation. Its efficacy in diseases driven by mTOR over-activation, such as TSC-related lesions and LAM, is established.

The PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is often dysregulated in soft tissue sarcomas, including liposarcoma. Mouse models also suggest that mTOR inhibition can slow tumour growth. This gives a plausible biological link between the original indications and liposarcoma.

The direct evidence is limited. The one registered trial combines sirolimus with cyclophosphamide, and no efficacy results were available in the compiled data. The literature is mainly reviews of targeted sarcoma therapy and transplant cancer-risk studies. For this reason the prediction is best treated as a research question rather than a proven new use.

## Safety Considerations

Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC for these.

- **Possible disease-related cautions (from a drug-disease knowledge graph, not label text):** hypertension, kidney disease, liver disease, hyperglycaemia and diabetes, hyperlipidaemia and hypertriglyceridaemia, interstitial lung disease, active infections (including cytomegalovirus infection and pneumocystosis), pericardial effusion, and a prior history of cancer.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. Major-level examples include amphotericin B (including the lipid complex), mesalazine, balsalazide, and clarithromycin. Moderate-level examples include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, budesonide), metformin, pioglitazone, aprepitant, and cimetidine.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mTOR pathway rationale is plausible, but direct evidence in liposarcoma is limited to one single-arm Phase 2 combination trial with no available results. The supporting literature is indirect. Sirolimus also has no valid EU marketing authorization at present.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Efficacy results from the Phase 2 trial (NCT02821507) and identification of the contribution of sirolimus versus cyclophosphamide
- Comparative or randomised evidence in liposarcoma
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Label safety data (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC, and a plan for drug interaction management
- Clarification of the regulatory pathway, since both EU authorizations are cancelled
