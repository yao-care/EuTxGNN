Sirolimus is an mTOR inhibitor used in transplant medicine and for certain rare tuberous-sclerosis-related conditions. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **liposarcoma**, but the evidence is currently limited to **1 registered Phase 2 trial without available results** and **no published study that tests sirolimus in liposarcoma**, so this remains a model-based prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, sirolimus inhibits mTOR, a kinase that sits in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling pathway. This pathway is frequently dysregulated in soft tissue sarcomas, including liposarcoma, and mouse models suggest that mTOR inhibition can slow tumour growth.

This gives a plausible biological rationale for the prediction. However, the available literature on sarcoma consists mainly of general reviews of targeted therapies and of studies on cancer risk in renal transplant patients. These offer only indirect support, and none of them shows that sirolimus works in liposarcoma.
