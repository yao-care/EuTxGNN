## Tocilizumab: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Ankylosing Spondylitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Tocilizumab is an IL-6 receptor antibody originally used to treat rheumatoid arthritis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, giant cell arteritis, COVID-19 and CAR-T-induced cytokine release syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **ankylosing spondylitis**, but the two Phase 3 trials in this disease were **terminated** and the literature does not show convincing efficacy.
Supporting material includes **4 registered trials** (2 directly relevant) and **20 publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, tocilizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that blocks the IL-6 receptor, its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis and related inflammatory conditions has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to ankylosing spondylitis.

IL-6 contributes to systemic inflammation and bone turnover, and ankylosing spondylitis is an inflammatory rheumatic disease that is often treated alongside RA. This gives the prediction a plausible biological basis.

However, the evidence points the other way. Two Phase 3 placebo-controlled trials (NCT01209689, NCT01209702) were terminated, and published literature indicates that IL-6 blockade is less effective than TNF or IL-17 inhibition in spondyloarthritis. The high model score therefore reflects pathway similarity rather than proven clinical benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. The most significant is **Deferiprone (Major)**. Moderate interactions include anticoagulants (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dicoumarol), statins (simvastatin, rosuvastatin), chloroquine and hydroxychloroquine, nitroimidazoles (metronidazole, tinidazole, secnidazole, benznidazole), naltrexone, disulfiram and lidocaine. Zinc salts are Minor.
- **Infection risk**: The literature highlights increased infection risk with biologics, including tuberculosis, which calls for screening before use.

Please refer to the SmPC for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Although two Phase 3 AS trials exist (which is why the trial-count level is L1), both were terminated without confirmed benefit, and reviews indicate IL-6 blockade is inferior to TNF and IL-17 inhibitors in spondyloarthritis. The drug is marketed in this country, with 4 of 4 licences valid.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results and termination reasons for NCT01209689 and NCT01209702
- Mechanism of action data and a clearer mechanistic argument for AS
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- A subgroup analysis (e.g., TNF-inadequate responders) showing a favourable benefit-risk balance versus established AS therapies

### Other predicted indications (brief note)

- **Polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis** and its **rheumatoid factor-positive** subtype are already approved indications. They validate the model but are not new repurposing opportunities (supported by the Phase 3 trial NCT00988221).
- **Rheumatoid vasculitis** has only case-level evidence (L4), and some reports describe tocilizumab-induced vasculitis-like reactions.
- **Inflammatory spondylopathy** overlaps with AS and has the same terminated trials.
- **Hypermobility of coccyx, Kummell disease, vertebral disease, spondyloarthropathy susceptibility** and **IL12B-deficiency mycobacterial susceptibility** have no meaningful evidence or mechanistic rationale. The last is a potential safety concern because of tocilizumab's infection risk.
