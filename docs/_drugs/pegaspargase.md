## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. In general, pegaspargase is a pegylated asparaginase. It depletes extracellular asparagine, and some lymphoid cancer cells have low asparagine synthetase activity and depend on that supply. Depriving them of it can lead to cell death.

"Lymphoid neoplasm" is a broad category that includes ALL, the approved indication. The prediction therefore largely restates a known use rather than proposing a new one. Other lymphoid cancers, such as NK/T-cell lymphoma, may share the same dependence on asparagine. The literature in the data shows pegaspargase-based regimens in that disease (see below), but it is a distinct entity from the predicted diseases listed here.

The other predictions in the data are weaker. Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia variants and follicular lymphoma have no trials or literature. Methylcobalamin deficiency type cblE has no plausible link to asparagine depletion and is likely a false positive. Hodgkin lymphoma has no direct study. The nearby NK/T-cell lymphoma evidence cannot be extrapolated to it. Blast-phase CML has only an indirect ALL trial.

## Safety Considerations

- **Toxicities described in the literature:** hepatotoxicity, hypersensitivity reactions, thrombosis and age-related adverse events. The 440-patient phase 3 trial NCT05602194 studies prevention of asparaginase-associated liver toxicity in adolescents and young adults.
- **Drug Interactions:** The interaction check returned 100 entries; the 20 shown are all Moderate-level. They include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), antidiabetic agents (acarbose, metformin, alogliptin, albiglutide, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, saxagliptin, dulaglutide, chlorpropamide), antibiotics (doxycycline, tetracycline, clarithromycin), bupropion, acetylsalicylic acid and chenodeoxycholic acid.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Pegaspargase is well established in ALL, but that is its approved use. No cited study directly examines the predicted lymphoid neoplasm entities as separate targets, so the evidence level is L5. The highest-scoring predictions (chronic lymphocytic leukaemia variants, follicular lymphoma, cblE deficiency) have no supporting data and look like computational artifacts.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clearly defined target disease; NK/T-cell lymphoma, where pegaspargase regimens show phase 2 activity, would be a candidate for a separate evaluation
- Trials that isolate pegaspargase's contribution rather than use it as part of a backbone regimen
- Mechanism of action data and the SmPC warnings and contraindications
- A safety monitoring plan covering hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, thrombosis, hypersensitivity and the interaction list above
