# Mepolizumab: From Severe Eosinophilic Asthma to Thrombocytopenia Due to Immune Destruction

## One-Sentence Summary

Mepolizumab is an anti-IL-5 antibody originally used as an add-on treatment for severe eosinophilic asthma and related eosinophilic conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **thrombocytopenia due to immune destruction**,
but the data compiled for this report contain **0 clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a single case report) for this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, mepolizumab is an anti-IL-5 monoclonal antibody that depletes eosinophils. Its efficacy in eosinophil-driven diseases (severe eosinophilic asthma, EGPA, HES and CRSwNP) is reflected in its approved indications.

The only supporting report is a single case (PMID 28648630). It describes a steroid-resistant hypereosinophilic immune diathesis that resolved with mepolizumab, together with improvement of a mixed thrombotic microangiopathy. This suggests that immune-mediated cytopenias might improve when eosinophil-driven immune dysregulation is the underlying cause.

This is indirect evidence from one case-level report. There is no evidence that mepolizumab works in primary immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) that is not driven by eosinophilia. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only.

Other top-ranked predictions have weaker support:
- **Primary release disorder of platelets**: only a review of HES management, with no direct link.
- **Pseudo-von Willebrand disease, Glanzmann thrombasthenia, autosomal thrombocytopenia with normal platelets**: no plausible IL-5 mechanism.
- **Autoimmune thrombocytopenic, Evans syndrome, mixed-type autoimmune hemolytic anemia**: speculative immunomodulatory rationale only, with no evidence retrieved.
- **Neonatal thrombocytopenia**: no evidence, and no neonatal safety or pharmacokinetic data.
- **Filariasis**: eosinophils help control parasites, so depleting them could be harmful.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a high computational score and a single indirect case report in an eosinophil-driven setting. There are no clinical trials, and the mechanism is not plausible for most predicted platelet disorders. The EU authorization is also listed as cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety information (warnings, contraindications) from the SmPC, since this is currently a blocking gap
- Mechanism of action data to support a mechanistic-link analysis
- Confirmation of the current EU authorization status, given that it is listed as cancelled
- Further evidence on whether any ITP subset is eosinophil-driven, such as case series or a pilot study
- Direct clinical data in immune thrombocytopenia before any further consideration
