## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. From pharmacology data, teduglutide is a peptide that acts on the GLP-2 receptor (GLP2R). It promotes growth and absorptive function of the intestinal lining, which is why it works in short bowel syndrome.

Papillary conjunctivitis is an ocular condition, typically allergic or related to mechanical irritation (for example, from contact lenses). No clear mechanistic link exists between GLP-2 receptor signalling in the gut and this eye condition. The high TxGNN score reflects proximity in a knowledge graph and is not backed by any trial or publication. This prediction should be treated as a hypothesis with low biological plausibility.

Other predictions in the list give a sense of where the model's signal is stronger. Gastroduodenitis and peptic ulcer disease have a plausible but unproven rationale (GLP-2 supports mucosal repair and reduces gastric acid secretion). Chronic intestinal vascular insufficiency and isolated mesenteric vein thrombosis relate to bowel loss that can lead to short bowel syndrome, but teduglutide would address the downstream intestinal failure and not the vascular problem itself.
