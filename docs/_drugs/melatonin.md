# Melatonin: From Insomnia to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Melatonin is a sleep-regulating hormone, authorised in the EU for insomnia in older adults and for sleep problems in children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) or ADHD.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction, migraine with brainstem aura (score 87.9%), has no direct trials. The best-supported predicted indication is the broader **migraine disorder**, with **6 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. This report evaluates migraine disorder as the lead candidate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Pharmacology data show that melatonin binds the MT1 and MT2 receptors (MTNR1A, MTNR1B) and the 5-HT2B receptor. It regulates circadian rhythm and sleep-wake timing, which is the basis of its insomnia use.

Migraine and sleep are closely linked. Poor or irregular sleep can trigger attacks, and several studies report lower nocturnal melatonin excretion in people with migraine. Beyond circadian regulation, melatonin has antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. It also acts on serotonergic and GABAergic systems and on pain-related pathways, including interaction with CGRP. These properties offer a plausible reason it could reduce attack frequency.

The evidence is stronger in adults than in adolescents. A 2022 meta-analysis supports prophylactic use in adults, while results in adolescents are mixed and some trials were terminated.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** The TxGNN knowledge graph links melatonin to contraindication with several conditions, including liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, autoimmune disease (including systemic lupus erythematosus), psoriasis, sarcoidosis, anxiety and neurotic disorders, schizophrenia, substance abuse/dependence, and nephrolithiasis. These are database associations and should be verified against the product information.

**Drug Interactions:** 17 database-listed interactions were found.
- **Moderate:** deferasirox, warfarin, alpelisib, anagrelide, lorlatinib, olaparib, osimertinib, rucaparib, teriflunomide, vemurafenib, ifosfamide.
- **Minor:** cimetidine, obeticholic acid, caffeine, osilodrostat, ethinylestradiol, diethylstilbestrol.

Warfarin deserves particular attention in practice. Sedation is also a consideration, and this should be monitored in any migraine trial.

Please refer to the SmPC for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Migraine disorder has a completed Phase 3 RCT (n=192), several completed Phase 2 trials, and a 2022 meta-analysis supporting prophylactic use in adults. Evidence in adolescents is mixed and some trials were terminated. All EU melatonin authorisations are currently cancelled, so any development would need a new regulatory pathway.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results and effect sizes from NCT01357031 and the Phase 2 trials, confirming efficacy against placebo
- Dose definition, and separate confirmation of the pediatric evidence
- Mechanism of action data to be completed
- EMA label warnings and contraindications to be obtained (currently missing) and reviewed before safety screening
- A sedation and interaction monitoring plan, especially for warfarin and other moderate-interaction drugs
- Clarification of why the EU authorisations were cancelled

**Other predictions:** Migraine with brainstem aura, restless legs syndrome and monogenic obesity are rated Hold (L4, indirect evidence only). Obesity disorder has one Phase 3 trial of unknown status and should stay a research question until results are available. The remaining predictions (hypervitaminosis, obsolete hypertelorism, frontorhiny, proximal 16p11.2 microdeletion syndrome, and acute encephalopathy with biphasic seizures) have no trials or literature and are model outputs only (L5, Hold).
