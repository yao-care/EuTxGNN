# Defibrotide: From Hepatic Veno-Occlusive Disease to Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura

## One-Sentence Summary

Defibrotide was originally authorised for severe hepatic veno-occlusive disease (VOD), also called sinusoidal obstructive syndrome (SOS), after haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation (HSCT).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **pseudo-von Willebrand disease** (top-ranked prediction). However, that prediction has no supporting studies, so this report also looks at the best-supported candidate, **thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)**, which has **0 registered clinical trials** and **8 publications** (mostly small case reports and reviews).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, defibrotide has proven efficacy in hepatic VOD/SOS after HSCT, a condition driven by injury to the endothelium (the lining of blood vessels). Its endothelial-protective, profibrinolytic and antithrombotic effects are the likely basis for that benefit.

**Pseudo-von Willebrand disease (top prediction).** This is a platelet bleeding disorder caused by a gain-of-function defect in the GPIb-alpha receptor. Defibrotide's effects do not obviously address that defect, and its antithrombotic activity could plausibly worsen bleeding. The high score (0.999) most likely reflects closeness to coagulation and platelet nodes in the knowledge network rather than a true therapeutic rationale. The same reasoning applies to the other highly ranked bleeding and platelet disorders (primary release disorder of platelets, Glanzmann thrombasthenia, Scott syndrome, collagen receptor defect, constitutional thrombocytopenia, congenital factor V deficiency, and fetal and neonatal alloimmune thrombocytopenia). None has any trial or literature support.

**Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP).** This is biologically plausible. TTP and transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (TA-TMA) involve endothelial injury and small-vessel clots, which overlap with the endothelial mechanism behind defibrotide's approved use. However, the evidence is old, uncontrolled and mostly from HSCT settings. Standard TTP therapy (plasma exchange, immunosuppression, caplacizumab) is well established, so any role for defibrotide would be niche, for example refractory TTP or TA-TMA.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The data compiled for this report list 100 interactions. The main ones are **Major** interactions with agents that affect bleeding or clotting: acetylsalicylic acid, abciximab, acalabrutinib, alteplase, anagrelide, fondaparinux, deferasirox, ibritumomab tiuxetan and tositumomab. **Moderate** interactions include NSAIDs (ibuprofen, ketorolac, celecoxib, topical diclofenac), omega-3 fatty acids, fluvoxamine, and fenfluramine-type agents (dexfenfluramine, fenfluramine, sibutramine).
- **Bleeding concern**: Defibrotide has antithrombotic and profibrinolytic activity. This is a particular worry for the bleeding and platelet disorders ranked highly by the model.
- **Case-report signal**: One 1994 report describes TTP occurring after defibrotide therapy, which needs careful evaluation.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications, as this information was not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for pseudo-von Willebrand disease and the other bleeding or platelet disorders). TTP and TA-TMA could be treated as a research question and, if pursued, would need guardrails.

**Rationale:**
- The top predictions rest on model scores alone (L5). There is no trial or literature support, the mechanism does not fit, and there is a plausible bleeding risk.
- For TTP, the evidence (L3) is limited to small, dated, uncontrolled reports and an in vitro study. Standard therapies are well established, and the single EU authorisation is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for defibrotide
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, which are required for safety screening
- A focused review of defibrotide in refractory TTP and TA-TMA after HSCT, including the post-defibrotide TTP report
- Clarification of the EU regulatory status (the authorisation is cancelled)
- A prospective study or registry in TA-TMA, with bleeding monitoring and a review of interactions with anticoagulants and antiplatelet agents
