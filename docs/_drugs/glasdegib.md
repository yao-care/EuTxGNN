# Glasdegib: From Acute Myeloid Leukaemia to Bulbar Polio

## One-Sentence Summary

Glasdegib is an oral Hedgehog pathway (Smoothened) inhibitor, originally used with low-dose cytarabine for newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) in adults who cannot receive standard induction chemotherapy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bulbar polio**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Glasdegib is known as a Smoothened inhibitor that blocks Hedgehog signalling. In AML, this pathway is thought to support the survival of leukaemic stem cells, which is the basis for its approved use.

For bulbar polio, a mechanistic link is hard to establish. Bulbar polio is a viral infection that destroys motor neurons in the brainstem. No established connection exists between Hedgehog/Smoothened inhibition and poliovirus-induced neuronal damage. The high TxGNN score reflects a pattern in a knowledge graph, not biological or clinical evidence. The original indication (a blood cancer) and the predicted one (a viral neurological disease) also share no obvious disease features.

This prediction should therefore be treated as a computational hypothesis only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a graph-based model score. There are no trials or publications, and no plausible mechanistic link between Hedgehog inhibition and poliovirus-related motor neuron loss. The product's only EU authorization is also cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Safety information from the SmPC, including warnings and contraindications
