## Ofatumumab: From Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis to Follicular Lymphoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ofatumumab (Kesimpta) is an anti-CD20 antibody authorized in the EU for adults with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis (RMS) with active disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **follicular lymphoma**, with **11 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction.
Of these, several Phase 2 studies directly address follicular lymphoma, but they use intravenous oncology dosing, not the subcutaneous MS regimen.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data from the drug database is not available for this report. Based on the data compiled, ofatumumab is a fully human anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody. It binds a distinct epitope of CD20 on B cells and kills them through complement-dependent cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.

In multiple sclerosis, the rationale is depletion of B cells. Follicular lymphoma is a CD20-positive B-cell malignancy, so the same target is directly relevant. The Phase 2 studies below, including activity in rituximab-refractory disease, support this rationale.

One important caveat: the lymphoma studies used intravenous oncology dosing, often in combination regimens. This differs from the subcutaneous 20 mg regimen of the MS product (Kesimpta). Results cannot be assumed to transfer directly.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 80 interactions were recorded in the interaction database. Major-level examples include deferiprone, samarium (153Sm) lexidronam, adalimumab, baricitinib, certolizumab pegol, cladribine and clozapine. Moderate-level examples include alemtuzumab, azathioprine, anakinra, canakinumab, chloramphenicol, zidovudine, and several vaccines (anthrax, tetanus toxoid, live cholera). Most of these reflect additive immunosuppression or myelosuppression. Vaccine interactions are relevant to a B-cell-depleting antibody.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 2 trials and publications, including randomized studies, directly study ofatumumab in follicular lymphoma. Response in rituximab-refractory disease was modest, and the Phase 3 trial in indolent B-cell NHL was terminated. Evidence is therefore intermediate (L2) rather than strong, and it comes from intravenous oncology regimens.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the SmPC warnings and contraindications, which could not be compiled for this report
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from the drug database
- Clarification of why the Phase 3 trial (NCT01077518) was terminated and a review of its results
- A dose and route strategy, since the lymphoma evidence uses intravenous dosing and differs from the subcutaneous MS regimen
- A comparison against established anti-CD20 options (e.g., rituximab) in follicular lymphoma
