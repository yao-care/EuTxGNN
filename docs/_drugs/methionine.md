## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Methionine is an essential sulfur-containing amino acid and the precursor of homocysteine through the methionine cycle. Its efficacy in the authorised MSUD setting concerns amino acid supply in metabolic decompensation, which differs fundamentally from acne.

The only acne-related link in the data is a small study reporting elevated plasma homocysteine in patients taking isotretinoin for cystic acne. This points to an indirect connection with one-carbon metabolism. It gives no evidence that methionine supplementation improves acne, and raised homocysteine could argue against supplementation. The second retrieved paper describes an MTHFR mutation with neonatal encephalopathy, in which neonatal acne was only one of several skin findings, and it is not relevant to acne treatment.

The very high TxGNN score is a model output and is not backed by clinical data. A rationale for repurposing methionine in acne is therefore not established.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone. No trials exist, and the two retrieved papers do not support methionine as an acne therapy. The only link, raised homocysteine during isotretinoin treatment, is indirect and could imply risk rather than benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for methionine
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, followed by a safety review
- Preclinical or clinical evidence that studies methionine directly in acne
- A review of homocysteine-related risk before any supplementation hypothesis is pursued
