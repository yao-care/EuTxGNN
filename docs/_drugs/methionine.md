# Methionine: From Maple Syrup Urine Disease to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Methionine (marketed as Maapliv) was originally authorised in the EU for acute decompensation episodes of maple syrup urine disease (MSUD).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **2 loosely related publications**, neither of which tests methionine as a treatment.
This is a model prediction with very weak supporting evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Methionine is an essential sulfur-containing amino acid and the precursor of homocysteine through the methionine cycle. Its use in MSUD is metabolic and nutritional, not anti-inflammatory or dermatological.

The link to acne is indirect. The only acne-related paper (PMID 11277950) found elevated plasma homocysteine in patients taking isotretinoin for cystic acne. This is an association with methionine metabolism, not evidence that methionine treats acne. The second paper (PMID 39357918) is a case report of an MTHFR mutation with neonatal encephalopathy, in which neonatal acne was one of several skin findings. It does not support a therapeutic role either.

The very high TxGNN score reflects a computational prediction from network features. Supplying more methionine could also raise homocysteine, so a mechanistic rationale for benefit in acne has not been established.

Other high-scoring predictions for this drug are mostly eye conditions (cataract subtypes and diabetic retinopathy). They have the same limitation: the literature describes methionine oxidation in lens proteins as pathology, not methionine as a treatment.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The acne prediction rests on a computational score alone. There are no clinical trials, and the two papers are indirect associations that do not test methionine as a treatment. The only EU authorisation is cancelled, and the possibility that methionine raises homocysteine is a safety concern for new uses.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for methionine and a plausible biological rationale for acne
- Preclinical or early clinical evidence that methionine supplementation improves acne
- Official safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC, which is not yet available
- A review of homocysteine-related cardiovascular and renal risks in the target population
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorisation status and available formulations and routes
