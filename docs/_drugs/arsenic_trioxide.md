## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for arsenic trioxide are not available in the data compiled for this report. The following is drawn from the literature and the rationale summaries compiled for this report. Arsenic trioxide is thought to act through induction of apoptosis, degradation of the PML-RARA fusion protein in APL, and effects on NF-kB and BCL2-family signalling.

APL and MDS are both haematological malignancies of the bone marrow. Laboratory studies in MDS cells show that arsenic trioxide induces apoptosis through NF-kB/FLIP modulation and changes in BCL2-family genes, and it may also promote cell differentiation. It has been tested in MDS alone and in combination with ascorbic acid, decitabine, azacitidine, thalidomide, retinoids and cytarabine.

The evidence is not specific to the exact predicted term. "Unclassified MDS" is a subtype of the broader MDS category, so the MDS evidence below is only indirect support. A 2023 systematic review and component network meta-analysis noted that arsenic trioxide has not been widely recommended in MDS because of small samples and conflicting trial results.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text)**: long QT syndrome, familial long QT syndrome, torsades de pointes, kidney disease, pyoureter, liver disease, gallbladder disease, potassium deficiency disease, atrioventricular block. The predominance of cardiac conduction disorders highlights QT prolongation as the key concern.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified in total. Major interactions among those listed include:
  - Hydrocortisone
  - Amphotericin B (conventional, lipid complex, cholesteryl sulfate and liposomal forms)
  - Cisapride
  - Clarithromycin
  - Dolasetron
  - Granisetron
  - Levofloxacin

  Moderate interactions include famotidine, loperamide, bisacodyl, castor oil, glycerin, lactitol, lactulose, magnesium citrate and magnesium hydroxide. Metronidazole is minor. Many of these relate to additive QT prolongation or electrolyte disturbance (for example through laxatives and amphotericin B).

Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The exact predicted term, unclassified MDS, has a very high model score (99.93%) but no linked trials or literature (L5). Evidence for the broader MDS category exists (L2) but is limited to small, early-phase or terminated trials with modest response rates and no Phase 3 trial. Advancing MDS as a research question is reasonable, but the current evidence does not justify progression for the exact term. In addition, all EU authorisations are cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mapping of "unclassified MDS" to the MDS trial and literature evidence, to confirm whether the broader evidence applies to this subtype
- Randomised, adequately powered trials of arsenic trioxide in defined MDS risk groups (for example the TP53-mutated and low-risk populations in ongoing studies NCT06778187 and NCT06670222)
- Label-based safety data (SmPC warnings and contraindications), with an ECG and electrolyte monitoring plan for QT risk
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Clarification of EU regulatory and supply status, since all authorisations are cancelled, and consideration of the oral formulation under study
- Mechanistic and route-compatibility assessment, which is still pending
