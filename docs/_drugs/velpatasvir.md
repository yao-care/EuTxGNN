# Velpatasvir: From Chronic Hepatitis C to Hepatitis B Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Velpatasvir is an HCV NS5A inhibitor, originally used to treat chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**, but the **5 related clinical trials** and **20 publications** retrieved are almost entirely about HCV treatment. They do not test velpatasvir against HBV.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Velpatasvir is known to be an NS5A inhibitor, used as part of fixed-dose combinations with sofosbuvir (Epclusa) and with sofosbuvir plus voxilaprevir (Vosevi). Its efficacy in chronic hepatitis C is well established.

HBV has no NS5A protein, so there is no direct mechanistic link between velpatasvir and HBV. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects shared "viral hepatitis" proximity in the knowledge graph rather than a real antiviral effect.

The clinically relevant connection is narrower. In patients co-infected with HCV and HBV, treating HCV with direct-acting antivirals can trigger HBV reactivation. One retrieved case report describes such a reactivation during sofosbuvir/velpatasvir therapy. The practical question is therefore how to manage HBV during HCV treatment, not whether velpatasvir treats HBV.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 57 interactions were recorded. Those listed are 20 of the 57.
  - **Major**: dexamethasone and the proton pump inhibitors dexlansoprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, omeprazole, pantoprazole and rabeprazole.
  - **Moderate**: antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magaldrate, magnesium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium oxide), H2 blockers (cimetidine, famotidine, nizatidine) and naldemedine.
  - **Minor**: fidaxomicin, naloxegol and prucalopride.
- **HBV reactivation**: The literature includes a report of HBV reactivation during SOF/VEL treatment in an anti-HBc-positive patient. Co-infected patients need HBV monitoring or prophylaxis.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Velpatasvir targets HCV NS5A, which HBV lacks, and none of the retrieved trials or publications tests it against HBV. The high TxGNN score appears to reflect network proximity among viral hepatitis diseases. The evidence supports only managing HBV reactivation risk during HCV therapy. Both EU authorizations are also cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and in vitro evidence of any velpatasvir activity against HBV
- Results of NCT04997564 on HBV reactivation outcomes in HCV/HBV co-infection
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC
- Clarification of the current EU regulatory status, given that both authorizations are cancelled
