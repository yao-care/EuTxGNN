# Tyrosine: From Maple Syrup Urine Disease (Acute Decompensation) to Cauda Equina Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Tyrosine (marketed in the EU as Maapliv) was originally authorised for acute decompensation episodes of maple syrup urine disease (MSUD) in patients who cannot use an oral or enteral BCAA-free formulation.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **cauda equina syndrome**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, so it is a computational hypothesis only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Tyrosine is an amino acid and a precursor of catecholamines (dopamine, norepinephrine) and thyroid hormones. Its role in MSUD relates to nutritional and metabolic support during acute decompensation.

The data compiled for this report do not support a mechanistic link between tyrosine and cauda equina syndrome. That condition involves compression of the lumbosacral nerve roots, causing radiculopathy and bladder, bowel and sphincter dysfunction. The high TxGNN score (99.77%) is a computational prediction only. No supporting trials or literature were found, and tyrosine's role as a catecholamine precursor has no established relevance to this condition.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for cauda equina syndrome rests only on a model score, with no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism. The only authorisation is cancelled, and safety data are missing. The other top-ranked predictions (thyroid-related and glaucoma conditions) also lack tyrosine-specific evidence, and some are mechanistically counterproductive, such as supplementing a thyroid hormone precursor in hyperthyroidism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the EMA product information
- Mechanism of action data for tyrosine
- A biological rationale linking tyrosine to cauda equina syndrome, or a review of other predicted indications for more plausible candidates
- Preclinical or exploratory clinical evidence of benefit
- Clarification of why the Maapliv authorisation was cancelled, and of the route of administration, since the product was intended for acute use in patients who cannot take enteral formulations
