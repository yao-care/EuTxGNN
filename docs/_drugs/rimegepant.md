# Rimegepant: From Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

Rimegepant is a CGRP receptor antagonist, originally used for the acute treatment of migraine and the prevention of episodic migraine in adults.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the published literature, rimegepant is a small-molecule CGRP (calcitonin gene-related peptide) receptor antagonist. It is approved for the acute treatment of migraine with or without aura, and for the preventive treatment of episodic migraine.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a subtype of migraine. The CGRP-pathway rationale is therefore strong. Arguably, this is a subtype within the existing label rather than a true repurposing indication. The gepant mechanism is non-vasoconstrictive. This may be an advantage over triptans, which have historically been used with caution in this subtype. A small imaging study in migraine patients (PMID 41574090) examined rimegepant's effect on cerebral and extracerebral arteries. This is consistent with the mechanism but does not address this subtype.

None of the retrieved publications presents data specific to brainstem aura. The evidence is indirect, extrapolated from the approved migraine indication. The subtype-specific efficacy and safety of rimegepant need to be verified.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screening identified 57 interacting drugs. The main ones in the data compiled for this report are listed below.
  - **Major**: dexamethasone, lumacaftor, apalutamide
  - **Moderate** (examples): aprepitant, clarithromycin, erythromycin, fluconazole, ketoconazole, rolapitant, cobicistat, tacrolimus, chloramphenicol, sulfasalazine, eltrombopag, ivacaftor, abiraterone, afatinib, alpelisib
- **Cardiovascular and vascular signals**: CGRP is a potent vasodilator. A pharmacovigilance analysis of CGRP antagonists (PMID 41706150) raised cardiac safety signals. This calls for caution in patients with vascular disease, although the evidence is of low strength.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The CGRP-pathway rationale is sound, since brainstem aura is a migraine subtype. However, no trials or subtype-specific literature exist. The only authorization is cancelled, and the label's warnings and contraindications could not be reviewed. Other predicted indications (for example thrombophilia, Prinzmetal angina and familial hypercholesterolemia) have no supporting evidence, and some point to safety concerns rather than benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC. This is currently a blocking gap.
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Subtype-specific evidence in brainstem aura, such as post-hoc analyses of existing migraine trials or case series.
- Clarification of why the EU authorization was cancelled and of the current regulatory status.
- Review of vascular safety, particularly in patients with cardiovascular risk.
