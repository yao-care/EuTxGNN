# Exenatide: From Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus to Primary Hereditary Glaucoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Exenatide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist originally used to improve blood sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary hereditary glaucoma**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction.
The top-ranked prediction rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, exenatide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist, and its efficacy in type 2 diabetes has been established. Whether this mechanism applies to primary hereditary glaucoma is unconfirmed.

Preclinical work suggests that GLP-1 receptor agonism may protect retinal ganglion cells (neuroprotection). This is a possible rationale, but the data compiled for this report contain nothing that supports it, and it has not been validated for hereditary glaucoma. The relationship between diabetes and glaucoma is therefore currently only a model-derived association.

The second-ranked prediction, open-angle glaucoma (score 95.44%), is also prediction-only, with no trials or literature. Other predictions in the top ten, including hair-related conditions, headache disorder, and the common cold, show no plausible mechanistic link in the available data.

One notable finding concerns **alopecia** (rank 8, score 90.20%). The five publications found, including two systematic reviews and pharmacovigilance analyses, describe hair loss as a possible **adverse effect** of GLP-1 receptor agonists, probably related to rapid weight loss or nutritional deficiency. This signal contradicts the predicted benefit, and the data concern the drug class rather than exenatide specifically.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by a model score (L5), with no clinical trials or literature for glaucoma. In addition, no EU authorization for exenatide is currently valid, and the related alopecia literature points to potential harm rather than benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Preclinical and mechanistic studies of GLP-1 receptor agonism in retinal neuroprotection and glaucoma models
- Mechanism of action (MOA) data for exenatide
- Label warnings and contraindications from the EU product information, for safety screening
- Review of the cancelled authorization status and whether any route of administration suits ocular use
- Literature search targeted at exenatide and GLP-1 receptor agonists in glaucoma
