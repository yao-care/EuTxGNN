## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Pharmacology records show that afamelanotide is a peptide ligand acting on melanocortin receptors (MC1R, MC3R, MC4R and MC5R). It is an analogue of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone. The literature describes its MC1R activity as increasing eumelanin production in melanocytes, which gives photoprotection.

EPP and X-linked protoporphyria (XLP) have the same clinical picture. In both, protoporphyrin IX accumulates and causes painful phototoxic reactions on sun exposure. EPP is caused by ferrochelatase deficiency, whereas XLP is caused by gain-of-function mutations in ALAS2. Because the disease phenotype is shared, the photoprotective effect of afamelanotide is expected to carry over. One cohort study (PMID 38929673) evaluated afamelanotide across both protoporphyrias.

**Note on the evidence level:** the data compiled for this report list no clinical trials for XLP. The L1 grade in the source data depends on Phase 3 RCTs in EPP that are not among the supplied records. Based only on the supplied records (reviews, a systematic review and cohort studies), this report assigns **L3**.

## Safety Considerations

Pharmacology records show afamelanotide acts at several melanocortin receptors (MC1R, MC3R, MC4R and MC5R). These are receptor-binding records, not drug-drug interaction findings, and no interaction severity levels are available.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
XLP has the same phototoxic phenotype as EPP, and several publications address afamelanotide in both conditions. However, there are no XLP-specific trial records, the evidence is mostly reviews and cohorts, and the EU authorisation is cancelled. Development should therefore proceed cautiously.
