# Nemolizumab: From Atopic Dermatitis and Prurigo Nodularis to Diabetic Cataract

Nemolizumab is an anti-IL-31 receptor alpha antibody, originally used to treat moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis and prurigo nodularis.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data from the original drug record is not available. Based on known information, nemolizumab is a monoclonal antibody that targets the IL-31 receptor alpha subunit (IL31RA) and blocks IL-31 signaling. This signaling mainly drives itch and type 2 inflammatory skin responses, which explains its use in atopic dermatitis and prurigo nodularis.

The link to diabetic cataract is weak. Diabetic cataract develops mainly through hyperglycaemia-driven polyol pathway activity, oxidative stress and glycation in the lens. No established connection exists between IL-31 signalling and these processes. The high score most likely reflects the knowledge graph's clustering of cataract-related diseases, not a biological rationale.

Atopic dermatitis is itself associated with atopic cataract. Any cataract signal may therefore reflect overlap between patient populations rather than a therapeutic effect. It should be examined as a safety question, not treated as an efficacy hypothesis.

Nine other cataract-type predictions (tetanic, immature, craniostenosis, mature, type 2 diabetes-associated, cortical, nuclear senile and senile cataract) and diabetic retinopathy also scored between 98.2% and 98.5%. All of them have the same L5 evidence level, no trials and no literature, and none has a plausible mechanism linked to IL-31 blockade.
