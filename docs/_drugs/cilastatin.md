## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. What is known is that cilastatin inhibits renal dehydropeptidase-I, the enzyme that degrades imipenem in the kidney. It has no antibacterial activity of its own.

Any anti-staphylococcal effect therefore comes from the partner carbapenem, imipenem. Older reports describe imipenem-cilastatin activity against methicillin-susceptible and methicillin-resistant *S. aureus*. Examples are a 23-patient series from 1986, animal endocarditis work, and a recent case commentary on imipenem-cilastatin plus fosfomycin. This supports the combination, not cilastatin as a repurposed single agent.

The model's score is therefore best read as a reflection of the combination's antibacterial use. It is not evidence of a cilastatin-specific effect.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level contraindication signals** (from the model's drug-disease data, not label text): epilepsy, kidney disease, pyoureter, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis.
- **Drug Interactions**: The only recorded interaction is pharmacological: cilastatin inhibits its target, dipeptidase 1, and is used in fixed-dose formulations with imipenem.

Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The retrieved studies address the imipenem-cilastatin combination or unrelated antibiotics, and none directly studies cilastatin as a single agent in *S. aureus* infection. The anti-staphylococcal activity comes from imipenem, and cilastatin only extends its exposure. The evidence level is therefore L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A study that separates cilastatin's contribution from imipenem's in *S. aureus* infection
- Mechanism-of-action data for cilastatin
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
