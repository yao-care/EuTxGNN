## Cilastatin: Staphylococcus aureus infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Cilastatin is a renal dehydropeptidase-I inhibitor that is given together with the carbapenem antibiotic imipenem to protect it from breakdown in the kidney. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant for **staphylococcus aureus infection**, with a score of 0.9994. The supporting evidence is **1 clinical trial** and **20 publications**, but this evidence concerns the imipenem-cilastatin combination, and the evidence level is **L3**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Cilastatin inhibits dehydropeptidase-I in the kidney, which prevents imipenem from being degraded there. It has no antibacterial activity of its own. Any effect against Staphylococcus aureus therefore comes from imipenem, and cilastatin only keeps imipenem exposure higher.

Older clinical reports show that imipenem-cilastatin was active against methicillin-sensitive and methicillin-resistant S. aureus. A 1986 clinical study treated 23 patients, including soft tissue, endovascular and skeletal infections and bacteraemia. This supports the combination, not cilastatin as a repurposed single agent. Detailed mechanism-of-action data for cilastatin are not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The only recorded item is pharmacological: cilastatin inhibits its target, dipeptidase 1 (dehydropeptidase-I). This is the basis of its use with imipenem.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings, contraindications and other safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L3. It rests on small, mostly older, uncontrolled studies of imipenem-cilastatin, and there is no randomized trial. The activity against S. aureus comes from imipenem, and cilastatin has no antibacterial effect of its own. These data therefore do not support cilastatin as a repurposed agent.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled studies that separate the effect of cilastatin from that of imipenem
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data for cilastatin
- Review of the SmPC warnings and contraindications
