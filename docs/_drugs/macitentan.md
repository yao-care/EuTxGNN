## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Macitentan is an endothelin receptor antagonist. Its efficacy in PAH is established, and it acts on endothelin-mediated pulmonary vascular tone. Mechanistically, it might be applicable to other pulmonary vascular diseases.

The link to pulmonary arteriovenous malformation (AVM) is weak, however. Pulmonary AVMs are structural vascular lesions, commonly associated with hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia (HHT). They are driven mainly by other pathways, such as VEGF and TGF-beta signalling, rather than by endothelin-mediated vasoconstriction. The high TxGNN score is therefore best read as a network-level similarity signal, not as evidence of clinical benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but there are no clinical trials or publications. The mechanistic link is weak, because pulmonary AVMs are structural lesions driven by pathways different from endothelin signalling. Evidence is at the model-prediction level (L5), and the EU authorizations are all cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A literature search and mechanistic review for endothelin receptor antagonism in pulmonary AVM and HHT-related disease
- Preclinical or exploratory clinical data showing a plausible effect on AVM
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA) and the full SmPC warnings and contraindications
- A drug-interaction and safety plan, covering CYP3A4-related interactions and embryo-fetal toxicity precautions
- Clarification of the regulatory and market situation, since all three EU authorizations are cancelled

For context, other TxGNN predictions for macitentan fall within the PAH label and have more supporting literature. These include PAH associated with congenital heart disease and with connective tissue disease. They may be better candidates for further evaluation than pulmonary AVM.
