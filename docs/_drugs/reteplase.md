# Reteplase: From Acute ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction to Posterolateral Myocardial Infarction

## One-Sentence Summary

Reteplase is a thrombolytic (clot-dissolving) drug, originally used to treat acute myocardial infarction with persistent ST elevation.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **posterolateral myocardial infarction**, but this is largely a subtype of the already-approved indication.
Currently there are **0 registered clinical trials** and **1 publication** (a case report) directly supporting this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, reteplase is a recombinant tissue plasminogen activator that dissolves fibrin clots in blocked coronary arteries. Its efficacy in ST-elevation myocardial infarction is established.

Posterolateral myocardial infarction is an anatomical subtype of acute myocardial infarction, so the same thrombolytic mechanism applies directly. This is therefore largely a subset of the existing label rather than true repurposing. The high prediction score likely reflects how close this disease is to the approved indication in the knowledge graph.

The only retrieved paper is a case report of percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) of an unprotected left main artery, facilitated by intracoronary reteplase. It does not provide reteplase-specific efficacy evidence for this subtype.

Other predictions in the list vary widely in plausibility:
- **Posteroinferior and septal myocardial infarction** are also MI subtypes and mechanistically consistent, but evidence is absent or limited to case reports.
- **Coronary stenosis** has the most literature: two reteplase-specific clinical studies (GUSTO-V and SPEED) in MI populations, but they do not study stenosis as a separate indication.
- **Congenital coronary artery anomaly, thrombophilias, thalassemia, and chromosomal or enzyme disorders** show no plausible direct link and appear to be graph artifacts.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication is essentially a subtype of the approved ST-elevation MI indication. No registered trials exist, and the only supporting paper is an indirect case report. The sole EU authorization is cancelled, and label safety data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety information from the SmPC (warnings and contraindications), currently a blocking gap
- Mechanism of action data
- Clarification of whether posterolateral MI is already covered by the existing STEMI label, which would make this a label-interpretation question rather than repurposing
- Confirmation of the current regulatory status of reteplase in the EU, given the cancelled authorization
- Reteplase-specific clinical evidence for the specific MI subtype, such as subgroup analyses of existing STEMI trials (e.g., GUSTO-V)
