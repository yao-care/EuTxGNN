# Dronedarone: From Atrial Fibrillation to Stroke Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Dronedarone is an antiarrhythmic drug originally used to maintain sinus rhythm after cardioversion in patients with paroxysmal or persistent atrial fibrillation (AF).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **stroke disorder**, but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** for this use and **20 publications** that support the direction only indirectly (mainly AF populations and post-hoc analyses).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. From the literature, dronedarone is a multichannel-blocking antiarrhythmic (inhibiting sodium, potassium and calcium currents) and a non-iodinated structural analogue of amiodarone.

AF is a major cause of cardioembolic stroke. By helping maintain sinus rhythm, dronedarone could reduce thromboembolic risk indirectly. Post-hoc analyses of the ATHENA trial, and a meta-analysis built on them, reported a lower stroke incidence in patients with paroxysmal or persistent AF. Laboratory work has also described possible protective effects against ischemia/reperfusion injury in heart and brain, and one study reported anticoagulant and antiplatelet effects independent of the antiarrhythmic action.

There is an important counterpoint. In the PALLAS trial in high-risk permanent AF, dronedarone increased major cardiovascular events, including stroke. The stroke benefit is therefore hypothetical, limited to specific AF populations, and has not been tested in a trial where stroke is the primary endpoint.

## Cytotoxicity

Not applicable. This section is omitted because dronedarone is not an antineoplastic drug.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from a knowledge graph, not label text)**: long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, liver failure, potassium deficiency disease, atrioventricular block, sick sinus syndrome (including familial).
- **Population limits from the approved indication and literature**: should not be given to patients with left ventricular systolic dysfunction or current or previous heart failure; PALLAS showed harm in high-risk permanent AF.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified in total; the 20 listed in the data compiled for this report include these major ones: hydrocortisone, amphotericin B (several formulations), loperamide, clarithromycin, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol (3350 with electrolytes) and dolasetron. Moderate interactions include famotidine, alvimopan, aprepitant, dexamethasone, bisacodyl, budesonide, cimetidine, saxagliptin and castor oil. Several of these may add to QT-prolongation or electrolyte-disturbance risk. Interactions with anticoagulants such as DOACs are also relevant in stroke prevention.

Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the SmPC for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is indirect (AF populations, post-hoc analyses, preclinical work), with no trial targeting stroke. The PALLAS result, the heart failure restrictions, the QT and conduction-related contraindications and the many interactions weigh against moving forward now. Dronedarone's only EU authorization is cancelled. The other predicted indications are prediction-only, and several (for example ABri amyloidosis, duodenal obstruction, Wildervanck syndrome) have no plausible mechanistic link.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the EMA product information (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A dedicated study, ideally a randomized trial or well-controlled cohort, with stroke as the primary endpoint in clearly defined paroxysmal or persistent AF populations
- A review of whether stroke reduction exceeds the risks seen in PALLAS, together with a DOAC co-prescription safety plan
- Clarification of the current EU regulatory status, since the single authorization is cancelled
