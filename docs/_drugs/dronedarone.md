## Dronedarone: Stroke disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Dronedarone is a multichannel-blocking antiarrhythmic used to maintain sinus rhythm in adults with atrial fibrillation.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant for **stroke disorder**, but no clinical trials are registered and no study tests dronedarone for stroke itself. The 20 publications compiled are about atrial fibrillation populations or other mechanisms, so the evidence level is **L4**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, dronedarone blocks several ion channels (sodium, potassium and calcium currents) and has rate- and rhythm-controlling properties. Its clinical use is in atrial fibrillation (AF).

AF is a major cause of cardioembolic stroke. Any benefit in stroke would most likely be indirect, through maintaining sinus rhythm and reducing thromboembolic risk. Post-hoc analyses of the ATHENA trial reported a lower stroke incidence in patients with paroxysmal or persistent AF. A preclinical study (PMID 28992468) also reported anticoagulant and antiplatelet effects that are independent of the antiarrhythmic action, and another review (PMID 22366819) discusses possible protection against ischaemia/reperfusion injury in heart and brain.

There is an important counterpoint: in high-risk permanent AF (the PALLAS trial, PMID 22082198), dronedarone was associated with excess cardiovascular events, which limits the population in which this idea could be considered.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications recorded for related conditions** (relations not drawn from label text): long QT syndrome, familial long QT syndrome, torsades de pointes, liver failure, potassium deficiency disease, atrioventricular block, sick sinus syndrome.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 recorded; main ones):
  - Major: hydrocortisone, amphotericin B (including lipid complex, cholesteryl sulfate and liposomal forms), loperamide, clarithromycin, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol (3350 with electrolytes), dolasetron.
  - Moderate: famotidine, alvimopan, aprepitant, dexamethasone, bisacodyl, budesonide, cimetidine, saxagliptin, castor oil.
- Published literature also highlights interactions with direct oral anticoagulants (PMID 41152878), which is directly relevant when stroke prevention is the goal.

Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high (0.9997), but no study tests dronedarone for stroke. Stroke appears only as an outcome in AF studies, and the PALLAS trial showed excess cardiovascular events in permanent AF. Evidence level is L4, and the safety profile limits the eligible population.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A study with stroke as the primary endpoint in a clearly defined AF population
- Clarification of the mechanism of action, including the reported anticoagulant and antiplatelet effects
- Review of label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, especially heart failure and permanent AF
- An interaction plan for patients also taking anticoagulants
