Histidine (marketed as Maapliv) was originally authorized in the EU for acute decompensation episodes of maple syrup urine disease (MSUD).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Histidine is an amino acid, and its authorized use in MSUD is as part of parenteral amino acid nutrition during acute metabolic decompensation. That use is nutritional and metabolic, and it has no direct connection to gastric motility.

Any link to gastroparesis is speculative. One possible route is histidine's role as the precursor of histamine, which can influence gastric secretion and motility. No study retrieved for this report tests this idea. The only publication found concerns gastric amyloid pathology and mucosal denervation in hereditary ATTR amyloidosis, and it does not involve histidine or treatment of gastroparesis.

The very high TxGNN score (rank 5664) reflects a pattern in the knowledge graph, not confirmed biological or clinical support.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but there is no clinical trial, no direct preclinical study, and no mechanistic data linking histidine to gastroparesis. The only retrieved publication is unrelated to histidine therapy. The EU authorization is also cancelled, so the drug has no active EU marketing status.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for histidine, and a plausible mechanistic hypothesis for gastric motility
- Preclinical studies of histidine or histamine-pathway modulation in gastroparesis models
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications, to complete safety screening
- Clarification of the knowledge-graph contraindication signals, especially renal and cardiovascular conditions, which are common in patients with gastroparesis
- Confirmation of why the EU authorization was cancelled, and of its regulatory implications
