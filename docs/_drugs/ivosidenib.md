## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for ivosidenib: "Ivosidenib is a small molecule inhibitor that targets the mutant isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (IDH1) enzyme. In patients with AML, susceptible IDH1 mutations are defined as those leading to increased levels of 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG) in the leukemia cells and where efficacy is predicted by 1) clinically meaningful remissions with the recommended dose of ivosidenib and/or 2) inhibition of mutant IDH1 enzymatic activity at concentrations of ivosidenib sustainable at the recommended dosage according to validated methods." ([DailyMed: Tibsovo (ivosidenib) prescribing information, Servier Pharmaceutical LLC](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=65d254c0-67ad-42c4-b972-ad463b755b2d), label version 2025-12-22; accessed 2026-11-19.) From general knowledge, ivosidenib inhibits the mutant IDH1 (R132) enzyme, which is relevant to certain cancers.

Bulbar polio is a viral infection that damages motor neurons in the brainstem. No plausible mechanistic link between mutant IDH1 inhibition and a viral infection of motor neurons could be identified. The high score (0.9931) is a model output without any supporting clinical or literature evidence, and it is most likely a model artifact rather than a true therapeutic signal.
