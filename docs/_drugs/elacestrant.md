## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Elacestrant is known to be an oral selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) that antagonizes and degrades ER-alpha. Its efficacy in ER-positive breast cancer comes from blocking estrogen-driven tumour growth.

Amenorrhea reflects dysregulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis, which involves estrogen feedback. Blocking ER signaling could theoretically alter this feedback. However, it would more plausibly cause or worsen menstrual disruption than treat it. The high TxGNN score is a knowledge-graph prediction only, with no clinical or literature support, and the direction of effect is doubtful.

Other top-ranked predictions are also speculative and lack any supporting studies. They include bone Paget disease, multiple endocrine neoplasia, primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, seborrheic dermatitis, candidiasis and several rare hormonal or developmental disorders. Most appear to arise from shared hormone-pathway nodes in the knowledge graph. Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism has a theoretical feedback-disinhibition rationale, as with clomiphene, but it is not supported by evidence for elacestrant.
