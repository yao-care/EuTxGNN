# Raloxifene: From Postmenopausal Osteoporosis to Duodenal Ulcer

## One-Sentence Summary

Raloxifene is a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), originally used to treat and prevent osteoporosis in postmenopausal women.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, raloxifene is a selective estrogen receptor modulator that acts on bone and breast tissue. Its efficacy in postmenopausal osteoporosis is established, with a significant reduction in vertebral fractures (but not hip fractures).

No clear mechanistic link to duodenal ulcer can be established from the data compiled for this report. Duodenal ulcer is driven mainly by acid secretion, *H. pylori* infection and weakened mucosal defence, and none of these is a known target of estrogen receptor modulation. The very high TxGNN score (0.997) reflects proximity in the knowledge graph, not demonstrated pharmacology. Several other predicted conditions with similar scores (duodenal obstruction, duodenogastric reflux) appear to share this graph-proximity pattern, which suggests an artifact.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (disease-drug links in the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text): hypertensive disorder, hypertension, liver disease, gallbladder disease, atrial fibrillation, hypertriglyceridemia, thrombophlebitis, coronary artery disease, stroke disorder, cerebrovascular disorder, cerebral infarction, pulmonary embolism, retinal artery occlusion, thrombophilia, impaired renal function, transient ischemic attack, vertebrobasilar insufficiency, antiphospholipid syndrome. Many of these are thromboembolic or cardiovascular conditions, consistent with the known venous thromboembolism risk of raloxifene.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs, and the severity level is not classified for any of them. Examples include calcitriol, pantoprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, glimepiride, metformin, simvastatin, doxycycline, tetracycline, morphine and sucralfate. Note that proton pump inhibitors and sucralfate are commonly used in duodenal ulcer, so any real co-use would need review.
- **Reproductive safety**: Animal studies (rats and rabbits) were conducted on developmental toxicity. Raloxifene is intended for postmenopausal women and is not appropriate in pregnancy.

Please refer to the SmPC for the full warnings and contraindications, as label-level data were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by a graph-based model score, with no clinical trials, no literature and no plausible mechanistic link to duodenal ulcer. The product is also no longer authorized in the EU. The other nine predicted indications are likewise L5 and are at best loosely linked, for example lipid disorders. The closest, pregnancy-associated osteoporosis, applies to a population in which raloxifene is not suitable.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for raloxifene (currently missing) and a defined biological rationale for duodenal ulcer
- Label warnings and contraindications from the EMA package insert
- Preclinical or observational evidence of effect in duodenal ulcer or related gastric mucosal models
- Clarification of the cancelled EU status, including whether other marketing routes exist
- A benefit-risk review, given the thromboembolic and cardiovascular safety signals
