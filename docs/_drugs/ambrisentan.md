## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ambrisentan is an endothelin type A (ETA) receptor antagonist. Blocking this receptor relaxes and remodels pulmonary blood vessels, which is why it is used for conditions of raised pressure in the lung's arteries. Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the data compiled for this report.

The link to pulmonary arteriovenous malformation (AVM) is weak. The only supporting paper is a case report of pulmonary arterial hypertension in a patient with hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (HHT), a disease in which pulmonary AVMs are common. That report reflects a shared genetic pathway (BMPR2/ACVRL1) between HHT and pulmonary arterial hypertension. It does not show that ambrisentan treats pulmonary AVMs themselves.

Pulmonary AVMs are structural vascular shunts, usually managed by embolization, and endothelin blockade has no established role. The high score (0.9941) is therefore not backed by clinical evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no trial studies this disease and the single publication is a case report on a related condition (PAH in HHT). Endothelin blockade has no established role in structural pulmonary AVMs, so the evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Studies that directly assess ambrisentan in pulmonary AVM, for example in patients with HHT
- Mechanism-of-action data explaining a possible benefit in this condition
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications for a safety review, including teratogenicity
