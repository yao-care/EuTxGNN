# Rivaroxaban: From Venous Thromboembolism to Rheumatoid Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Rivaroxaban is an oral Factor Xa inhibitor, originally used to prevent and treat venous thromboembolism and to prevent stroke in atrial fibrillation.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Rheumatoid Arthritis**,
but **no clinical trials** and only **4 publications** (none testing rivaroxaban in rheumatoid arthritis) are available, so this prediction rests almost entirely on the model score.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, rivaroxaban is a direct oral anticoagulant that acts on coagulation Factor Xa. Its efficacy in venous thromboembolism and atrial fibrillation is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to rheumatoid arthritis.

The link is speculative. Thrombin generation is altered in autoimmune disease, and coagulation and inflammation influence each other, so inhibiting Factor Xa could plausibly modulate inflammation. However, none of the studies compiled for this report tests rivaroxaban as a treatment for rheumatoid arthritis. The literature deals with VTE, anticoagulant adherence and thrombosis, which are the drug's original uses. The high prediction score is therefore not backed by clinical data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text)**: liver failure, kidney disease, pyoureter, liver disease, gallbladder disease, blood coagulation disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major-level examples include acetylsalicylic acid, cobicistat, deferasirox, ibritumomab tiuxetan and tositumomab (I-131). Moderate-level examples include aprepitant, dexamethasone, cimetidine, clarithromycin, miconazole, clotrimazole and eliglustat. Bleeding risk with aspirin deserves particular attention, since rheumatoid arthritis patients often take NSAIDs, aspirin or corticosteroids.

Please also refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but there are no clinical trials and no study testing rivaroxaban in rheumatoid arthritis (Evidence Level L5). The mechanistic link is speculative, and the bleeding risk and the many interactions, including with commonly used anti-inflammatory drugs, add concern. In addition, all EU authorizations are currently cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a clearer rationale for Factor Xa inhibition in rheumatoid arthritis
- Preclinical or early-phase studies of efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Clarification of EU regulatory status and of which authorized product would be used
- A bleeding-risk and interaction assessment for rheumatoid arthritis co-medications (NSAIDs, corticosteroids, aspirin)
