# Iloprost: From Primary Pulmonary Hypertension to Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension Associated with Congenital Heart Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Iloprost is a prostacyclin analogue, originally authorised for primary pulmonary hypertension (NYHA functional class III).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary arterial hypertension associated with congenital heart disease (PAH-CHD)**.
The data compiled for this report include **no registered clinical trials** but **20 publications**, mostly observational studies, case series and reviews.

> Note: TxGNN's top-ranked prediction is "hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp" (score 99.45%), but it has no trial or literature support and is not mechanistically plausible. This report therefore focuses on the best-supported prediction, PAH-CHD (rank 3).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, iloprost is a stable prostacyclin (PGI2) analogue. It acts on IP receptors to cause pulmonary vasodilation, inhibit platelet aggregation, and exert antiproliferative effects on vascular smooth muscle.

The original indication (primary/idiopathic pulmonary hypertension) and the predicted indication (PAH associated with congenital heart disease, including Eisenmenger syndrome) belong to the same disease family, Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension. They share obstructive changes in the pulmonary microvasculature and similar pathobiology. This is therefore an extension within one disease family rather than a distant repurposing.

The supporting evidence is observational: biomarker studies, case series, paediatric acute-response studies and reviews. No Phase 3 RCT is documented.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SmPC for safety information. Label warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report.

- **Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph (not label text)**: liver failure, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (including severe early-onset COPD), hypotensive disorders, pulmonary oedema, and bronchial disease. These should be verified against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query returned 100 records. The main ones listed are Moderate, including epinephrine, acetylsalicylic acid (additive antiplatelet and bleeding considerations), the SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin), inhaled human insulin, mannitol, ephedrine, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, glycerin, papaverine and sibutramine. Interactions with calcitriol, pantoprazole, morphine, metformin and omeprazole are of unknown severity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Iloprost has a coherent mechanistic fit with PAH-CHD, and consistent observational and pilot evidence, including a placebo-controlled cross-over pilot study in Eisenmenger syndrome. However, evidence is limited to L3 with no registered trials or Phase 3 RCT. The EU authorisation is cancelled, and label safety data could not be obtained.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieval and review of the SmPC (warnings and contraindications), the most important blocking gap
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A prospective, adequately powered controlled study in PAH-CHD/Eisenmenger syndrome, with paediatric-specific evaluation
- Clarification of the regulatory status and reasons for cancellation of the EU authorisation, and the route and formulation (inhaled versus intravenous) needed
- A safety plan covering hypotension, bleeding risk with antiplatelet agents, and the knowledge-graph contraindication signals

**Other predictions (for reference):**
- **PAH associated with connective tissue disease (rank 7, L3)**: Proceed with Guardrails. Supported by a long-term cohort of intravenous iloprost and several reviews.
- **PAH associated with HIV (rank 8, L4)**, **chronic haemolytic anaemia (rank 5, L5)** and **schistosomiasis (rank 6, L4)**: Research Question. Evidence is indirect or absent.
- **Hypotrichosis simplex, congenital hypotrichosis milia, pulmonary arteriovenous malformation, diffuse alopecia areata and alopecia (L5)**: Hold. There is no supporting evidence and no credible mechanistic link. Vasodilation could theoretically worsen shunting in pulmonary arteriovenous malformation.
