# Selpercatinib: From RET-Altered Cancers to Pulmonary Hypertension

## One-Sentence Summary

Selpercatinib is a selective RET kinase inhibitor, originally used to treat RET fusion-positive lung cancer, RET-altered thyroid cancer and other RET fusion-positive solid tumours.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary hypertension**, but **no clinical trials** and only **3 publications** were found, none of which studies pulmonary hypertension as a treatment target.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, selpercatinib is a selective RET kinase inhibitor, and its efficacy is established in RET-driven cancers. No data link RET signalling to the development of pulmonary hypertension.

The original indications (RET-driven tumours) and pulmonary hypertension are biologically very different. Vascular remodelling in pulmonary arterial hypertension involves other pathways, such as BMPR2 and PDGFR. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors have been explored in this setting (for example imatinib, which targets PDGFR), but that rationale does not carry over to selective RET inhibition.

The high TxGNN score (0.992) reflects knowledge-graph patterns rather than demonstrated biology. The three retrieved publications concern cancer treatment and adverse-event profiling, so they do not support efficacy in pulmonary hypertension. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by the model score (evidence level L5). There are no clinical trials, no relevant literature and no plausible mechanistic link between RET inhibition and pulmonary hypertension. The only EU authorization has been cancelled, and the interaction burden is substantial.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Preclinical evidence that RET signalling contributes to pulmonary vascular remodelling
- Mechanism of action data (MOA) and a documented rationale linking it to pulmonary hypertension
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, to assess safety in this population (especially hypertension)
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorization status
