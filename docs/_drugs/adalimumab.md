# Adalimumab: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Adalimumab is an anti-TNF-alpha antibody, originally authorised for rheumatoid arthritis and a range of other inflammatory diseases. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid vasculitis**, but this direction is supported by only **1 tangentially related clinical trial** and a small set of publications, mostly case reports with conflicting directions of effect.

Note: the highest-ranked prediction is the one this report centres on. Among the other predictions, inflammatory spondylopathy and polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis have much stronger evidence, but both are already labelled indications (see the Conclusion).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Adalimumab is known to be a fully human monoclonal antibody that neutralises TNF-alpha, a central driver of inflammation in rheumatoid arthritis.

Rheumatoid vasculitis arises in the setting of long-standing rheumatoid arthritis, so a shared TNF-driven inflammatory pathway offers a plausible rationale. However, the picture is mixed: TNF inhibitors, including adalimumab, have also been reported to induce vasculitis paradoxically. The available evidence consists mainly of case reports pointing in opposite directions, so the mechanistic argument is not yet supported by robust clinical data.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions identified; main ones listed):
- **Major:** corticosteroids (betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, triamcinolone including ophthalmic), deferiprone, ibritumomab tiuxetan, iobenguane (I-131), tositumomab and tositumomab (I-131)
- **Moderate:** metronidazole, tinidazole, rosuvastatin, simvastatin
- **Minor:** zinc acetate, zinc gluconate, zinc sulfate

Published reports also describe vasculitis-like and lupus-like events during TNF-inhibitor therapy, which is directly relevant to this predicted indication. For other warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but direct evidence for rheumatoid vasculitis is limited to case reports with conflicting directions of effect, and the one registered trial is not about this question. In addition, all EU authorisations are currently cancelled.

**Other predictions in the data (for context):**
- **Inflammatory spondylopathy** (L1, Proceed with Guardrails): supported by multiple completed Phase 3 trials, but this is essentially an existing labelled indication (ankylosing spondylitis and axial spondyloarthritis) rather than true repurposing.
- **Polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis** (L1, Proceed with Guardrails): supported by a placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial and a pivotal RCT, but is also already a labelled indication.
- **Vertebral disease** (L4, Hold): the term is too broad; evidence is indirect and the disease term needs narrowing.
- **Hypermobility of coccyx, Kummell disease, and polyps of the middle ear, vocal cord, ureter and frontal sinus** (L5, Hold): no mechanistic rationale or clinical evidence; these are model predictions only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled or prospective cohort data on adalimumab in rheumatoid vasculitis, including how to reconcile reports of benefit with TNF-inhibitor-associated vasculitis
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Full warnings and contraindications from the SmPC (currently unavailable)
- Clarification of the current EU regulatory status, since all authorisations are cancelled
