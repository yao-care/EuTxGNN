## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Riluzole is generally known to modulate glutamatergic signaling, which underlies its use in ALS, a disease in which glutamate excitotoxicity is thought to contribute to motor neuron injury.

The data compiled for this report do not link this mechanism to polymicrogyria, a developmental malformation of the cerebral cortex. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only, and the data provide no mechanistic, clinical or literature support for it.

**Other predicted indications.** The model also lists several diseases that are biologically closer to ALS:
- **ALS, susceptibility to** (rank 8): This corresponds to riluzole's approved indication, so it is not a true repurposing signal. The supplied literature is mostly reviews and mechanistic papers, including a discussion of the glutamate hypothesis. It contains no trial data.
- **ALS type 22** (rank 10): A genetic ALS subtype. Riluzole's ALS label makes this plausible, but no subtype-specific evidence was supplied.
- **Other motor neuron conditions** (lower motor neuron syndrome, monomelic amyotrophy, Mills syndrome, lethal arthrogryposis–anterior horn cell disease syndrome): These are conceivable on a motor neuron rationale. The label states that riluzole was studied only in ALS and should not be used in other forms of motor neuron disease.
- **Remaining predictions** (e.g., axial spondylometaphyseal dysplasia, trichomegaly–retinal pigmentary degeneration–dwarfism syndrome, autosomal dominant mitochondrial myopathy): No plausible mechanism is evident from the data.
