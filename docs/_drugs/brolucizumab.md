# Brolucizumab: From Neovascular (Wet) Age-Related Macular Degeneration to Mitochondrial Oxidative Phosphorylation Disorder Due to Nuclear DNA Anomalies

## One-Sentence Summary

Brolucizumab is an anti-VEGF-A antibody fragment, originally used to treat neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration (AMD).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation disorder due to nuclear DNA anomalies**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, brolucizumab is a single-chain antibody fragment that targets VEGF-A. Its efficacy in wet AMD comes from blocking VEGF-driven abnormal blood vessel growth and leakage in the retina.

The link to the predicted indication is weak. Mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation disorders are not known to be driven by VEGF-A signaling, so any systemic or retinal benefit is speculative. The high TxGNN score (99.67%) comes from a graph-based model and is not backed by any trial or publication.

Other predictions for this drug are also all L5, model-only. Among them, some retinal conditions (for example, senile reticular retinal degeneration, which is close to AMD) are anatomically nearer to the approved use. Esophageal varices have a conceivable but unvalidated angiogenesis rationale. None has supporting clinical evidence in the data compiled.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease contraindications from the knowledge graph** (not label text): uveitis, iris disease, ciliary body disease, panuveitis, eye infectious disease, conjunctivitis.
- **Class concern for the predicted use**: Anti-VEGF agents carry bleeding and thromboembolic risk. This matters especially for any use in acute bleeding settings, and the intravitreal route and exposure profile are not suited to systemic or gastrointestinal conditions.

No drug interaction data were found. Please refer to the SmPC for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a graph-based score. There are no clinical trials or publications, no mechanistic link is supported, and the only EU authorization is cancelled.
