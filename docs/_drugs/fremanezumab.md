# Fremanezumab: From Migraine Prophylaxis to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Fremanezumab (Ajovy) is a monoclonal antibody against CGRP, originally used for the prophylaxis of migraine in adults.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**,
but no clinical trials are registered for this subtype. Support is limited to **20 publications**, most of which cover CGRP antibodies in migraine generally or in migraine with aura.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Fremanezumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds the CGRP ligand. CGRP signaling in the trigeminovascular system is central to migraine pathophysiology, and preclinical work shows fremanezumab selectively inhibits trigeminovascular neurons (PMID 28642283). Formal mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description draws on the literature.

Brainstem aura is a subtype of migraine with aura, so the mechanism is plausibly shared with the approved migraine prevention indication. Literature on related aura subtypes is encouraging. These include small observational series on migraine aura, a systematic quantitative synthesis in hemiplegic migraine, and a case report of reduced attack frequency in hemiplegic migraine.

However, none of the retrieved evidence studies the brainstem aura subtype specifically. The high TxGNN score probably reflects the close relationship to the approved migraine indication rather than independent evidence. A preclinical study also suggests fremanezumab does not alter cortical spreading depression–induced arterial dilatation (PMID 31127003). This leaves the role of CGRP in aura uncertain.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered.

## Safety Considerations

- **Vascular and cerebrovascular safety**: Because CGRP has vasodilatory roles, the literature raises an open question about cerebrovascular risk with CGRP mAbs (see PMID 42390441). This is especially relevant for patients with thrombotic or vascular risk. In addition, a recent multinational cohort study examined glaucoma risk with CGRP inhibitors (PMID 42090640).

Please refer to the SmPC for full safety information, including warnings, contraindications and drug interactions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link between CGRP blockade and migraine with brainstem aura is plausible. However, no clinical trials exist for this subtype, and the literature is limited to general migraine, migraine with aura and hemiplegic migraine. The high prediction score likely reflects proximity to the approved migraine indication. In addition, the only EU authorization is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Subtype-specific data on brainstem aura, such as a pooled analysis of aura trial subgroups or a registered prospective study
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- SmPC warnings and contraindications for safety screening
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorization status and the route to supply
- Cerebrovascular and vascular safety assessment in aura populations
