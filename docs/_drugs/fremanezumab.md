# Fremanezumab: From Migraine Prevention to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Fremanezumab is a humanized anti-CGRP (calcitonin gene-related peptide) monoclonal antibody, established as a preventive treatment for episodic and chronic migraine. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Migraine with Brainstem Aura**, but this direction is currently supported only by **20 publications** (mostly preclinical/mechanistic and observational studies) and **no registered clinical trials** specific to this subtype.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available from DrugBank (Data Gap DG002). Based on the literature in this evidence pack, fremanezumab is a fully humanized monoclonal antibody (IgG2Δa) that selectively binds and neutralizes CGRP, a key mediator in migraine pathophysiology (PMID 30725283, PMID 28642283). It is established as a preventive therapy for episodic and chronic migraine, including in patients with and without aura or associated neurological dysfunction (PMID 35302681).

Migraine with brainstem aura is a migraine subtype whose aura component is thought to arise from cortical spreading depression (CSD), a wave of neuronal depolarization distinct from the trigeminovascular pain mechanism that fremanezumab primarily targets. Because both conditions fall under the broader migraine spectrum and share overlapping neurovascular pathways, a mechanistic link is biologically plausible.

However, the strongest mechanistic evidence available here is actually cautionary rather than confirmatory: animal studies show fremanezumab does **not** significantly affect CSD-induced arterial dilatation or plasma protein extravasation (PMID 31127003), and while it can slow CSD propagation and shorten cortical recovery time, it does **not prevent CSD from occurring** (PMID 31895266). Clinical literature on anti-CGRP antibodies in aura subtypes (including hemiplegic migraine, PMID 41618146, PMID 40264646) remains limited to case reports and small observational series, with efficacy in aura-specific presentations still largely unproven.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication (migraine with brainstem aura) has no registered clinical trials, and the most mechanistically direct evidence available — animal CSD models — indicates fremanezumab does not reliably block the underlying aura mechanism. Combined with a Blocking data gap on TFDA-equivalent safety labeling (warnings/contraindications), the evidence does not yet support progressing this candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA (or equivalent) label data — warnings and contraindications (Data Gap DG001, Blocking; source: agency label PDF)
- DrugBank-sourced mechanism of action detail (Data Gap DG002, High; source: DrugBank API)
- Dedicated clinical trials or prospective studies in migraine-with-aura/brainstem-aura populations, rather than post-hoc subgroup or case-series data
- Monitoring of emerging hemiplegic/aura-subtype case literature (e.g., PMID 41618146, 40264646) for signal strengthening
