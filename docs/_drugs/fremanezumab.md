## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for fremanezumab: "Fremanezumab-vfrm is a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds to calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) ligand and blocks its binding to the receptor." ([DailyMed: Ajovy (fremanezumab-vfrm) prescribing information, Teva Pharmaceuticals USA, Inc.](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=98e344ea-5916-4947-b6f2-4a76ccc04b6b), label version 2026-06-05; accessed 2026-11-19.) Fremanezumab belongs to the class of antibodies directed against the CGRP pathway, which is the basis of its use in migraine prevention.

No plausible mechanistic link between CGRP inhibition and atrophoderma vermiculata is evident. This is a rare skin disorder that affects hair follicles and causes atrophy of the skin. No clinical trials or publications support the prediction. The high TxGNN score (0.9904) is best treated as a computational output that has not been confirmed by any study, and it should not be read as evidence of benefit.
