## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Vonicog alfa is recombinant VWF, which mediates platelet adhesion and aggregation at sites of vascular injury and carries Factor VIII. Its efficacy in von Willebrand disease is the basis of its authorization.

For primary release disorder of platelets, the defect lies in the platelet's own granule content or signaling. Exogenous VWF is therefore not expected to correct it. The high TxGNN score reflects a network-based association only, and the mechanistic link is weak.

Other predictions follow the same pattern. Glanzmann thrombasthenia (a defective platelet GPIIb/IIIa receptor), Scott syndrome (a platelet phosphatidylserine exposure defect), collagen receptor defects and constitutional thrombocytopenias all involve abnormalities in platelets themselves, not a VWF shortage. The predicted "flood factor deficiency" cannot be confidently mapped to a defined clinical entity. "Acquired coagulation factor deficiency" is too broad to be testable; a narrower question such as acquired von Willebrand syndrome would be more meaningful.

Hemophilia is the only prediction with indirect literature, with a plausible link through VWF stabilizing Factor VIII. However, the EU label explicitly states vonicog alfa should not be used to treat Haemophilia A.
