## Botulinum Toxin Type A: From Glabellar Lines to Primary Hereditary Glaucoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Botulinum toxin type A was originally authorized in the EU for the temporary improvement of moderate to severe glabellar (frown) lines in adults under 65. The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **primary hereditary glaucoma**, but this prediction has **no clinical trials and no publications** behind it. The best-supported candidate in the same list is **parkinsonian disorder** (**6 clinical trials** and **22 publications**, including one completed Phase 3 trial of sialorrhea), and this report treats it as the more actionable direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, botulinum toxin type A blocks the presynaptic release of acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction and in autonomic glands. This produces temporary, local chemodenervation. In glabellar lines, the toxin relaxes the frown muscles.

**Primary hereditary glaucoma (rank 1).** The only plausible link is reduced periocular muscle tone. There is no demonstrated effect on intraocular pressure or on the optic nerve, and no hereditary-pathology mechanism. This is a prediction without supporting evidence. The same applies to the related entries "open-angle glaucoma" and "glaucoma 1, open angle", which also have no trials or literature.

**Parkinsonian disorder (rank 2).** This is mechanistically more coherent. The same cholinergic blockade can reduce salivary secretion (sialorrhea), focal dystonia and some tremor. It is **symptomatic** treatment only. It does not modify the underlying neurodegeneration. Sialorrhea has the strongest support. Evidence for tremor, freezing of gait and dyskinesia is weaker and mixed.

Other predictions are weaker. Insomnia rests on a comorbidity association with blepharospasm. Restless legs syndrome has a small, low-quality evidence base. Bronchitis appears to be a spurious association.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 41 interactions were identified in total. The main ones are:
  - **Major:** aminoglycoside and polymyxin antibiotics (Kanamycin, Neomycin, Polymyxin B, Streptomycin). These can potentiate neuromuscular blockade.
  - **Moderate:** anticholinergic agents (Atropine, Clidinium, Dicyclomine, Glycopyrronium, Hyoscyamine, Mepenzolate, Methscopolamine, Propantheline, Scopolamine, Trospium) and sedating antihistamines (Promethazine, Acrivastine, Azatadine, Brompheniramine, Carbinoxamine, Chlorcyclizine).

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** for primary hereditary glaucoma and the other glaucoma entries. **Proceed with Guardrails** (as a research question) for symptomatic use in parkinsonian disorder.

**Rationale:**
- The top-ranked prediction, primary hereditary glaucoma, is supported only by a model score. There are no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism.
- Parkinsonian disorder has a completed Phase 3 randomized trial in sialorrhea and several supporting studies. The benefit is symptomatic, not disease-modifying. The EU authorization is also cancelled, so any development would need a new regulatory pathway.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA) and the EU product information (warnings and contraindications), which are currently unavailable
- Verification of the NCT02091739 results (population, indication and dose) and of the outcomes of the unfinished Parkinson's trials
- Confirmation of the regulatory status and a development route, since the only EU authorization (Nuceiva) is cancelled
- A safety monitoring plan for interacting medicines (aminoglycosides, polymyxins, anticholinergics) and for elderly or dysphagic patients
- No further work on the glaucoma predictions unless new mechanistic or preclinical evidence emerges
