## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, octreotide is a somatostatin analogue, and its efficacy in acromegaly is established through the approved indication. Somatostatin analogues act on somatostatin receptors to suppress hormone secretion.

The data compiled for this report does not establish any plausible somatostatin receptor-mediated mechanism in vulvar inverted follicular keratosis, a benign epidermal keratinocyte lesion. Acromegaly is a hormone-excess endocrine disorder, while the predicted condition is a benign skin lesion, so the two share little clinical or pathophysiological similarity. The high TxGNN score (0.996) reflects a graph-based computational prediction and should not be read as evidence of efficacy.

The same applies to the other top-ranked predictions. Seborrheic keratosis (99.55%) also has no supporting trials or literature. Addison disease (98.74%) and adrenocortical insufficiency (98.47%) are mechanistically unfavorable, since octreotide suppresses hormone secretion and is not expected to treat adrenal failure. The few publications found for those conditions are indirect case reports or physiological studies.
