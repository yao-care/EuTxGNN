# Ertapenem: From Bacterial Infections (Intra-abdominal, Pneumonia, Gynaecological, Diabetic Foot) to Bacterial Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Ertapenem is a once-daily carbapenem antibiotic, originally authorised in the EU for intra-abdominal infections, community-acquired pneumonia, acute gynaecological infections, diabetic foot infections of skin and soft tissue, and prevention of surgical site infection after elective colorectal surgery.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Bacterial Arthritis**, but **no clinical trials** are registered and support is limited to **10 publications**, mostly case reports and observational series.
Of the ten predicted indications, two have meaningful evidence: staphylococcus aureus infection (Phase 2 and Phase 4 trials ongoing) and urinary tract infection (several completed Phase 3 trials).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ertapenem is a broad-spectrum carbapenem beta-lactam. It is active against many Enterobacterales, anaerobes and streptococci, and its efficacy in intra-abdominal, pneumonia, gynaecological and diabetic foot infections is established.

Many of the organisms that cause bone and joint infections overlap with those it already covers. Once-daily dosing also makes it practical for outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy (OPAT). A retrospective cohort of 306 outpatients on long-term ertapenem included bone and joint infections among the common indications.

Evidence for bacterial arthritis itself is limited to case reports (for example, septic arthritis caused by *Klebsiella pneumoniae* and *Citrobacter koseri*) and small observational series. No trials are registered. Several retrieved publications are not about ertapenem in arthritis at all (for instance, a hidradenitis suppurativa review).

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: epilepsy, kidney disease, pyoureter, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis.
- **Drug Interactions**: 38 interactions were identified. The one rated Major is **bupropion**. Moderate interactions are with bowel-preparation agents (picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol 3350 with electrolytes, sodium sulfate). The remaining interactions listed (for example vancomycin, metronidazole, metformin, omeprazole) have an unknown severity rating.
- **Other considerations**: ertapenem is highly protein bound, so hypoalbuminemia may lower drug exposure. A published cohort addresses this in the S. aureus setting.

Please refer to the SmPC for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for bacterial arthritis)

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but support for bacterial arthritis consists only of case reports and small observational series, with no registered trials. The EU authorisations for ertapenem are also all cancelled. Evidence is stronger for urinary tract infection (several completed Phase 3 trials, suggested "Proceed with Guardrails", reserved for ESBL or multidrug-resistant organisms under antimicrobial stewardship, noting no Pseudomonas or Enterococcus activity). For S. aureus bacteremia, the cefazolin-plus-ertapenem combination is a research question pending the ongoing Phase 2 and Phase 4 trials. The other seven predictions (for example hyperamylasemia, toxocariasis, Ureaplasma urethritis) have no plausible mechanism or evidence and should be treated as model artifacts.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Registered prospective trials, or a well-designed comparative cohort, of ertapenem in septic arthritis and bone and joint infection
- Bone and joint penetration and pharmacokinetic data
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- EMA label warnings and contraindications, which are missing from the data compiled for this report and block safety screening
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorisation status and the current regulatory pathway
- Safety monitoring plan for patients with epilepsy, kidney disease, low albumin, or concurrent bupropion
