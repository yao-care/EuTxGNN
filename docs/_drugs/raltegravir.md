# Raltegravir: From HIV-1 Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Raltegravir is an HIV integrase strand transfer inhibitor, originally used in combination with other antiretroviral medicines to treat HIV-1 infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and the **15 publications** retrieved are almost entirely preclinical (macaque and in vitro) studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, raltegravir is an HIV integrase strand transfer inhibitor. Its efficacy in HIV-1 infection is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to SIV, a closely related lentivirus that also depends on an integrase enzyme.

SIV and HIV-1 are closely related. Preclinical work shows raltegravir is active against SIV in macaque models. One study built an antiretroviral regimen for SIVmac251-infected macaques from two nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors plus raltegravir (PMID 20233398). Another showed that topical integrase inhibitors protected macaques after vaginal SHIV exposure (PMID 24622515).

This is an animal-model extension of the existing HIV indication, not a new human therapeutic use. SIV does not infect humans in a clinically relevant way, so the prediction mainly supports raltegravir's value as a research tool in nonhuman primate studies.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations from the knowledge graph** (not label text): the TxGNN knowledge graph links raltegravir to myopathy, muscular disease, skeletal muscle disease, familial periodic paralysis, rhabdomyolysis and nodular fasciitis as contraindication-type edges. These should be verified against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions**: 36 interactions were found in total, and 20 are listed here.
  - **Major**: zinc sulfate, zinc acetate, aluminum hydroxide, calcium carbonate (polyvalent cation and antacid chelation is the likely concern).
  - **Moderate**: magnesium oxide, magnesium sulfate, magnesium chloride, orlistat.
  - **Minor**: famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, nizatidine, omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole, pioglitazone, dexamethasone.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only support for the SIV prediction is preclinical macaque and in vitro work, with no clinical trials. SIV is an animal-model extension of the existing HIV indication, not a human therapeutic need. The EU authorization is also cancelled, so no currently valid license exists.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A defined purpose for the prediction (for example, use as a nonhuman primate research tool versus a human indication)
- Mechanism of action data (MOA)
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications, which are required for safety screening
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorization status and of any current market availability
- Review of the other predicted indications, since congenital HIV and AIDS-related complex overlap with the original HIV indication and are not true repurposing
