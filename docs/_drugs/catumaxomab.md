# Catumaxomab: From Malignant Ascites in EpCAM-Positive Carcinomas to Severe Nonproliferative Diabetic Retinopathy

## One-Sentence Summary

Catumaxomab is a bispecific antibody (EpCAM x CD3), originally used for intraperitoneal treatment of malignant ascites in adults with EpCAM-positive carcinomas.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, and the proposed mechanism is implausible.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in a structured form. Pharmacology data indicate that catumaxomab is a trifunctional bispecific antibody that targets EpCAM on tumour cells and CD3 (CD3E) on T cells. It redirects T cells and accessory immune cells to kill EpCAM-positive cancer cells.

This mechanism does not fit the predicted indication. Diabetic retinopathy is a non-neoplastic microvascular disease driven by hyperglycaemia-related vascular damage, VEGF signalling and inflammation, not by EpCAM-positive cell populations. Immune-mediated cytotoxic killing is not a plausible therapy for it. Intraperitoneal dosing and the risk of cytokine release also make use in a chronic eye condition unrealistic.

The high TxGNN score (99.64%) is a graph-based prediction only. Without supporting trials or literature, it should be treated as a model artefact rather than a credible lead.

*Other predictions in the same list:* The top-ranked predictions include several more biologically plausible candidates. These are urothelial carcinomas such as prostatic urethra urothelial carcinoma, renal pelvis papillary urothelial carcinoma and transitional cell carcinoma, plus HER2-positive breast carcinoma. They are classed as research questions because EpCAM is commonly expressed in these tumours, but none has any trial or literature support, and EpCAM expression would need to be confirmed first.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: One pharmacology entry was recorded. The target is CD3E, which is the intended site of action rather than a classical drug-drug interaction. No interaction severity level is available.
- **Class-related concern**: Because it activates T cells, cytokine release and systemic inflammatory reactions are plausible, and this is unfavourable for a chronic non-oncology setting.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone. There are no trials or publications, the mechanism (T-cell redirection against EpCAM-positive cells) does not match the biology of diabetic retinopathy, and the safety profile is unfavourable. The only EU authorisation on record is cancelled, which also limits practical access.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Any preclinical or mechanistic evidence linking EpCAM/CD3 engagement to retinal disease (none is currently identified)
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap for safety screening)
- Structured mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of the current EU authorisation status
- If the programme is redirected, prioritise the EpCAM-expressing candidates (e.g., urothelial carcinomas), starting with confirmation of EpCAM expression and a suitable delivery route
