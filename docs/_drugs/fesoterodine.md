## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Fesoterodine is an antimuscarinic. Its active metabolite, 5-hydroxymethyl tolterodine, acts on muscarinic receptors in the bladder detrusor muscle, which reduces involuntary bladder contractions. Its efficacy in overactive bladder is established.

For polycystic kidney disease 3, no mechanistic link has been identified. Cyst formation in polycystic kidney or liver disease is not known to be modified by muscarinic receptor blockade. The score reflects proximity in a knowledge graph, not a pharmacological rationale. The prediction should therefore be treated as a hypothesis-generating signal and not as evidence of benefit.

Other top-ranked predictions are similarly weak. Most are rare genetic or developmental conditions (for example thoracic malformation, renal-hepatic-pancreatic dysplasia, Joubert syndrome with renal defect, karyomegalic interstitial nephritis, polycystic kidney disease) with no plausible antimuscarinic mechanism and no supporting studies. Attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder is also predicted, but anticholinergic burden may worsen attention and cognition.

The most biologically plausible prediction is **low compliance bladder** (rank 7, score 86.46%, evidence level L4). Antimuscarinics reduce detrusor overactivity and can improve bladder capacity and compliance. A 2020 study examined fesoterodine's urodynamic effects in neurogenic detrusor overactivity and/or low compliance bladder. A 2013 rat study suggests that chronic anticholinergic use may shift bladder signalling toward purinergic transmission, which is a mechanistic caveat.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related disease associations (knowledge graph, not label text)**: The knowledge graph links fesoterodine to contraindications in liver failure, angle-closure glaucoma, myasthenia gravis (including adult-onset), neuromuscular junction disease and bladder neck obstruction. These have not been checked against the label.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in total. The first 19 listed include one **Major** interaction, with **clarithromycin**. The others are Moderate. Most of the Moderate ones are other anticholinergic or antimuscarinic agents (hyoscyamine, atropine, glycopyrronium, clidinium, dicyclomine, trospium, mepenzolate, methscopolamine, propantheline, scopolamine), which carry additive anticholinergic effects. Other Moderate interactions include bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, dexamethasone, cimetidine, eluxadoline, miconazole, prucalopride and fenfluramine.

Please refer to the SmPC for warnings and contraindications from the label.
