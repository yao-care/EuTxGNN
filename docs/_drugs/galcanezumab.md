## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Galcanezumab is known to be a humanized monoclonal antibody that neutralizes calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), a neuropeptide central to migraine pathophysiology. Its efficacy in migraine prevention is established in phase 3 trials.

**The predicted link does not hold up.** Heparin cofactor 2 deficiency is a defect in a serpin-type coagulation inhibitor. Blocking the CGRP pathway has no known effect on it, and there is no plausible mechanistic bridge between the two. The very high TxGNN score appears to be an artifact of the knowledge graph rather than a biologically grounded signal.

The other top predictions show the same pattern. They are coagulation or thrombosis disorders (antithrombin deficiency type 2, factor 5 excess, thrombophilia) with no supporting evidence. CGRP is a vasodilator, and vascular safety concerns with CGRP inhibitors run opposite to any therapeutic rationale in thrombophilia.

The only prediction in the top 10 with a real evidence base is **migraine with brainstem aura** (rank 5, score 98.33%, L3). It is better understood as an extension within the already-approved migraine indication than as true repurposing.

## Safety Considerations

Safety information from the label (warnings, contraindications) and drug interaction data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC for safety information.

Recent literature on CGRP-pathway inhibitors in migraine patients points to signals worth reviewing in any safety assessment:
- **Glaucoma risk:** a 2026 multinational cohort study examined the association between CGRP inhibitors and glaucoma.
- **Cerebrovascular events:** a 2026 pharmacoepidemiologic study compared stroke/TIA risk with CGRP antibodies against onabotulinumtoxinA, and a 2024 case report described reversible cerebral artery vasoconstriction after a galcanezumab loading dose.

These are class-level findings from migraine populations, not from the predicted indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for heparin cofactor 2 deficiency is supported only by a model score, with no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism. The only currently valid regulatory record is a cancelled EU authorization, which further limits practical development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Any mechanistic or preclinical evidence linking CGRP blockade to heparin cofactor 2 or the coagulation pathway (none currently exists)
- Label-level safety data (warnings and contraindications) and mechanism of action data to be compiled
- Clarification of the cancelled EU authorization status
- If the goal is a realistic opportunity, redirect evaluation to **migraine with brainstem aura** (rank 5), with a subtype-specific evidence review and a safety review covering the glaucoma and cerebrovascular signals
