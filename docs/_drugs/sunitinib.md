## Sunitinib: Liposarcoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Sunitinib is an oral multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor originally authorised in the EU for imatinib-resistant gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST), metastatic renal cell carcinoma and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **liposarcoma**, with **2 completed Phase 2 trials** and **9 publications** (including one Phase 2 study in soft tissue sarcoma and one case report in liposarcoma) currently supporting this direction.
No Phase 3 confirmation exists for this indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, sunitinib inhibits receptor tyrosine kinases including VEGFR, PDGFR and KIT, which drive angiogenesis and tumour cell proliferation. Its efficacy in GIST, renal cell carcinoma and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours has been established.

GIST is itself a sarcoma of the gastrointestinal tract, and liposarcoma is a soft tissue sarcoma. Both can depend on growth-factor receptor signalling, so a mechanistic bridge is plausible. However, liposarcoma subtypes vary in their dependence on these pathways, so the link is not subtype-specific.

The clinical signal comes from small Phase 2 studies that pool several sarcoma histologies. Liposarcoma-specific conclusions are therefore limited.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level contraindication signals (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: long QT syndrome and torsades de pointes, heart failure, liver failure and liver disease, drug-induced liver injury, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), thrombocytopenia, anaemia, thyroid disorders (hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism), adrenocortical insufficiency, epilepsy, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, potassium deficiency and posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified; the first 20 reviewed include one Major interaction and the rest Moderate.
  - Major: dolasetron.
  - Moderate examples: clarithromycin, dexamethasone, palonosetron, loperamide, famotidine, glimepiride, chlorpropamide, and multiple insulin products (antidiabetic drugs may need glucose monitoring).
- A case-report literature signal also notes sunitinib-induced thrombotic microangiopathy.

Please refer to the SmPC for complete safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for liposarcoma consists of two small, pooled-histology Phase 2 trials, one published Phase 2 report and a single case report, with no Phase 3 confirmation. In addition, the EU authorisations are currently cancelled, and label-based safety data are not yet available. The data are not enough to move forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- EU SmPC warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking data gap)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Liposarcoma subtype-specific efficacy data (e.g., dedifferentiated vs. well-differentiated vs. myxoid), ideally from a randomised trial
- A cardiac, hepatic and drug-interaction monitoring plan
